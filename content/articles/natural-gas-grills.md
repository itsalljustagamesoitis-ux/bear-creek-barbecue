Natural gas grills solve a real problem for backyard cooks: you fire up without hunting for a propane tank, and the fuel line never runs dry mid-cook. If you're spending serious weekend time at the grill, that convenience adds up faster than you might expect. The [gas grills](/gas-grills/) category has grown substantially, and natural gas options now span freestanding models, built-in heads, and full outdoor kitchen islands , each with a different installation commitment and cooking philosophy.

The challenge is matching the right format to your actual setup. A four-burner freestanding grill suits one backyard; a built-in island head suits another entirely. BTU output, construction material, and convertibility all matter , and getting those decisions wrong is expensive.

## What to Look For in Natural Gas Grills

### BTU Output and Heat Distribution

BTU ratings get thrown around as a proxy for power, but the number alone doesn't tell the whole story. What matters is how heat distributes across the cooking surface. A grill with 40,000 BTUs spread across four burners can create distinct temperature zones , high heat for searing, moderate heat for finishing , which is more useful than raw output concentrated in one area.

Look at the burner layout relative to the grate dimensions. A grill where the burners are spaced evenly under the full grate area will cook more predictably than one where the outer edges run cool. For most backyard cooks, 10,000, 12,000 BTUs per burner is a reasonable benchmark for adequate heat recovery after you open the lid.

### Construction and Material Quality

Stainless steel is the default material for quality gas grills, but not all stainless is equal. 304-grade stainless , sometimes called marine-grade , resists corrosion substantially better than 430-grade, which you'll find in budget builds. If a grill is going to live outside year-round in a wet climate, that grade distinction matters.

Cast-iron grates hold heat better than stainless rod grates, which is why they're common on higher-end freestanding grills. Porcelain-enameled cast iron adds rust resistance to that equation. For built-in and island configurations, the surrounding cabinetry material matters as much as the grill head itself , thin-gauge steel will dent and rust; heavy-gauge or powder-coated steel holds up.

### Natural Gas vs. Convertible Models

A dedicated natural gas grill is plumbed directly to your home's gas line and requires a licensed plumber for installation , that's a fixed cost and a fixed location. Convertible models ship configured for propane and include a conversion kit (or sell one separately) that allows you to switch to natural gas. The conversion is typically straightforward, but confirm the kit is included before you buy.

For most homeowners adding a natural gas connection for the first time, a convertible model offers flexibility if your circumstances change. If you're building a permanent outdoor kitchen with a dedicated gas stub, a natural-gas-specific model is the cleaner choice. Either way, reviewing the full range of [outdoor gas grill options](/gas-grills/) before you commit to a format helps you avoid buying the wrong category entirely.

### Format: Freestanding, Built-In, or Island

This is the decision that constrains everything else. Freestanding grills on carts are movable, require no structural modification, and can be connected to a natural gas line with a flexible hose. Built-in grill heads drop into an outdoor kitchen frame or counter , they look integrated but require a fixed installation. Island grills arrive as a complete unit with cabinetry and workspace built in.

Your patio or deck dimensions, HOA rules about permanent installations, and whether you're building or buying a ready-made setup all feed into this choice. A 30-inch built-in head in an island frame needs more planning than a cart grill connected to a quick-disconnect fitting on your gas line.

## Buying Guide

### Getting Your Gas Line Right Before You Buy

Natural gas grills require a fixed gas line connection, and that connection needs to be in the right location before the grill arrives. A licensed plumber or gas fitter handles this work , not a general contractor and definitely not a DIY project in most municipalities. The stub-out location, valve type, and hose length all need to match the grill you're buying.

If you're adding a new gas line, do this before you finalize your grill choice. The grill format , freestanding, built-in, island , determines where the connection needs to be. Getting the stub-out in the wrong location for your eventual grill is an avoidable mistake that creates a more expensive fix later.

### Matching Grill Format to Your Patio Reality

Cart grills connect to a natural gas line via a flexible hose and a quick-disconnect fitting, which means you retain some ability to reposition the grill , though not infinite flexibility. Built-in heads and island units are committed to a location. Measure your patio carefully, account for clearance requirements from structures and fences, and confirm your HOA's rules about permanent outdoor cooking installations before committing to a built-in format.

The [gas grills](/gas-grills/) category spans a wide range of footprints. A 30-inch built-in head needs a frame or countertop structure around it. A full island unit might run 60 inches or more across. A freestanding four-burner cart sits in a defined space but needs adequate clearance on all sides for safe operation and airflow.

### BTU Output and Cooking Surface Together

A common mistake is evaluating BTU output without considering the cooking surface area it's distributed across. More BTUs on a smaller grate produces hotter, less controllable heat. Fewer BTUs on a larger grate can mean sluggish recovery after you open the lid. The ratio matters more than either number in isolation.

For a four-burner grill in the 40,000, 52,000 BTU range, you want a primary cooking surface large enough to set up distinct heat zones. If every burner is running at the same setting because the grate doesn't give you room to work, you've lost the main advantage of multiple burners.

### Thinking About Long-Term Maintenance

Cast-iron grates require seasoning and occasional re-seasoning , they're not maintenance-free, but they reward the effort with cooking performance. Stainless rod grates are lower maintenance but don't hold heat as well. Porcelain-enameled cast iron sits between those two: better corrosion resistance than bare cast iron, better heat retention than stainless.

Burner tubes corrode over time regardless of material. On quality grills, replacement burners are available and reasonably priced. On off-brand grills, sourcing replacement parts two years after purchase can be difficult or impossible. This is one concrete reason brand reputation matters in this category , it affects the long-term cost of ownership, not just the initial build quality.

### Convertible vs. Dedicated Natural Gas

If your home currently has a propane setup and you're considering switching to natural gas, a convertible grill gives you a transition path. If you're starting fresh with a new gas line connection, a dedicated natural gas grill removes the conversion hardware from the equation and is typically cleaner to install.

The conversion kits that reputable manufacturers include , or sell separately , are reliable when installed correctly. The key word is correctly: if you're not confident in your ability to swap out orifices and adjust regulators, have a professional handle the conversion. A gas leak from a botched DIY conversion is a serious safety issue, not a grilling inconvenience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a plumber to install a natural gas grill?

Yes, in most cases. Connecting a natural gas grill to your home's gas line requires a licensed plumber or gas fitter in most municipalities , local codes vary, but this is not a project to handle without verified expertise. The connection involves the right fittings, a shutoff valve, and a leak test before first use. Budget for this as part of the total installation cost, not as an optional step.
### What's the difference between a dedicated natural gas grill and a convertible model?

A dedicated natural gas grill is factory-configured for natural gas and cannot run on propane without modification. A convertible model ships configured for propane and includes or sells a conversion kit that swaps out the orifices and regulator for natural gas use. Convertible models offer flexibility if your fuel source might change; dedicated models are simpler if your gas line is already in place.
### How does the Monument Grills convertible compare to the Weber Spirit E-425 for everyday grilling?

The [Weber Spirit E-425](product:weber-spirit-e-425-natural) has a build quality advantage , component engineering, igniter reliability, and grate quality are consistently stronger. The [Monument Grills convertible](product:monument-grills-larger-convertible-4) offers a more accessible price band and bundles the conversion kit, which Weber doesn't need because its model is dedicated natural gas. For daily heavy use over many years, the Weber's durability makes the comparison easy. For moderate use with a budget ceiling, Monument is a reasonable alternative.
### Is a built-in grill head or a freestanding cart grill better for a new outdoor kitchen?
It depends on whether you're building a permanent structure or keeping the setup flexible. A built-in head like the [Brand-Man 30-Inch 4-Burner](product:brand-man-gas-grill-built) integrates cleanly into a designed outdoor kitchen frame and looks intentional. A freestanding cart grill connected to a quick-disconnect hose retains some repositioning ability and requires less structural commitment. If you're building a permanent kitchen island, the built-in is the better fit.
### How much cooking surface do I actually need for a family of four?

Four burners and roughly 500, 600 square inches of primary cooking surface handles a family of four comfortably with room for different items at different heat levels. Two burners is adequate for weeknight cooking for four, but creates real constraints when you're hosting or cooking multiple proteins simultaneously. For households that entertain beyond immediate family, four burners and zone-cooking capability is worth the larger footprint.
