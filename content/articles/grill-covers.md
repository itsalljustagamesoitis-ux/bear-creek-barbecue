Grill covers don't get much respect until the season you skip one. A summer of rain, UV exposure, and windblown debris will strip paint, corrode burners, and leave your grates looking like they've been through a war , all problems a decent cover prevents for cheap. I've gone through enough [Tools & Grates](/tools-and-grates/) research to know that the cover is always the last thing people budget for and the first thing they regret ignoring.

The market for grill covers is crowded with products that look identical on a listing page but perform very differently after a winter. Fit, fabric weight, and fastening system separate covers that stay put from covers that blow into your neighbor's yard on the first gusty night.

## What to Look For in a Grill Cover

### Size and Fit

The most common mistake buyers make is ordering by grill brand alone. A cover listed as fitting a "Weber Genesis" might fit your specific model perfectly or hang awkwardly at the bottom , because even within a brand, grill dimensions vary by year and configuration. Measure your grill before you search: width at the widest point (usually the side tables folded down), depth from front to back, and height from the ground to the top of the lid handle.

Universal-fit covers address this with adjustable straps and wider size ranges, which works well for standard box-frame grills. Where universal fit becomes a liability is on grills with unusual shapes , side burners that protrude significantly, cart-style grills with offset shelves, or kamado eggs that no rectangular cover will ever seat cleanly. Know your grill's profile before you assume universal means adequate.

### Fabric and Material Weight

Oxford fabric is the industry standard for mid-range grill covers, and for good reason: it balances UV resistance, water shedding, and reasonable durability at a mass-market price point. What varies is the denier count , the thread thickness that determines how the fabric holds up under abrasion, puncture, and sustained UV load. Higher denier means heavier fabric and longer service life, though it also means more weight to wrestle on and off the grill.

A cover that's merely water-resistant will soak through in a sustained rain. Look for waterproof construction , typically a bonded inner lining , not just water-resistant coating. UV resistance matters more than most buyers expect, especially in southern exposures where summer sun will bleach and crack a cover in a single season.

### Fastening System

A cover that doesn't stay put in wind is functionally useless. Adjustable straps under the grill body are the baseline expectation , they keep the cover from lifting in gusts and let you cinch the fit on a grill that runs slightly under the cover's maximum dimension. Hook-and-loop (Velcro) systems offer easy on-and-off with solid hold. Buckle straps hold more securely in high wind but take an extra few seconds to operate.

The strap attachment point matters too. Straps threaded through a fabric loop at the hem work until the loop tears. Reinforced attachment points or straps that connect to a webbing band around the entire hem perimeter hold considerably longer. This is a detail worth checking in product photos before you buy.

### Ventilation

Moisture trapped under a cover does as much damage as moisture from rain. A cover sealed tight against a warm grill , or a grill that hasn't fully dried before covering , will trap condensation and accelerate corrosion. Vented covers include mesh panels or air channels at the top or sides to allow airflow without letting in precipitation.

Not every cover includes venting, and for most climates it's a nice-to-have rather than a must-have. In humid coastal climates or areas with significant temperature swings, ventilation becomes more important. The broader universe of [grill accessories and tools](/tools-and-grates/) includes grill mats and rust inhibitors that can complement a cover in high-humidity environments.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Cover Size to Your Grill

Size mismatch is the most common reason a cover fails to protect. A cover too wide will billow in wind and funnel water toward the grill body rather than away from it. A cover too narrow won't seat over the lid without forcing , and forcing a cover damages both the cover seams and the grill's handle hardware over time. Measure width, depth, and height before you buy. Most product listings include a size chart; cross-reference it against your actual grill dimensions, not the model name.

Universal-fit covers work well when your grill falls comfortably within the stated range. If you're at either end of the range , say, a grill right at the cover's minimum dimension , cinch the straps fully and check that the hem clears the ground without dragging. A cover that drags will wick ground moisture upward.

### Climate and Material Selection

A buyer in Phoenix and a buyer in coastal Maine need different things from a grill cover. In dry, high-UV climates, UV resistance and fade resistance are the primary durability factors , a cover that chalks and cracks in two seasons costs more over time than a higher-quality cover bought once. In humid climates with significant precipitation, waterproof construction (not just water-resistant) and ventilation matter more.

Heavy-duty Oxford fabric with a bonded waterproof lining is the right specification for most North American climates. Buyers in extreme cold should also look for material that stays flexible at low temperatures , some cheaper fabrics become brittle and crack when folded or cinched in freezing conditions.

### Fastening System and Wind Resistance

Wind is the condition most buyers underestimate. A cover that fits perfectly in still air can lift, shift, and eventually blow off entirely in sustained wind. The fastening system , how the cover attaches to or beneath the grill , determines how much wind the cover can handle. Adjustable straps that pass under the grill cart and buckle or Velcro on the opposite side are the most secure configuration for open patio installations.

Reviewing the broader category of [grill tools and accessories](/tools-and-grates/) reveals that cover retention is a common afterthought purchase , buyers add bungee cords or weight clips after their first cover blows away. A cover with an integrated strap system eliminates that extra step.

### Storage When Not in Use

The best cover is one you'll actually put on the grill consistently. A heavy-duty cover that takes two minutes to fold and store gets used less than a lighter cover that bundles quickly. Most covers include a storage bag , verify the bag dimensions are practical before you assume it's useful. Some storage bags are sized so tightly that getting the cover back in them requires more patience than the situation warrants.

For patio setups with limited storage, a lighter cover hung on a hook is a better behavioral outcome than a heavy cover folded in a bin that requires effort to retrieve. Match the cover to the actual storage workflow you'll sustain through a season, not just the one you intend to.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know what size grill cover to buy?

Measure your grill at its widest points , width with side tables folded down, depth from front to back, and height from ground to the top of the lid handle. Cross-reference those numbers against the cover's listed dimensions, not just the brand compatibility list. If your grill falls near the maximum of a cover's stated range, size up rather than forcing a fit. A slightly oversized cover secured with adjusted straps performs better than an undersized cover stretched at the seams.
### What's the difference between water-resistant and waterproof construction?

Water-resistant covers shed light rain and surface moisture but will soak through in sustained precipitation. Waterproof covers have a bonded interior lining that blocks water penetration regardless of how long or hard it rains. For most outdoor grill applications , meaning a grill left outside through a season , waterproof construction is the appropriate specification. Water-resistant is adequate only if the grill is under a covered patio or in a climate with very low precipitation.
### Is the GrillTough cover better than the Unicook for heavy seasonal use?

They serve different size ranges rather than competing directly. The [GrillTough Heavy Duty BBQ Grill Cover](product:grilltough-heavy-duty-bbq-grill) is the stronger choice for standard-size grills where 58 inches is a good fit. The [Unicook Heavy Duty Waterproof Barbecue Gas Grill Cover](product:unicook-heavy-duty-waterproof-barbecue) is purpose-built for large grills that standard 58-inch covers undersized. If both options fit your grill, the GrillTough's layered construction and established durability make it the practical pick for harsh seasonal conditions.
### Do I need a vented grill cover?

In most climates, ventilation is a useful feature but not a strict requirement. Where it becomes important is in humid environments with significant temperature variation , coastal climates, the Gulf Coast, Pacific Northwest , where trapped condensation under a sealed cover can accelerate corrosion faster than unprotected exposure. If you're covering a grill in a dry climate or a covered patio, an unvented cover with solid waterproofing is adequate.
### Can I leave a grill cover on during a cookout and put it back immediately after cooking?

No. A cover placed over a still-hot grill will trap heat and moisture from condensation as the grill cools, and most cover materials are not rated for direct heat contact. Let the grill cool completely , typically 30 to 60 minutes after the last burner is shut off , before covering. Covering a warm grill also creates interior condensation that sits on grates and burners, accelerating the corrosion the cover is intended to prevent.
