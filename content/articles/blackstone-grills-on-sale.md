Flat-top griddles have earned serious shelf space in the backyard cooking world, and Blackstone leads that category for a reason. If you've been watching for [Blackstone grills on sale](/griddles/), this is a good moment to understand what separates a solid buy from a regrettable impulse purchase , because the model differences matter more than most people realize before they commit.

The range runs from compact 22-inch portable units to full 36-inch stations with hoods and caster wheels. Cooking surface area, burner count, grease management, and portability all interact differently depending on how and where you cook. Here's what to know before you buy.

## What to Look For in a Blackstone Griddle

### Cooking Surface Size

Surface area is the most consequential spec on any flat-top griddle. A 36-inch surface , the largest common Blackstone format , handles a genuine crowd: burgers, eggs, bacon, and smashed potatoes simultaneously without crowding. A 22-inch surface is workable for a family of four but creates real bottlenecks if you're feeding more than six people at once.

The practical question isn't just how many square inches you have , it's how many independent cooking zones you can manage. Larger surfaces paired with multiple burners let you run high heat on one side and a gentle warm zone on the other. That flexibility matters more than raw size once you're past feeding two people.

### Burner Count and BTU Output

Burner count determines how precisely you can control temperature across the cooking surface. Two-burner configurations split the griddle into left and right zones. Four-burner setups give you finer granularity , high heat on the far left for searing, medium in the center for pancakes, low on the right to hold finished food warm.

Total BTU output matters, but it's less predictive of performance than people expect. A 60,000 BTU four-burner station sounds impressive, and the heat recovery is genuinely fast after loading cold food. What matters more in practice is whether the burners heat the surface evenly , and that varies by model, not just by BTU count.

### Portability vs. Permanence

Foldable legs and caster wheels solve a real problem if you're cooking at a campsite, tailgate, or HOA-restricted patio where a permanent installation isn't an option. But portability comes with trade-offs. Folding-leg frames flex more than fixed-stand designs. Cart-style griddles with caster wheels are easier to move around a patio but harder to load into a truck.

Before you optimize for portability, be honest about how often you'll actually move the thing. Most backyard griddles that ship with foldable legs stay in one spot. If that describes your situation, a cart-style station with better stability is probably the smarter buy.

### Hood Design and Heat Retention

A built-in hood on a flat-top griddle does two things: it traps heat for faster cooking on thick proteins, and it protects the surface when the griddle isn't in use. Not every Blackstone model ships with a hood, and aftermarket hoods introduce fitment complications.

Covered cooking on a flat top is different from grilling with a lid , you're not creating convection the way a domed grill lid does. What you get is radiant heat bouncing back down onto the food, which speeds up the cook on chicken thighs or a thick burger without requiring you to flip constantly. For most backyard cooks, a hood is worth having.

### Grease Management

This is the feature most buyers underestimate until after they've cooked on a flat top for the first time. Fat renders off meat in volume on a griddle surface , more than most people expect , and it needs somewhere to go. Rear grease management systems channel runoff away from the cook surface to a collection cup in the back. Front-cup systems work, but they require more active management during cooking.

Exploring the full range of [flat-top cooking options](/griddles/) before committing to a specific model is worth the time, because grease management design affects your real-world cooking experience significantly , not just cleanup. A rear-drain system on a 28-inch two-burner griddle is more practical for daily use than a front-cup system on a larger station.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Surface Size to Your Cooking Reality

The 36-inch format dominates this lineup for a reason , it's the most versatile size for backyard cooking across different group sizes. But most buyers overestimate how often they cook for large groups. A 28-inch two-burner station handles a family of five without issue, takes up less patio space, and stores more easily than a 36-inch cart.

The honest calibration: if you cook for more than eight people regularly, the 36-inch surface earns its footprint. If your typical cook is four to six people, the 28-inch hits a more practical sweet spot.

### Portable vs. Cart-Style Station

Foldable-leg designs and scissor-leg portables exist for buyers who genuinely move their griddle , camping, tailgating, catering, or HOA-restricted patios that require equipment to be stored between uses. If that describes you, the portability trade-offs are worth accepting.

Cart-style stations with caster wheels are better for buyers who want the option to reposition on a patio but mostly cook in one place. The frame rigidity, grease management integration, and overall cooking experience on a cart-style unit is meaningfully better than a foldable-leg design at equivalent surface sizes. Understanding the full range of [griddle formats available](/griddles/) helps clarify which category fits your actual situation before you buy.

### Hood: Have It or Hunt for It

If a Blackstone model ships with a built-in hood, that's worth factoring into your comparison. Aftermarket hoods are available but fit varies by model year, and a poorly fitting hood creates more frustration than it solves.

A hood matters most for three things: cooking thick proteins faster, keeping the surface protected between sessions, and managing wind during outdoor cooking. Buyers who cook primarily breakfast foods and smash burgers , both fast-cooking applications , will miss the hood less than those cooking chicken or thicker cuts regularly.

### Grease Management Is Not a Minor Feature

Rear grease management is the quality-of-life upgrade most buyers overlook in spec comparisons. On a busy griddle cook, fat accumulates fast. A system that channels it to the back and into a collection cup you can remove and empty is dramatically easier to manage than nudging grease toward a front-mounted cup while simultaneously managing food on the surface.

If two models are otherwise equivalent, the one with rear grease management is the better daily-use buy. It's that simple.

### Construction Materials and Long-Term Durability

Rolled steel tops require consistent seasoning to stay rust-free. Iron-forged surfaces hold seasoning more reliably and resist warping at high heat better than standard rolled steel. Powder-coated steel frames resist rust better than raw steel frames but will still show wear if the unit lives outdoors year-round without a cover.

A griddle cover is not optional if your unit stays outside. It costs very little, extends the surface life significantly, and is the single most impactful maintenance decision you'll make after buying. Budget for it before you complete your purchase.

### What is the difference between the 28-inch and 36-inch Blackstone griddles?

The 36-inch models offer significantly more cooking surface and, on most configurations, four burners rather than two. The 28-inch Blackstone handles family cooking for four to six people without issue and occupies less storage and patio space. If you cook regularly for eight or more people, the 36-inch earns its footprint. For smaller groups, the 28-inch is often the more practical buy.
### Is the iron-forged Blackstone worth the upgrade over a standard rolled-steel top?

Iron-forged construction holds seasoning more consistently and resists surface warping at sustained high heat better than standard rolled steel. The [BLACKSTONE 2310 Iron Forged 36" Omnivore Griddle](product:blackstone-2310-iron-forged-36-omnivore) is the right choice for buyers who cook frequently and want a surface that performs better over time with proper maintenance. For occasional use, standard rolled steel is adequate.
### Can I use a Blackstone griddle for camping?

The [BLACKSTONE 22" On The Go Scissor-Leg Griddle](product:blackstone-22-on-the-go-scissor) is specifically designed for portable use , scissor legs collapse for transport, integrated wheels make it rollable, and the stainless steel construction tolerates outdoor conditions well. The 28-inch two-burner station is campsite-capable but less optimized for transport. Foldable-leg 36-inch models can make the trip but require a truck bed and two people to move safely.
### How important is rear grease management on a flat-top griddle?

Rear grease management matters significantly for daily cooking convenience. Fat renders from meat in volume on a flat-top surface, and a system that channels it automatically to a rear collection cup keeps you focused on cooking rather than managing runoff. The [Blackstone Flat Top Gas Grill Griddle 2 Burner](product:blackstone-flat-top-gas-grill) handles this well for its size category.
### Do Blackstone griddles require seasoning before first use?

Yes. Every Blackstone flat-top surface requires seasoning before the first cook and periodic re-seasoning during its service life. Iron-forged surfaces hold seasoning longer between applications than standard rolled steel, which is one of the practical arguments for the premium construction on high-use units.
