Finding the right outdoor fireplace and pizza oven combination means navigating a category that spans pure fire pits, gas fire tables, and dedicated pizza ovens , each with a different answer to what "outdoor cooking" actually means. The [pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) hub covers the full spectrum, but this guide focuses on the products most likely to anchor a backyard setup for someone who wants fire, food, and flexibility without a permanent install.

The honest question is whether you're optimizing for cooking or for atmosphere , because those two goals pull in different directions more than most product listings admit.

## What to Look For in an Outdoor Fireplace and Pizza Oven

### Fuel Type and What It Actually Costs You

Wood burning and propane gas represent genuinely different ownership experiences, not just aesthetic preferences. A wood-burning fire pit gives you real flame character and the ability to cook over embers , but you're managing fuel sourcing, storage, and ash cleanup every time you use it. For a suburban patio where you're firing this up on Saturday afternoon, that's a real consideration.

Propane is cleaner operationally. Push a button, adjust a valve, done. The tradeoff is ongoing fuel costs and the visual flatness of gas flame compared to a wood fire. Neither is objectively better , it depends on how you actually use the thing, not how you imagine you will.

### Cooking Surface vs. Gathering Space

The 42-inch fire pit dominates this category for a reason: it hits a sweet spot between cooking area and social footprint. A 42-inch round gives you enough grate space to cook for four to six people while still functioning as a gathering point.

Sizing down to 36 inches saves meaningful space on a smaller patio, but you'll feel it when you're trying to manage multiple items on the grill. Sizing up adds footprint , and on a concrete slab with HOA restrictions, footprint matters more than the specs sheet suggests. Measure your actual space before you commit to a diameter.

### Pizza-Specific Considerations

A standard fire pit or gas table is not a pizza oven. That distinction matters. If pizza is your primary cooking goal, you need a unit with an enclosed chamber that can sustain temperatures high enough to char a crust in under two minutes , typically north of 700°F. An open fire pit grill can cook a pizza on a stone with patience, but it won't replicate what a purpose-built oven does.

The [pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) category exists precisely because an open fire and an enclosed oven are different tools. Buying a fire pit to make pizza is fine if fire is your primary goal and pizza is occasional. Buying a fire pit primarily to make pizza is the wrong order of priority.

### Build Quality and Weather Exposure

Steel gauge matters more than product descriptions usually communicate. Thin steel fire pits warp under sustained high-heat use , not immediately, but within a season or two of regular fires. Look for weight as a proxy: a heavier unit generally uses thicker gauge material. Powder-coated finishes extend surface life on painted steel, but no coating survives years of outdoor exposure without some maintenance.

Lava rock for gas tables serves an aesthetic and a practical function: it distributes flame evenly and retains heat. Models that include it are ahead of those that don't. Spark screens on wood-burning models are not optional safety theater , they're genuinely necessary on any unit you're using near seating.

### Setup and Storage Realities

Permanent installation versus portable changes the calculus considerably. A gas fire table rated for a patio deck has different setup requirements than a wood-burning pit you can reposition. Portable propane pizza ovens require no installation at all , they set up in minutes and store in a shed.

If you're renting, or have an HOA that treats anything anchored to the ground as a structure, portability is not a feature , it's a requirement. Factor in where these units live when they're not in use. A 42-inch fire pit in a storage shed takes up real space.

## Buying Guide

### Wood Burning vs. Gas: Making the Actual Call

The fuel debate sounds philosophical until you've hauled wet wood to a cold patio in October and reconsidered your life choices. Wood burning delivers real fire , variable flame, ember heat, the smell that makes a backyard feel like something is actually happening. Gas delivers consistent, push-button output with no cleanup.

For weekend cooks who use their fire pit regularly, wood's operational overhead is manageable. For buyers who want fire as a secondary feature , ambiance while you're already outside , gas is more likely to get used consistently. Honest self-assessment about usage frequency matters more than the fuel preference you have in the abstract.

### Sizing for Your Actual Space

Forty-two inches is the category standard for a reason, but it assumes you have the patio to support it. A 42-inch round needs clearance on all sides from seating and structures , most fire safety guidelines suggest at least three feet. On a 16x14 patio, that arithmetic gets tight fast.

Measure the actual usable space, subtract safety clearance, and then check whether a 36-inch unit gives you more functional outdoor room even if it reduces cooking surface. A fire pit that dominates the patio is worse than a slightly smaller one with room to move around it.

### Pizza Ovens as a Separate Decision

If pizza is a primary goal, treat it as a separate purchase from your fire pit , not a dual-use feature. Open fire pit grills can cook pizza with patience and a stone, but they can't reach the enclosed temperatures that make Neapolitan-style crusts possible. The full range of [outdoor pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) includes dedicated propane and wood-fired enclosed ovens designed specifically for that task.

Buying a fire pit for pizza is fine if fire is the reason and pizza is occasional. Buying a fire pit as a pizza oven substitute is a compromise that usually leaves you dissatisfied with both functions.

### BTU and Heat Output for Gas Models

Sixty thousand BTU is meaningful at an outdoor fire table , it generates enough heat to be felt at seating distances in cool weather. Lower-output gas tables (in the 40,000, 50,000 BTU range) can feel underwhelming outdoors where heat dissipates quickly. If heating capacity matters to your use case , fall entertaining, cool evenings , prioritize BTU output in gas selections.

That said, BTU output is only one variable. Flame distribution, pan geometry, and lava rock or glass media all affect how that heat reaches you. A well-designed 50,000 BTU table can outperform a poorly designed 65,000 BTU unit in perceived warmth.

### Portability and Storage

Permanent or semi-permanent placement changes maintenance and liability calculus. A heavy 42-inch steel fire pit isn't going anywhere once placed , that's a feature if your patio is fixed, a burden if you need to clear space or store it seasonally.

The BIG HORN pizza oven is genuinely portable in a way none of the fire pits are. If your outdoor setup changes based on season or living situation, portability may matter more than feature count. Plan for where the unit lives in January , storage space is finite and a 42-inch fire pit in a shed takes up real territory.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a fire pit grill to make pizza?

You can cook pizza on a fire pit grill using a pizza stone, but the results will differ significantly from a dedicated pizza oven. Open fire pits can't maintain the enclosed 700°F-plus temperatures that produce a properly charred crust quickly. For occasional pizza over fire, the [Verdeluxe 42 Inch BBQ Fire Pit](product:verdeluxe-42-inch-bbq-fire) with its dual grill setup gives you the most flexibility. For regular pizza cooking, the [BIG HORN Gas Pizza Oven](product:big-horn-gas-pizza-oven) is the right tool.
### What's the difference between the Verdeluxe 42-inch and the Verdeluxe 36-inch?

The primary difference is diameter and footprint , six inches less on the 36-inch model translates to meaningful patio space savings. Both share the 3-in-1 fire pit, grill, and table functionality with lid and fire poker included. The [Verdeluxe 36 Inch Outdoor Fire Pit](product:verdeluxe-36-inch-outdoor-fire) suits smaller patios and one-to-four person groups. The [Verdeluxe 42 Inch BBQ Fire Pit](product:verdeluxe-42-inch-bbq-fire) is the better choice when cooking surface and gathering space are the priority.
### Is the Ciays gas fire pit table safe to use on a wood deck?

The Ciays carries a CSA listing, which means it has been independently tested and certified against safety standards , that certification is specifically relevant for deck placement where fire codes and HOA rules often require it. You should still confirm the clearance requirements for your specific deck material and local fire regulations. The [Ciays 42 Inch Gas Fire Pit Table](product:ciays-42-inch-gas-fire) also includes a steel lid for when the unit is not in use, which reduces weather exposure and eliminates the open-fire risk when the fire isn't burning.
### How much ongoing cost should I expect from propane-fueled fire pits and pizza ovens?

Propane consumption varies considerably based on BTU output and session length. A 60,000 BTU fire table running at full output burns through a standard 20-pound tank in roughly four to five hours of continuous use. Most users run at partial output, which extends tank life meaningfully. Both the [Ciays 42 Inch Gas Fire Pit Table](product:ciays-42-inch-gas-fire) and [BIG HORN Gas Pizza Oven](product:big-horn-gas-pizza-oven) require standard 20-pound tanks, available at most hardware and grocery stores.
### Do wood-burning fire pits require any special installation or permits?

Most wood-burning fire pits are considered portable appliances and don't require permits, but local regulations vary by municipality and HOA rules layer on top of that. Open combustion fire pits are restricted or prohibited in some areas during dry conditions or fire season. The [Panovue 42 Inch Outdoor Fire Pit](product:panovue-42-inch-outdoor-fire) and both Verdeluxe models are freestanding units with no anchoring required, but you should check your local fire ordinances and HOA guidelines before purchasing any open-flame outdoor appliance.
