Replacement grill grates are one of those purchases that feel minor until you realize how much they affect every cook. Warped, rusted, or broken grates change the way food sits, sears, and releases , and replacing them is almost always cheaper than replacing the grill itself. The [Tools & Grates](/tools-and-grates/) hub covers the full range of what goes on and around your cooking surface, and grates are the piece that matters most.

The challenge is that compatibility drives everything here. A grate that fits a Nexgrill 5-burner won't drop into a Weber Spirit, and even within a single brand, model numbers vary enough to matter. Before you buy on price alone, you need to know what you're fitting, what material makes sense for how you cook, and what maintenance you're actually willing to do.

## What to Look For in Replacement Grill Grates

### Material: Cast Iron vs. Porcelain-Coated vs. Stainless Steel

Cast iron holds heat better than any other grate material. It takes longer to get there, but once it's hot, it produces the kind of sear marks and crust that porcelain-coated steel can't consistently match. The tradeoff is maintenance , cast iron rusts if you leave it wet, and it needs occasional re-seasoning to stay non-stick and protected. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real commitment.

Porcelain-coated grates are the factory default on most mid-range gas grills. They're easier to maintain, reasonably non-stick when new, and forgiving if you forget to dry them. They chip over time, though, and once the coating breaks down, rust follows quickly. If you're replacing factory porcelain grates with cast iron, you're upgrading , but you need to be honest about whether you'll maintain them.

Stainless steel grates land between the two on both heat retention and maintenance. They don't require seasoning and they resist rust well, but cheaper stainless warps under high heat and doesn't deliver the same sear quality as cast iron. Gauge matters , thicker rods hold up better and store heat more effectively.

### Fit: Model Number Compatibility

This is where most replacement grate purchases go wrong. Grill manufacturers use different cooking surface dimensions across model lines, and sometimes across years of the same model. The safest approach is to measure your existing grates before ordering , length, width, and the spacing of any support rails. A grate listed as compatible with your model number is a good start, but measurements confirm it.

Pay attention to how many grates your grill uses. Some grills take a single cooking grid; others use two or three sections that fit together. Replacing only one section of a multi-section configuration usually means mismatched wear and potentially uneven cooking.

Exploring the full range of [grill tools and grates](/tools-and-grates/) options before you commit to a specific replacement is worth the time , particularly if your grill is an older model and compatibility documentation is thin.

### Thickness and Rod Spacing

Thicker rods retain heat longer between flips and between batches. On a cast iron grate, rod thickness also affects the quality of sear marks and how aggressively the grate preheats. Thinner rods heat faster but drop temperature more quickly , which matters when you're cooking multiple pieces of protein in sequence.

Rod spacing affects what you can cook. Wider spacing is fine for burgers and steaks, but vegetables, fish, and smaller cuts can fall through or stick awkwardly. If your cooking involves anything smaller than a chicken thigh, closer rod spacing matters.

### Maintenance Requirements Before You Buy

Cast iron grates need to be seasoned before first use and re-seasoned periodically. That means coating them with a thin layer of oil and running them through a burn-in cycle. It takes about an hour and it's not complicated, but if you skip it, you'll be dealing with rust within a season.

Porcelain-coated grates need gentler cleaning , wire brushes can chip the coating, which accelerates deterioration. Stainless grates are the most forgiving on cleaning but still benefit from brushing while hot and a light wipe-down after each cook. Whatever material you choose, maintenance consistency determines lifespan more than the grate itself.

## Buying Guide

### Start With Your Grill Model Number

Every other decision follows from fit. Before you evaluate material, price band, or brand, pull your grill's model number. It's usually on a label inside the lid, on the back of the firebox, or stamped on the cart frame. Write it down , you'll need it to cross-reference compatibility lists on any replacement grate listing.

Don't rely solely on grill brand. Weber makes dozens of grill configurations across Spirit, Genesis, and kettle lines, and a grate that fits a Spirit E-310 won't fit a Genesis II. Charbroil, Nexgrill, and Chargriller have the same problem. Model number is the only reliable starting point.

### Cast Iron vs. Aftermarket Alternatives: Making the Call

The case for cast iron replacement grates is straightforward for anyone who cooks regularly and doesn't mind a few minutes of maintenance after each session. Better sear, better heat retention, longer useful life , those aren't marketing claims. They're what cast iron does when maintained.

The case against cast iron is equally honest. If your grill sits unused for weeks between cooks, or if you live in a humid climate and aren't consistent about drying the grates after cleaning, rust will find you. Porcelain-coated or stainless replacement grates may actually perform better in practice for a buyer who grills occasionally and wants minimum fuss.

Be honest about your cooking habits before you make this call. The [grill grates and tools](/tools-and-grates/) category has options across all three materials , buying the right one for how you actually cook beats buying the technically superior one that you won't maintain.

### OEM Parts vs. Third-Party Replacements

OEM (original equipment manufacturer) parts come from the grill's own brand. Weber-branded grates for a Weber grill, for example. They fit as designed, carry the brand's quality standards, and usually come with warranty coverage. The trade-off is cost , OEM parts are generally more expensive than third-party alternatives.

Third-party replacements can offer equivalent or sometimes better material quality at a lower cost. The risk is fit and consistency. A grate listed as compatible with your model may arrive slightly undersized, oversize, or with rod spacing that differs from what you expected. Reading reviews from buyers with your specific model number is the most useful due-diligence step before ordering third-party.

For Weber grills specifically, the OEM vs. aftermarket question is particularly active because Weber parts are widely stocked and the brand holds its value in the used market. A genuine Weber grate on a Weber grill is a safe choice. An aftermarket grate at a lower cost is a reasonable gamble if the compatibility reviews are strong.

### How Many Grates Do You Need?

Most gas grills use two or three grate sections across the full cooking surface. Replacing only one section is almost always a mistake , the old and new grates will wear differently, and you'll have inconsistent surface height, potentially uneven cooking, and a mismatched seasoning situation on cast iron.

Buy a full set when you replace. If your grill uses two sections, replace both. If it uses three, replace all three. The C6505A 4-pack and the Charbroil 3-pack listings are structured this way deliberately , the quantity matches typical multi-section cooking surface configurations.

### Seasoning and First-Use Preparation

Any cast iron grate needs to be seasoned before it touches food. The process is simple: wash to remove any factory coating or residue, dry completely, apply a thin layer of oil to all surfaces, and run your grill at high heat for 45, 60 minutes. Let it cool, wipe off any excess, and repeat once more.

After that, maintain seasoning by brushing the grates while hot after each cook and applying a light oil coat before storage if the grill will sit unused for more than a week. Re-season fully if you see orange rust developing , it's recoverable, but it means the protective layer broke down somewhere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do replacement grill grates from third-party brands fit as well as OEM parts?

They can, but fit quality varies more than with OEM parts. Third-party manufacturers sometimes produce grates with slight dimensional differences that affect how securely the grate seats in the grill frame. Reading reviews specifically from buyers who own your grill model , not just the model line , is the most reliable way to assess fit before ordering. When reviews confirm a clean fit on your exact model, third-party grates are a reasonable choice.
### Is cast iron worth the maintenance compared to porcelain-coated replacement grates?

For regular grillers, yes. Cast iron retains heat more effectively, produces better sear marks, and lasts longer when maintained. The maintenance requirement , seasoning before first use, drying thoroughly after cleaning, re-seasoning if rust appears , is roughly 10 minutes per session. If you grill infrequently or live somewhere humid and won't be consistent about maintenance, porcelain-coated grates may serve you better in practice.
### Can I use replacement Weber grates on a non-Weber grill if the dimensions match?

Technically yes, if the dimensions are truly identical and the grate sits properly in the frame. In practice, grill manufacturers design their cooking surfaces around specific grate profiles, and a grate designed for one brand's support system may not seat correctly in another's even when the outer dimensions are close. Measure carefully and confirm the seating geometry, not just the length and width.
### How do I know if I need to replace my grates or just clean them?

Surface rust that hasn't pitted into the metal is recoverable , re-season cast iron or sand and repaint porcelain-coated grates with heat-resistant coating. Deep pitting, warped rods that create uneven cooking height, or broken welds at the rod junctions are the signs that replacement makes more sense than repair. If grates flex noticeably when you push down on them or have missing sections, replace them.
### What's the difference between the Leship aftermarket Weber grates and the OEM Weber Hinged Cooking Grate?

The [WEBER HINGED COOKING GRATE](product:weber-hinged-cooking-grate) is made by Weber and carries the brand's manufacturing standards and quality assurance. The [Leship 17.5 inch Grill Grates Replace for Weber 7638](product:leship-17-5-inch-grill) is a third-party alternative sized for many of the same Spirit and Genesis models, at a lower cost but without the established brand warranty. The hinged design of the Weber grate also adds practical functionality that the Leship flat grate doesn't offer , useful if you run charcoal inserts or need mid-cook fire access.
