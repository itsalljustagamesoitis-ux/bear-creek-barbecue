Getting great pizza off a pellet grill isn't as simple as cranking up the heat and hoping for the best. The right attachment , or a dedicated outdoor oven , changes what your backyard setup can actually do, pushing temperatures well past what a standard grill grate ever reaches and creating the conditions for a properly blistered crust. If you're already running a Traeger or another pellet grill, there's a real question worth answering: do you add to what you already own, or do you buy something purpose-built?

I've spent enough time chasing that answer to have opinions. This is the breakdown of the [pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) and attachments that make the most sense for pellet grill owners.

## What to Look For in a Traeger Pizza Oven Setup

### Temperature Ceiling

Pizza dough behaves differently at 700°F than it does at 400°F. A standard pellet grill runs somewhere in the mid-500s at absolute maximum , and most top out lower than that under real cooking conditions. Authentic Neapolitan-style pizza needs a floor that's radiating intense, direct heat while the dome reflects it back down onto the top of the pie. That's a very different environment from standard grilling.

Attachments and dedicated ovens address this by creating an enclosed chamber that concentrates heat. The best designs in this category push past 800°F, with some claiming over 1,000°F. The practical difference between 700°F and 950°F is measurable in cook time and crust texture , a 60-second cook at high heat produces a fundamentally different result than a 4-minute cook at moderate heat.

### Enclosed Chamber Design

Heat containment is the mechanism that makes the temperature numbers real. An open grate with high ambient heat isn't a pizza oven , it's just a hot grill. The attachments that work well create a box around the pizza: a stone floor, a ceiling that reflects radiant heat, and minimal openings that bleed temperature. The tighter that enclosure, the faster the oven recovers after you open the door or slide a pie in.

Stone mass matters here. Cordierite or ceramic pizza stones absorb and re-radiate heat. A metal-only floor gets hot, but it doesn't hold or release heat the way stone does. Any attachment worth considering includes a proper pizza stone , or at minimum, provisions to use one.

### Compatibility and Fit

Pellet grill attachments aren't universal. A Traeger Pro 575 has a specific barrel dimension, lid clearance, and grate width. An attachment designed for that form factor may not physically fit a Pit Boss or Camp Chef, even if the marketing copy implies otherwise. Check the listed compatible models against your actual grill model number before ordering.

The issue isn't just physical fit , it's also airflow. Pellet grills draw combustion air from the bottom and exhaust from the rear. An attachment that blocks that airflow path will cause your grill to run hotter, produce more smoke than intended, and potentially trigger thermal anomalies. The best attachments account for grill-specific airflow geometry, not just footprint dimensions. Browsing the full range of [outdoor pizza oven options](/pizza-ovens/) is worth doing before you commit to an attachment route , sometimes a standalone unit solves the compatibility problem entirely.

### Portability and Storage

A dedicated pizza oven like the Ooni Karu is built to travel. It folds into a compact form, weighs under 30 pounds, and can go from a tailgate to a camping site to a backyard deck without adaptation. Pellet grill attachments stay in one place , they assume a fixed grill location.

If your cooking setup is mobile, or if you want pizza capability independent of your pellet grill, a portable standalone unit has a structural advantage. If your Traeger lives permanently on your patio and you want pizza nights without setting up additional gear, an attachment approach keeps things simpler.

## Buying Guide

### Attachment vs. Standalone: The Core Decision

The attachment vs. standalone question drives every other decision in this category. Attachments are cheaper, require existing compatible hardware, and keep your equipment footprint smaller. Standalone units are more capable, thermally optimized for pizza from the ground up, and work regardless of what else you own. If you have a compatible pellet grill and pizza nights are occasional rather than weekly, an attachment is the practical choice. If pizza is a regular pursuit and you want the best possible result, a standalone oven earns its patio space.

### Compatibility Verification

Don't assume compatibility , verify it. Attachment manufacturers list supported models by brand name, but pellet grills within the same brand vary significantly by generation and size. A 575-square-inch Traeger and an 885-square-inch Traeger have different grate widths, lid heights, and side shelf configurations. The attachment that fits one may not fit the other. Pull up the grill's exact model number and cross-reference it against the manufacturer's compatibility list before ordering.

Fitment issues aren't just inconvenient , a poorly seated attachment leaks heat, undermines the temperature ceiling you paid for, and can affect grill performance on non-pizza cooks. Getting this right before purchase saves a return shipment.

### Temperature as a Decision Variable

The temperature ceiling you need depends on the style of pizza you're making. Thick-crust or pan-style pizza bakes well in the 500, 600°F range that a pellet grill handles without any attachment. Thin Neapolitan-style pizza, the kind with a blistered, chewy crust and a slightly charred undercarriage, needs 700°F at minimum and performs best above 900°F. NY-style falls somewhere in between. Know what you're making before you let the temperature spec drive the purchase , there's no reason to buy a unit rated for 1,100°F if your household runs exclusively thick-crust pies.

The [pizza oven options](/pizza-ovens/) available today cover a meaningful range of temperature performance. Matching the ceiling to your actual cooking style avoids both overspending on capability you won't use and under-buying into a setup that frustrates you.

### Pizza Stone Quality

The stone is where the crust actually cooks. A thin, small, or low-quality stone will crack under repeated thermal cycling, heat unevenly, and produce inconsistent results across a pizza. Cordierite stone is the material to look for , it handles thermal shock better than standard ceramic and retains heat more effectively. Thickness matters too: a thicker stone holds more heat mass and recovers faster after each pie.

Some attachments include a stone; some don't. If the attachment you're evaluating doesn't include one, price in a quality cordierite stone as part of the total investment. A cheap stone will fail, and a failed stone mid-cook is a ruined dinner.

### Fuel Type and Flavor

Pellet grill attachments run on whatever pellets your grill uses, which means you get wood-smoke character baked into your pizza , subtly, but it's there. That's a genuine differentiator compared to gas-only pizza ovens. If the wood-smoke flavor is part of why you're pursuing this route, stay in the pellet attachment category. If you want maximum temperature repeatability and you don't care about smoke flavor, a propane-capable standalone unit like the Ooni Karu gives you more precise heat control. Multi-fuel units offer the most flexibility, but running them in a single-fuel configuration usually gives better performance than switching modes frequently.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a pizza oven attachment work with any Traeger model?

No. Compatibility depends on your specific Traeger model's dimensions and lid clearance, not just the brand name. Pro series grills typically fit the most attachments on the market. Ironwood and Timberline models have different geometries that can cause clearance issues with attachments designed for standard barrel configurations. Check the attachment's compatibility list against your exact model number , not just "Traeger."
### How hot does a pellet grill pizza attachment actually get?

Most attachments on this list are rated between 800°F and 1,000°F under optimal conditions. Real-world performance depends on ambient temperature, pellet quality, and how long you preheat. Budget at least 20, 30 minutes of preheat time before cooking. Standalone dedicated units like the [Ooni Karu 12](product:ooni-karu-12-multi-fuel) reach 950°F and tend to be more consistent because the entire oven is optimized for that temperature range rather than adapted from a general-purpose grill.
### What size pizza can I cook with these attachments?

Most attachments and small portable ovens in this category accommodate 12-inch pizzas. That's a reasonable single-pie size for two to three people. If you're regularly feeding a family of four or hosting groups, expect to cook multiple pies sequentially , there's no single-chamber option here that handles a 16-inch pie. The [BIG HORN 12" Multi-Fuel Outdoor Pizza Oven](product:big-horn-12-multi-fuel) names the 12-inch limit directly in the product title, which is honest sizing disclosure that not all options provide.
### Should I buy the Green Mountain Grills attachment if I own a different pellet grill brand?

No. The [Green Mountain Grills Wood-Fired Steel Pizza Oven Attachment](product:green-mountain-grills-wood-fired-steel-pizza-oven) is designed specifically for the Daniel Boone and Jim Bowie models. If you don't own one of those two grills, the GMG attachment isn't compatible regardless of how closely your grill resembles them in size.
### Is a standalone pizza oven better than a pellet grill attachment?
For pure pizza performance, yes , a purpose-built oven outperforms an adapted grill. Standalone units reach higher temperatures more consistently and recover faster between pies. The trade-off is cost, equipment footprint, and the loss of that wood-smoke flavor layered in through pellet combustion. If you own a compatible pellet grill and cook pizza occasionally, an attachment delivers a meaningful upgrade at a reasonable cost.
