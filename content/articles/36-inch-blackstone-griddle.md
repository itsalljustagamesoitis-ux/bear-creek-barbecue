The 36-inch Blackstone griddle has become the default answer for backyard cooks who want serious flat-top capacity without committing to a built-in outdoor kitchen. If you're comparing models and wondering which configuration actually fits your setup, you're in the right place. One detail separates the satisfying purchase from the frustrating one: these models share a footprint but differ meaningfully in hood design, portability, and durability. Explore the full range of [griddles](/griddles/) before narrowing your focus.

The honest answer is that most buyers don't need every feature on the most expensive configuration. Hood style, shelf design, and whether the legs fold matters more to long-term satisfaction than BTU ratings most people never push to their limit.

## What to Look For in a 36-Inch Blackstone Griddle

### Hood Design and Coverage

The hood is where these models diverge most visibly. A hard cover included from the factory , not a separate purchase , protects the cooking surface from weather, debris, and oxidation between cooks. That matters in Ohio weather, where a griddle sitting uncovered through a rainy week will need seasoning work before it's usable again.

Beyond protection, an integrated hood changes how you cook. Covered cooking traps heat above the surface, letting you finish thicker cuts without cranking the burners. An X-braced hood offers structural stability over multiple seasons. Standard hoods work fine; they just flex more over time. Know which you're getting before you buy.

Not every 36-inch Blackstone ships with any hood at all. Baseline models leave you exposed , which means purchasing a separate cover becomes mandatory, not optional.

### Cooking Surface Construction

All Blackstone griddles use rolled steel cooking surfaces, and that's the right material for flat-top cooking. Steel seasons over time, becomes naturally non-stick with use, and handles high heat without warping when it's properly cared for. The distinction worth paying attention to is surface thickness and how evenly the four burners distribute heat across that full 36-inch span.

Iron forged construction with powder-coated steel framing adds durability to the structure underneath the cooking surface. That's the frame and body taking a different approach from sheet metal , more resistant to denting and corrosion over multiple outdoor seasons. If you cook regularly and leave the griddle outside, construction quality below the cooking surface matters as much as what's on top.

For a deeper look at how flat-top surfaces compare to traditional grates, the [griddles](/griddles/) hub covers the full category.

### Portability and Leg Configuration

Foldable legs are a genuine feature, not a gimmick, if you have a specific storage constraint. A patio that doubles as a kids' play area, a garage with limited floor space, or any setup where the griddle needs to move regularly makes folding legs worth having. They add a small amount of flex to the assembled unit but nothing that affects cooking performance.

Fixed legs with caster wheels solve a different problem. You can roll the unit around a patio to chase shade or reposition for serving flow, but it stays assembled and stable between cooks. The trade-off is floor space. A 36-inch griddle on fixed casters occupies its footprint permanently.

### Side Shelf Configuration

Extendable side shelves are the detail that separates a griddle that works well as a cooking station from one that creates frustration mid-cook. Smash burgers need a landing zone. Breakfast for six means you're managing eggs, bacon, pancakes, and toast simultaneously , you need somewhere to stage food that's off the heat but not on a separate table across the patio.

Fixed folding shelves are more common. They work adequately and collapse for storage. Extendable shelves , ones that slide out to expand the work surface , provide meaningfully more room without permanently widening the unit's footprint. If you cook for groups regularly, this is worth factoring into the decision.

### Grease Management

Flat-top cooking generates more grease runoff than grates, and how that grease exits the cooking surface affects both cleanup time and cook quality. A rear grease system routes runoff away from the front of the griddle , cleaner than systems that drain toward the cook. A front cup works but requires more attention mid-cook to prevent overflow.

Manual grease pan management means emptying a cup after every heavy cook. That's not a dealbreaker. It is a ten-minute job you need to build into the habit, because skipping it leads to flare-ups and a griddle that smells like old grease from six feet away.

## Buying Guide

### Choosing Between Portable and Stationary Configurations

The foldable-leg versus caster-wheel question comes down to how you actually use the griddle, not how you imagine using it. Foldable legs serve cooks who regularly transport the unit or need to reclaim floor space after cooking. Caster wheels serve cooks who want to stay put but need occasional repositioning across a patio. Most suburban backyard cooks fall into the second category and don't realize it until after they've bought a folding model.

Think about where the griddle lives between cooks. If it stays outside year-round, portability matters less than weather resistance and structural stability. If it comes in and out of a garage, foldable legs earn their value quickly.

### Hood vs. No Hood

A hood changes the cooking envelope, not just the weather protection. Without a hood, you're cooking in open air , heat radiates up and away, which limits how effectively you can finish thicker proteins without overcooking the exterior. With a hood down, you're creating a contained heat environment that behaves more like an oven above the griddle surface. That's useful for melting cheese on smash burgers, steaming vegetables, or finishing chicken thighs through to temperature.

The cover function matters separately. A hard cover included from the factory protects the cooking surface between uses. A weather-resistant polyester cover does the same job, though not quite as rigidly. If your chosen model doesn't include either, budget for the [BLACKSTONE 5482 Griddle Cover](product:blackstone-5482-griddle-cover-fits-36) as a required add-on rather than optional accessory. Explore how different [griddle](/griddles/) configurations handle weather protection to understand what you're trading off.

### Heat Output and Burner Count

All 36-inch Blackstone models run four burners. The standard output is 60,000 BTU total , 15,000 per burner. That figure gets cited frequently in marketing because it sounds impressive. The more meaningful number is how evenly those four burners distribute heat across the full width of the cooking surface, which is a function of burner spacing and griddle thickness rather than raw BTU count.

For practical outdoor cooking at suburban scale, 60,000 BTU is more than enough. You're not cooking at full blast on all four burners simultaneously in most weekend-cook scenarios. Zone cooking , keeping one side lower for holding and one side high for searing , is how most experienced flat-top cooks actually use the surface.

### Assembly and Storage Reality

First-time griddle buyers routinely underestimate setup complexity. A 36-inch Blackstone with a hood, extendable shelves, and caster wheels requires real assembly out of the box , typically 45 to 90 minutes the first time. That's not a complaint, just a realistic expectation. Budget the time and have two people available if possible.

Storage is the other underestimated variable. A 36-inch griddle on fixed legs occupies roughly four feet of width and two feet of depth. Measure your storage space before purchasing, not after. Models with foldable legs collapse the height dimension but not the footprint , plan accordingly.

### Seasoning and First-Cook Preparation

Every Blackstone griddle requires seasoning before the first cook. This is non-negotiable, not optional. A new cooking surface has manufacturing residue and no protective oil layer. Running through the seasoning process , three to four cycles of thin oil application and high-heat burn-off , creates the non-stick surface the griddle will maintain for years with proper care.

The first cook should be something forgiving: bacon works well because the fat contributes to the seasoning layer while you cook. Don't start with eggs. Don't start with delicate fish. Earn the surface with a few cooks before demanding precision from it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the iron forged Blackstone and the standard 36-inch model?

The iron forged construction on the BLACKSTONE 2310 refers to the frame and body material , thicker, more rigid, and more resistant to corrosion than standard sheet steel construction. Both models use a rolled steel cooking surface and four-burner layout. The meaningful difference is long-term durability in outdoor conditions. The iron forged model is the better investment if the griddle lives outside year-round through variable weather.
### Do all 36-inch Blackstone griddles come with a hood included?

No. Some configurations ship with a built-in hood and some do not. The baseline portable model with foldable legs is typically sold without a hood. If weather protection matters to your setup , and it should , either choose a model that includes a hood, or add the [BLACKSTONE 5482 Griddle Cover](product:blackstone-5482-griddle-cover-fits-36) to your order as a required accessory rather than treating it as optional.
### Is a 36-inch Blackstone too large for a standard suburban patio?
It depends on the patio configuration, not just the square footage. A 36-inch griddle on casters occupies roughly four feet of width with shelves extended. The more common constraint is clearance from walls, fences, or structures , propane-fueled griddles need open-air ventilation. Measure the intended cooking position, not just the storage location.
### How does the rear grease system compare to a front grease cup?

Rear grease systems route runoff to the back of the cooking surface, away from the cook's position, and tend to manage high-volume grease output more cleanly during active cooking. Front cup systems work but require more monitoring during extended cooks , an overflowing front cup creates a mess and a flare-up risk. For breakfast cooks managing bacon and sausage simultaneously, rear grease routing is a genuine quality-of-life improvement over the cook session.
### Can I use a 36-inch Blackstone griddle year-round in cold weather climates?
Yes, with adjustments. Cold ambient temperatures slow the surface heat-up time and require higher burner settings to maintain cooking temperature. Wind is a more significant factor than cold , a windscreen or sheltered position matters more in winter cooks than the temperature itself. Proper seasoning maintenance before winter storage prevents rust during extended non-use periods.
