A grill cover is the cheapest insurance you'll buy for your [gas grill](/gas-grills/). One Ohio winter , freeze-thaw cycles, ice, wind , and an uncovered grill looks five years older than it is. The burners corrode, the igniter fails, and the grease trap turns into something you don't want to think about.

The problem is that most covers look identical on the listing page. Heavy-duty. Waterproof. UV-resistant. The marketing copy is almost interchangeable. What separates covers that last two seasons from covers that crack and fade by July is material weight, fastening design, and fit , and none of those things show up clearly in a thumbnail.

## What to Look For in a Gas Grill Cover

### Material Weight and Construction

A cover's weight tells you more than the marketing language does. Thin polyester feels fine in the store bag but turns brittle after a few months of UV exposure. Look for descriptions of denier rating , the higher the number, the denser the weave. Most covers in this category are 420D to 600D polyester or Oxford cloth. The difference between them is real and noticeable after a full summer outdoors.

Waterproofing is applied as a coating, not woven in. That means it degrades over time, especially with repeated folding and UV exposure. A heavier base fabric holds the coating longer. Covers that advertise both waterproofing and UV resistance are worth prioritizing , UV degradation is what causes cracking and fading, and it starts faster than most buyers expect.

### Fit: Sizing and Shape

A 58-inch cover fits most standard two- or three-burner gas grills. A 55-inch cover suits compact grills. Before ordering, measure your grill at its widest point , usually the side shelf handles , not the cooking surface. A cover that's too wide balloons in wind and traps moisture underneath. One that's too narrow won't protect the side tables where corrosion actually starts.

Shape matters for grills with side burners or storage cabinets. A boxy, squared-off cover fits differently than one cut for a narrower profile. If your grill has an extended side burner, measure depth as well as width.

### Fastening Systems

Wind is the enemy of a loose cover. A cover that blows off your Traeger at 2 a.m. isn't protecting anything. Straps with buckles or hook-and-loop closures at the bottom hem are the most secure option. Drawstrings alone stretch and fray. The best fastening systems use a combination , an adjustable hem drawstring to cinch the bottom close to the grill legs, plus side straps to prevent the whole thing from lifting in a gust.

Check that the buckles are weather-resistant plastic or coated metal. Cheap metal buckles rust and become impossible to operate by the second spring.

### Ventilation

A fully sealed cover sounds appealing but creates a condensation problem in temperature-swing climates. Moisture that gets trapped inside has nowhere to go and accelerates the rusting it was supposed to prevent. Vents , usually small mesh panels near the top , allow airflow without letting rain in. They're a small detail that makes a real difference over a full year of use. If you're reviewing the full range of [gas grills and accessories](/gas-grills/) before buying, ventilation in the cover is worth weighing alongside grill material and finish.

### Durability Markers to Look For

Reinforced handle cutouts, double-stitched seams, and fade-resistant dyes are the details that distinguish a cover that makes it three seasons from one that doesn't. Seam failure is the most common point of breakdown , water wicks through unfinished seams even when the main fabric is waterproof. Double-stitched seams with sealed edges hold up. Single-stitch construction around the hem is where covers typically split first.

## Buying Guide

### Measure Your Grill Before You Order

The single most common mistake buyers make is ordering by grill burner count rather than actual grill dimensions. A three-burner Weber Genesis and a three-burner Nexgrill can differ by four inches in width. Measure from the outermost points of your grill , typically the side shelf handles , at the widest horizontal span. Then measure depth from front to back, and height from the ground to the highest point of the lid. Width is the critical number, but depth determines whether the cover sits flush or gaps at the front and rear.

If your grill measures between 50 and 55 inches, a 55-inch cover fits cleanly. If it measures 56 to 60 inches, a 58-inch cover is the right range. A 60-inch cover handles grills on the larger end of the standard residential spectrum. Going one size up is generally fine; going significantly oversized creates wind and pooling problems.

### How Weather Conditions Should Drive Your Priorities

A buyer in Phoenix needs UV resistance above everything else. Sun degrades cover coatings faster than rain does, and a cover that fades and cracks after a single summer offers no protection in its second year. A buyer in a freeze-thaw climate , Ohio, Michigan, the mid-Atlantic , needs material that stays flexible at low temperatures. Thin polyester becomes brittle and cracks when folded or compressed in freezing conditions. Material weight and flexibility matter more in cold climates than in warm ones.

Coastal environments add a third variable: salt air accelerates corrosion on exposed metal components, including buckles and straps. Coated plastic buckles outlast metal ones in salt-air conditions. If you're on the coast, prioritize covers with all-plastic fastening hardware.

### Fastening Systems and Wind Exposure

Most cover failures in wind aren't fabric tears , they're the cover blowing off entirely and the grill sitting uncovered for days before anyone notices. A cover with secure fastening is more important than marginally thicker fabric if your grill is in an exposed location. Dual-fastening systems , drawstring plus side straps , are meaningfully more secure than drawstring alone.

For grills on covered patios or in sheltered areas, fastening is a secondary concern. A simpler strap system is sufficient when wind isn't a primary threat. Match the fastening spec to your actual exposure conditions rather than always defaulting to maximum hardware.

### Storage Versus Active-Season Use

There's a practical difference between a cover used for long-term off-season storage and one used repeatedly through grilling season. A heavy, stiff cover that's excellent for winter storage can be annoying to wrestle on and off every weekend during June through September. Softer, more flexible materials , like spun polyester , make active-season use more convenient. Heavier Oxford cloth is the better choice for storage-focused use.

If you grill frequently and only cover between sessions, factor in ease of installation. If the cover lives on the grill from October through April and comes off in spring, prioritize durability over convenience. ### Ventilation and Condensation

A common issue with grill covers in climates with significant temperature swings is interior condensation. When warm air trapped under the cover meets a cold grill surface, moisture forms on the burner components and interior surfaces , exactly what the cover was supposed to prevent. Vented covers with mesh panels near the top allow airflow and dramatically reduce condensation buildup. The vent opening is small enough to prevent rain ingress but large enough to prevent the greenhouse effect that drives condensation.

If you store your grill in an unheated garage or shed and use a cover for extra protection, condensation is less of an issue. Outdoors in a climate with temperature-swing seasons, it's worth checking whether the cover you're considering includes ventilation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know what size grill cover to buy?

Measure your grill at its widest point , usually the outer edges of the side shelf handles, not the cooking surface. Width is the critical dimension. Most standard residential gas grills fall between 50 and 60 inches wide, which is why covers cluster at 55 and 58 inches. A cover that's slightly large is preferable to one that's too small, but significant excess fabric catches wind and pools water.
### What's the difference between a 55-inch and a 58-inch grill cover?

Three inches of coverage width determines whether the cover fits your grill cleanly or gaps at the sides. Compact two-burner grills typically measure 50, 55 inches wide and fit best under a 55-inch cover like the [SunPatio Grill Cover](product:sunpatio-grill-cover-55-inch). Standard three-burner grills typically measure 55, 58 inches and fit the 58-inch options well. Measure before ordering , burner count is an unreliable proxy for width.
### Is a heavier cover always better?

Heavier covers with higher denier fabric hold their waterproof coating longer and resist UV degradation better than thin alternatives. That said, heavier covers are stiffer and harder to install and remove repeatedly. If you're covering and uncovering your grill multiple times per week during grilling season, a mid-weight cover with good fastening , like the [Grill Cover 58 Inch BBQ](product:grill-cover-58-inch-bbq) with its dual hook-and-loop and drawstring system , is often more practical than the heaviest option available.
### Can a grill cover cause rust instead of preventing it?
Yes, if the cover traps moisture rather than repelling it. A fully sealed cover with no ventilation creates a condensation problem in climates with temperature swings. Moisture forms on the interior surfaces and has nowhere to escape. Vented covers with mesh panels near the top prevent this by allowing airflow.
### How long should a quality grill cover last?

A well-made cover with 420D or heavier fabric, a proper UV-resistant coating, and double-stitched seams should last three to five years with normal outdoor exposure. The waterproof coating degrades first, typically showing signs of moisture penetration in years two or three. UV-protective treatments extend the coating's useful life. Budget covers with thin fabric often show cracking and fading within a single season in high-UV or high-freeze-thaw environments.
