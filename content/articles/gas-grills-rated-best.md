Finding a gas grill that earns the label "best rated" takes more than scanning star counts. The details that separate a grill worth owning from one that disappoints after a season , heat consistency, grate quality, burner layout , get buried in spec sheets. I've spent enough time on [gas grills](/gas-grills/) to know that the right choice depends heavily on how you actually cook, not just how many burners the box advertises.

The field here is narrower than it looks. Three Weber lines and one Charbroil model cover a range of cooking surfaces, price bands, and design philosophies. What follows is a straight assessment of each, starting with what the criteria actually mean before any product gets named.

## What to Look For in a Gas Grill

### Burner Count and Cooking Zones

Burner count matters less than burner layout. A two-burner grill can run a two-zone setup , direct heat on one side, indirect on the other , which handles most of what a backyard cook needs. Add a third burner and you gain the ability to keep a zone at a low, steady hold while cooking something hotter elsewhere. Four burners open up more simultaneous temperature management, which becomes genuinely useful when you're feeding a crowd and need to move food between zones without pulling it off the grates.

The number that actually determines capacity isn't the burner count , it's the primary cooking area measured in square inches. Know how many people you're regularly cooking for before you commit. A grill sized for two households takes longer to preheat and burns through fuel faster than you'd expect for a household of four.

### Grate Material and Heat Retention

Cast-iron grates retain heat better than stainless steel but require more maintenance. A porcelain-enamel coating on cast iron splits the difference: rust resistance without sacrificing the thermal mass that gives you a real sear. Bare stainless grates heat unevenly unless the gauge is heavy, and most entry-level stainless grates are not heavy enough to cover the gap.

Sear zone features , areas of concentrated infrared heat or boosted BTUs , address this limitation directly. If getting crust on a steak matters to you, look for a grill that designates part of the cooking surface to high-heat output rather than distributing all burners at equal intensity.

### BTU Output and Efficiency

BTU figures in marketing material are almost always misleading in isolation. A grill that outputs 40,000 BTUs across a small cooking area is running hotter per square inch than one outputting 48,000 BTUs across a much larger surface. The ratio of BTUs to primary cooking area is the number worth calculating.

Infrared burner technology changes this calculation further. Infrared systems heat grate surfaces directly rather than heating air that then heats the grate, which means they achieve equivalent cooking results at lower fuel consumption. For propane grills specifically , where fuel cost is a running expense , efficiency compounds over a full season of weekend cooking.

### Build Quality and Longevity

A grill is outdoor furniture that runs at high temperatures and sits in weather. The weakest points in any grill's lifespan are the ignition system, the burner tubes, and the grease management system. Ignitions fail from moisture; burner tubes corrode or clog; grease management systems that trap grease near heat are fire risks.

Before purchasing, check whether replacement burner parts are available and what they cost. Weber's parts availability is strong , a meaningful long-term advantage over brands that discontinue components within two or three model cycles. For more guidance on durability factors, the [gas grill category](/gas-grills/) covers long-term ownership considerations in depth.

### Cabinet Design and Side Burners

An enclosed cabinet base keeps propane tanks contained, protects storage from weather, and improves the overall look of the grill station. Open-cart designs are lighter and less expensive but leave the tank visible and offer less workspace. Side burners are more useful than they appear in marketing photos , having a burner for a sauce or a side dish without running back inside solves a real problem for anyone who cooks full meals outdoors.

## Top Picks

### Weber Spirit E-210 Liquid Propane Gas Grill

The [Weber Spirit E-210](product:weber-spirit-e-210-liquid) is where I'd point anyone who wants a reliable Weber in a smaller footprint. Two burners keep the cooking surface manageable, and the Snap-Jet ignition system is consistent enough that you stop thinking about it after the first few uses. Porcelain-enameled cast-iron grates give you the heat retention you'd expect from a grill twice the size.

Two burners do impose a real constraint: you're running a single split-zone setup, which works for most weeknight cooks but starts to feel limited when you're managing multiple proteins at different temperatures. The Spirit E-210 is honest about being a two-burner grill , it doesn't pretend to replace a larger setup.

This is the right starting point for a household that grills two or three times a week, cooks for four or fewer people regularly, and doesn't want to maintain a larger grill through the whole season. Weber's parts ecosystem means you're not orphaned when something eventually needs replacing.

### Weber Spirit E-325 Liquid Propane Gas Grill

Three burners plus two boost burners make the [Weber Spirit E-325](product:weber-spirit-e-325-liquid) a meaningfully different grill than the E-210, not just a marginal upgrade. The boost burners create a dedicated sear zone , high-output heat concentrated where you want it , while the main burners handle indirect or lower-heat cooking on the rest of the surface.

The sear zone matters if you're cooking steaks, thick chops, or anything where crust development is the goal. Spirit-series grills have historically been Weber's workhorse line , built to hold up over years of regular use without requiring the attention a higher-tier grill demands.

Five burner elements running at different outputs add some complexity to the learning curve. The first few cooks involve finding which combination of main burners and boost burners gets the grates to the temperature you want. Once you've mapped it out, the setup becomes intuitive.

### Weber Genesis E-325 Liquid Propane Gas Grill

The [Weber Genesis E-325](product:weber-genesis-e-325-liquid) sits a tier above the Spirit line in build quality and feature set. PureBlu burners run more evenly than conventional tube burners, which translates to fewer hot spots and more predictable results across the full cooking surface. Porcelain-enameled cast-iron grates hold temperature the way they're supposed to.

Three burners give you a full three-zone cooking setup , high heat, medium, and a hold zone , which is enough flexibility for most backyard cooks. Genesis-series grills are built heavier than the Spirit line, and that mass shows up in heat retention and overall longevity.

The trade-off is that you're paying for that construction. If you cook frequently and plan to own the grill for eight or more years, the Genesis line's durability earns the investment. If you grill occasionally or are still figuring out how much you'll actually use it, the Spirit line is the more practical entry point.

### Weber Genesis S-315 Liquid Propane Gas Grill

The [Weber Genesis S-315](product:weber-genesis-s-315-liquid) shares the same PureBlu burner system as the E-325 with one significant difference in materials: stainless steel grates replace the porcelain-enameled cast-iron. That's a genuine trade-off, not a straightforward upgrade. Stainless is easier to clean and doesn't risk chipping, but it gives up the heat retention that makes cast iron perform the way it does.

Three burners across the same cooking footprint as the E-325 mean the performance profile is similar, with the grate material being the meaningful differentiator. Stainless steel also handles outdoor exposure well , it won't rust if you let the cover slip for a week, whereas neglected cast iron will show it.

The S-315 is the better choice for someone who prioritizes low-maintenance ownership over maximum sear performance. If you want the Genesis-tier build without managing cast-iron grates, this is the model to pick.

The [Charbroil Pro Series with Amplifire Infrared](product:charbroil-pro-series-with-amplifire-infrared) is the outlier in this group, and deliberately so. Four burners running infrared technology across a cabinet-style base is a different cooking experience than a conventional gas grill. Infrared heat reaches the grate surface directly, producing higher surface temperatures at lower fuel consumption than standard convective burners.

The cabinet design earns its place. Enclosed storage for the propane tank, solid workspace, and a side burner for managing sauces or sides make this a complete outdoor cooking station rather than a standalone grill. The side burner alone eliminates a meaningful number of trips back to the kitchen during a cook.

Four infrared burners require more attention during the first few sessions than conventional burners , the heat profile is different, and food that would take a specific time on a conventional grill will cook faster here. Infrared rewards the cook who pays attention to what's on the grates rather than relying on timer-based instinct.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Grill Size to Your Actual Usage

The most common mistake in buying a gas grill is overestimating how often you'll cook for a crowd. Most backyard cooks grill for four to six people the majority of the time, with larger gatherings being the exception. A grill sized for twenty people will preheat slowly and cycle through fuel on every routine weeknight cook.

A two-burner model handles the four-person household. A three-burner opens up zone flexibility for the cook who wants to run simultaneous temperatures. Four burners make sense when you're regularly cooking for eight or more, or when you want the station-style setup that comes with a cabinet grill.

### Propane vs. Natural Gas Conversion

All five grills reviewed here run propane. Propane is convenient , no permanent installation, portable, and available at any hardware or grocery store. The ongoing cost and logistics of tank management are real, though. Running out mid-cook is a problem everyone eventually has once.

Natural gas conversions are available for some Weber models, which connects the grill to a home gas line and eliminates tank logistics permanently. Verify conversion kit availability for your specific model before purchasing if you have a gas line available on your patio , not all Spirit and Genesis variants support it.

### Weber vs. Charbroil: Two Different Design Philosophies

Weber's approach is incremental refinement: the Spirit and Genesis lines have been iterated over decades, with parts availability and proven burner longevity as core selling points. Charbroil's Amplifire infrared approach is a different engineering decision , different heat delivery, different learning curve, different long-term performance profile.

Weber suits the cook who wants proven reliability and an extensive support ecosystem. The Charbroil infrared suits the cook who wants higher grate temperatures and fuel efficiency and is willing to adapt technique to get there. Both are legitimate choices; they're optimizing for different things. Browsing the full [gas grill category](/gas-grills/) helps clarify which design philosophy fits your cooking style before committing.

### How Long Should a Gas Grill Last?

A well-maintained gas grill should run eight to twelve years before major components need replacement. The factors that shorten that lifespan are moisture exposure, inadequate grease management, and difficulty sourcing replacement burners. Cover your grill when it's not in use. Clean the grease management system regularly. Verify that replacement burner tubes are available and affordable before purchasing.

Weber's replacement parts ecosystem is one of the strongest in the category , burner tubes, grates, and ignition systems for Spirit and Genesis models are widely available. This matters more in year five than it does at purchase, but it's worth accounting for at the point of decision.

### Spirit vs. Genesis: Understanding the Tier Difference

Within Weber's own lineup, the Spirit and Genesis lines represent different price bands and build specifications. Spirit grills are the entry-to-mid-range tier , solid construction, reliable performance, manageable price. Genesis grills are heavier-built, with PureBlu burners that distribute heat more evenly and materials that hold up better over a longer service life.

If your budget allows and you cook frequently, the Genesis tier pays back the difference over a long ownership period. If you're uncertain how much you'll use the grill or are buying a first gas grill, the Spirit line is the honest recommendation , reliable enough to learn on, durable enough to keep if you decide to cook more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Weber Spirit and Weber Genesis grills?

The Spirit line is Weber's entry-to-mid-range tier , reliable construction, standard burners, manageable footprint. The Genesis line builds heavier, uses PureBlu burners for more even heat distribution, and is designed to hold up over a longer ownership period. The [Weber Genesis E-325](product:weber-genesis-e-325-liquid) and [Weber Spirit E-325](product:weber-spirit-e-325-liquid) both run three burners with a sear zone, but the Genesis costs more and delivers better long-term performance for the frequent griller.
### Is infrared technology worth it on a gas grill?

Infrared burners heat the grate surface directly rather than heating air first, which produces higher surface temperatures more efficiently. The [Charbroil Pro Series with Amplifire Infrared](product:charbroil-pro-series-with-amplifire-infrared) uses this system across four burners, meaning it reaches searing temperatures faster than conventional burners at comparable fuel consumption. The learning curve is real , food cooks faster, and timing instincts built on conventional grills need recalibration , but the performance payoff is genuine for the cook who wants maximum sear.
### How many burners do I actually need for a family of four?

Two burners handle most family cooks comfortably. The [Weber Spirit E-210](product:weber-spirit-e-210-liquid) runs two burners with a two-zone setup that covers direct and indirect cooking , enough for four people without oversizing the grill. Three burners add a meaningful zone when you need to hold cooked food at temperature while finishing something else. Four burners are for consistent large-group cooking or when you want a multi-zone station setup.
### Can I convert these propane grills to natural gas?

Some Weber Spirit and Genesis models support natural gas conversion through separately sold conversion kits, but availability varies by model year and variant. Verify conversion kit compatibility for the specific model you're purchasing before buying. The Charbroil Pro Series does not have a widely documented conversion pathway. If you have a natural gas line on your patio and want to eliminate propane tank logistics permanently, confirm conversion availability before committing to any specific grill.
### What grate material should I choose , cast iron or stainless steel?

Porcelain-enameled cast iron, as used on the [Weber Genesis E-325](product:weber-genesis-e-325-liquid) and Spirit line, retains heat better and produces a stronger sear. Stainless steel, as used on the [Weber Genesis S-315](product:weber-genesis-s-315-liquid), is easier to clean and more forgiving of inconsistent maintenance. If sear performance is the priority, cast iron wins. If you want lower-maintenance ownership without the risk of chipping enamel or rusting neglected grates, stainless is the practical choice.
