Replacing a grate on a [kamado grill](/kamado-grills/) sounds simple until you're standing in front of a browser with sixteen nearly identical options and no clear way to tell them apart. The Large Big Green Egg uses an 18 3/16-inch cooking surface, and that spec alone filters out most of the noise , but material choice, coating, and brand still matter more than most listings make clear.

The right grate affects heat retention, food release, and how much maintenance you're willing to do every season. Cast iron, stainless steel, and porcelain-coated options each make a different set of trade-offs, and the one that suits a weekend rib cook isn't necessarily the one that suits daily grilling.

## What to Look For in a Big Green Egg Large Grill Grate

### Sizing and Fit

The Large Big Green Egg has a specific cooking diameter, and a grate that's off by even a quarter inch will sit wrong or fail to stay seated. Most quality replacements are spec'd at 18 3/16 inches , that's the measurement you want to confirm before anything else. Some listings round to 18 inches, which can be close enough depending on manufacturing tolerances, but the more precise figure is the safer search term.

Fit also depends on the rim design. The Large BGE uses a raised rim to seat the grate, so replacements need to match that profile. Aftermarket options that fit multiple kamado brands , including Kamado Joe Classic and Vision Grill models , often share the same rim spec, which is why cross-compatibility is common at this diameter.

### Material: Cast Iron vs. Stainless Steel vs. Porcelain

Cast iron holds heat exceptionally well and produces strong sear marks, but it requires regular seasoning to stay rust-free. Skip the maintenance cycle and you'll be dealing with surface rust within a season. Stainless steel is more forgiving , it won't rust with basic care, and it heats more evenly across the surface. Porcelain-coated grates occupy a middle ground: the coating reduces sticking and simplifies cleanup, but it's only as durable as the care it receives.

None of these is objectively superior. Cast iron rewards cooks who stay on top of maintenance. Stainless suits cooks who want lower upkeep without sacrificing performance. Porcelain is a reasonable compromise for cooks who prioritize food release above all else.

### Gauge and Construction Quality

Grate gauge , the thickness of the wire or rod , determines how long the grate stays flat and true under repeated high-heat cycling. A thin grate warps. A heavy-gauge grate doesn't. This is especially relevant for kamado cooking, where temperatures can run well past 600°F for searing. Look for descriptions like "heavy duty gauge" or explicit rod diameter specs when available.

Welded intersections matter too. A grate that's spot-welded at the grid crossings holds its shape longer than one that's just bent and crimped. You won't always find this information in a product listing, but reviews from other kamado owners are usually explicit about whether a grate stayed flat after two seasons or started to buckle after six months.

### Brand Reputation and Aftermarket Confidence

OEM grates from Big Green Egg carry a price premium and guaranteed fit. Aftermarket options from established suppliers can match or exceed OEM quality at a lower cost, but the burden of due diligence shifts to the buyer. Check that the aftermarket brand explicitly lists your exact BGE model , not just "large kamado grills" , and look for customer reviews from Large BGE owners specifically.

Exploring the full range of [ceramic and kamado grill accessories](/kamado-grills/) before committing to a replacement is worth the extra twenty minutes of research , the difference between a grate that lasts five years and one that rusts out in eighteen months usually comes down to a detail buried in the fine print.

## Buying Guide

### Understanding the Large BGE's Cooking Diameter

The Large Big Green Egg's 18 3/16-inch grate diameter is the spec that drives every replacement purchase. This isn't a rounded figure , it's the actual measurement that separates a grate that seats correctly from one that wobbles or won't drop into position. Always verify this dimension against the product listing before buying a replacement.

The Large is the most popular size in the BGE lineup, which means aftermarket supply is strong and competition keeps quality relatively high. Buyers of other BGE sizes , Medium, XL , will need different dimensions entirely, so confirm your model before searching.

### Material Choice Based on Your Cooking Style

Cast iron suits cooks who run high-heat cooks frequently and already maintain cast iron cookware. If seasoning a grate feels like a reasonable addition to your routine, cast iron's heat retention and sear performance are hard to beat at this cooking diameter.

Stainless steel suits cooks who want reliable performance without the maintenance overhead. It heats evenly, resists corrosion without intervention, and holds up well through heavy kamado temperature cycling. The tradeoff is slightly less dramatic searing compared to bare cast iron.

Porcelain-coated grates suit cooks who cook a wider variety of foods , fish, vegetables, delicate proteins , and want food release to be predictable. Handle the surface carefully and the coating performs well for several seasons.

### OEM vs. Aftermarket

Big Green Egg sells OEM replacement grates directly and through authorized dealers. The fit is guaranteed, and the quality is consistent with the original equipment. Aftermarket options from reputable suppliers can match that quality and often cost less, but they require more research to confirm compatibility.

When evaluating aftermarket options, check for explicit Large BGE compatibility (not just "18-inch kamado"), read reviews from confirmed BGE Large owners, and look for brands with clear return policies. The better aftermarket suppliers in the [kamado grill accessory space](/kamado-grills/) are transparent about their fit guarantees , a supplier that won't confirm compatibility directly is worth avoiding.

### Considering a Full Kamado vs. a Replacement Grate

A buyer replacing a grate on an existing BGE has a different calculus than a buyer who's still deciding on the kamado itself. If the BGE is several years old and the grate is the only worn component, a quality replacement extends the useful life of a solid investment.

If the BGE is older and other components are also showing wear, weigh the total accessory spend against the cost of a comparable kamado at current prices. The Kamado Joe Big Joe Series I is the most relevant alternative at the large-format tier , a bigger cooking surface, included accessories, and a comparable ceramic construction. That comparison is worth running before committing to a round of replacement parts on an aging unit.

### Maintenance Expectations by Grate Type

Regardless of material, all grates benefit from basic post-cook care. For cast iron, that means a brush while warm, a thin coat of oil before storage, and keeping it out of prolonged moisture. For stainless, a brush and occasional soap-and-water cleaning is sufficient. For porcelain-coated grates, avoid metal tools against the cooking surface and don't use abrasive cleaners.

Establishing a maintenance routine early extends grate life regardless of material choice. A cast iron grate that's seasoned consistently from day one will outlast a stainless grate that's left wet after every cook. The material matters less than the habit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the correct grate size for the Large Big Green Egg?

The Large Big Green Egg uses an 18 3/16-inch cooking grate. This is the precise measurement you need to confirm when buying any replacement, whether OEM or aftermarket. Some listings round down to 18 inches, which can fit depending on manufacturing tolerances, but the full 18 3/16-inch spec is the correct reference. Searching for that exact figure filters out incompatible grates more reliably than the rounded number.
### Should I choose cast iron or stainless steel for a Big Green Egg replacement grate?

Cast iron delivers better heat retention and more pronounced sear marks, but it requires regular seasoning and careful drying after each use to prevent rust. Stainless steel is more forgiving on maintenance , it resists corrosion without intervention and performs consistently across a range of cooking temperatures. If you cook primarily high-heat cooks and already maintain cast iron, cast iron is the stronger performer. For cooks who want reliable results without a maintenance routine, stainless is the practical answer.
### Is the Kamado Joe Big Joe a direct alternative to the Large Big Green Egg?

Not exactly , the two grills overlap in category but differ meaningfully in cooking capacity. The Kamado Joe Big Joe Series I runs a 24-inch cooking surface compared to the Large BGE's 18 inches, which gives it substantially more room for larger cooks. Both use ceramic construction and operate on the same kamado principles, but the Big Joe is better suited for cooks who regularly feed larger groups or want the expanded cooking area for multi-protein cooks.
### How often does a cast iron Big Green Egg grate need to be seasoned?

A cast iron grate should be lightly oiled before its first use and re-seasoned whenever the surface looks dry or shows early rust spots. For cooks who use the BGE two or three times per week, a light oil application every few cooks is sufficient to maintain the seasoning layer. Infrequent users should season before each cooking season at minimum. The [18 3/16" Cooking Grate CIF999A](product:18-3-16-cooking-grate) and [GasSaf cast iron option](product:gassaf-18-3-16-cast-iron) both reward this kind of consistent maintenance with extended service life.
### Can aftermarket grates perform as well as OEM Big Green Egg replacement grates?

Quality aftermarket grates can match OEM performance when the fit spec is correct and the material gauge is comparable. The key is confirming explicit Large BGE compatibility in the product listing , not just "fits large kamado grills" , and checking reviews from confirmed Large BGE owners. Reputable aftermarket suppliers back their fit claims directly. An aftermarket grate from an established brand with clear compatibility specs and strong user reviews is a reasonable alternative to OEM pricing.
