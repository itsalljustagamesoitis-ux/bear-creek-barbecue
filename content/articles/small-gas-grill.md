A small gas grill can turn a concrete patio, a campsite, or a tailgate lot into a functional cook station without the commitment of a full-size rig. For anyone navigating HOA restrictions, limited outdoor space, or a packed trunk, a compact propane grill does what a Weber kettle can't , fires up in minutes with no charcoal math involved. The [gas grills](/gas-grills/) category covers an enormous range, and the small end of that range is where most weekend cooks actually live.

The difference between a good small gas grill and a frustrating one comes down to three things: heat output relative to cooking surface, build quality that holds up to repeated transport, and controls simple enough to use without a manual. Get those right and the grill gets out of the way and lets you cook.

## What to Look For in a Small Gas Grill

### BTU Output and Cooking Surface Area

BTU ratings look impressive in spec sheets and mean almost nothing in isolation. What matters is BTU output relative to the cooking surface , roughly 80, 100 BTUs per square inch of grill space is a reasonable baseline for a portable gas unit. A single-burner grill with 8,000 BTUs and a compact grate can cook two steaks efficiently. That same rating on a two-burner unit with twice the surface area will leave you waiting longer than you should.

Cooking surface area on portable grills is almost always smaller than advertised primary area suggests. Warming racks inflate the number. Count only the main grate when comparing models, and think realistically about what you're cooking. Two burgers and a sausage link, or four chicken thighs and some corn , that's the realistic load for most small grills.

### Burner Count and Temperature Zones

A single burner means one temperature across the grate, which forces you to move food off heat entirely if it's cooking too fast. That's manageable for simple cooks , hot dogs, burgers, anything that benefits from uniform heat , but it limits your technique. Two burners open up the possibility of a two-zone setup, which is how experienced grillers handle most proteins: sear over direct heat, finish over indirect.

For anyone coming from a full-size grill, two burners is the minimum that will feel familiar. A single-burner portable is genuinely useful, but it asks you to adjust your process rather than transplanting the approach you already use at home.

### Portability and Build Quality Trade-Offs

Portability and durability pull in opposite directions. A heavier grill , thicker grates, denser housing , holds heat better and lasts longer. A lighter grill fits in a bag and doesn't ruin your back on a campsite. The honest middle ground is stainless steel construction with cast iron or porcelain-coated grates. Stainless housing handles weather and transport reasonably well. Cast iron grates hold and distribute heat better than thin stamped steel alternatives.

Look for a locking lid if you're actually going to move the grill while it travels. Loose lids rattle, expose the cooking surface to road grime, and eventually warp. Folding legs are the other feature worth prioritizing , they compress the footprint for storage without requiring you to disassemble anything.

### Fuel and Setup Simplicity

Small gas grills almost universally run on 1-pound disposable propane canisters or connect to a standard 20-pound tank via hose. Disposable canisters are convenient for camping and tailgating. For patio use, a 20-pound tank with a regulator hose is cheaper per cook and eliminates mid-session swaps.

Before buying, confirm the grill you're considering can connect to your preferred fuel format , or that adapters are available and reasonably priced. Some portable grills ship only with a regulator designed for 1-pound canisters, which gets expensive quickly if you're cooking at home every weekend. Exploring the full range of [gas grills](/gas-grills/) options is worth the time before committing to a format that doesn't match how you actually cook.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Grill Size to Your Actual Use

The most common mistake with small gas grills is buying for the occasional large cook instead of the typical small one. If you're feeding two people on a weeknight patio dinner most of the time and occasionally need to cook for six at a family gathering, buy for the weeknight. You can run multiple rounds on a small grill for the occasional larger event. You can't shrink a large grill to fit a studio balcony.

Think through the three or four meals you actually cook outdoors most often. If that list is burgers, chicken thighs, and corn, almost any grill on this list handles it. If it includes pancakes, eggs, and smash burgers, the griddle combo is your answer and nothing else on the list is.

### Portability Versus Permanence

There's a meaningful difference between a grill designed to move frequently and a grill that's merely not bolted down. True portability means a locking lid, folding or removable legs, a manageable carry weight, and ideally a handle or carry bag. Grills with all of those features are designed to travel. Grills with one or two of those features are compact stationary units that happen to move occasionally.

Be honest about which you need. If the grill is going to the campsite six weekends a year and sitting on the patio the other forty-six, portability features matter. If it's staying on the patio year-round and moving to storage in November, prioritize build quality and cooking performance over fold-flat convenience.

### Single Versus Two-Burner Operation

The technique difference between one and two burners is real. A single-burner grill runs one temperature. Anything cooking too fast has to come off the grate entirely , there's no cooler zone. For simple cooks with uniform proteins, that's fine. For anything requiring different temperature zones simultaneously , searing a steak while keeping vegetables warm, for instance , it's a genuine limitation.

Two burners solve that limitation but add complexity and usually add weight. If your grill use is mostly simple cooks for one or two people, don't pay for the second burner. If you've been frustrated by single-zone cooking before, the two-burner step-up is worth it. Browsing the full [gas grills](/gas-grills/) range before deciding will help you calibrate where the small portables sit relative to full-size two-zone options.

### BTU Claims and Real-World Performance

Twenty thousand BTUs sounds more impressive than eight thousand BTUs until you account for the cooking surface each is serving. A smaller burner serving a compact grate will often preheat faster and cook more consistently than a higher-output burner spread thin across a large surface. Ignore the raw BTU number and think about BTUs per square inch of cooking surface.

Preheating time matters more than maximum temperature for most cooks. A grill that reaches cooking temperature in eight minutes is meaningfully more convenient than one that takes fifteen, regardless of the peak BTU number on the spec sheet. If you can find user reports on preheat time for a model you're considering, weight those more heavily than the BTU marketing.

### Fuel Format and Running Costs

One-pound disposable propane canisters are convenient. They're also expensive per cook compared to a 20-pound refillable tank and get wasteful quickly if you're cooking at home frequently. Most portable grills can run on either format with the right regulator or adapter, but confirm before buying.

If your primary use is the backyard patio and you cook two or three times a week through the summer, the 20-pound tank option will pay for the adapter cost inside a month. If you're traveling and convenience outweighs cost, disposable canisters make the logistics simple. Build cost into your buying decision, not just the grill's initial price.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many BTUs do I need in a small gas grill?

For most small gas grills, eight thousand to twelve thousand BTUs is adequate for a compact cooking surface. The BTU number matters less than the ratio of output to grate area , you want sufficient heat to reach cooking temperature within ten minutes and maintain it under load. More BTUs on a small grate doesn't always mean better results; consistent, controllable heat matters more than peak output.
### Can I use a small gas grill as my only outdoor grill?

Yes, for one or two people cooking simple meals, a compact propane grill handles most of what a full-size unit does. The honest limitation is cooking capacity , you'll cook in batches for anything beyond two to three people, and you won't have the two-zone flexibility of a larger grill. If you live alone or cook as a couple and don't entertain large groups often, a well-chosen small grill covers the practical need.
### What's the difference between the Cuisinart Grillster and the Royal Gourmet GT1001?

Both are stainless steel tabletop grills with locking lids and folding legs, but the [Royal Gourmet GT1001](product:royal-gourmet-gt1001-stainless-steel) offers ten thousand BTUs versus the Cuisinart's eight thousand. The [Cuisinart Grillster](product:cuisinart-grillster-portable-gas-grill) adds a dishwasher-safe grate, which simplifies cleaning over time. The BTU difference is modest enough that cooking performance will feel similar; the grate cleaning advantage is real if you cook frequently and dislike scrubbing.
### Is a griddle combo grill worth it for a small patio setup?

If your cooking repertoire includes flat-top techniques , smash burgers, breakfast foods, stir-fry , a 2-in-1 unit like the [Green Party Griddle Grill Combo](product:green-party-2-burner-griddle) eliminates the need for a separate griddle pan and a dedicated grill. If you cook only traditional grill foods, the griddle plate is weight and bulk you don't need. The combo format earns its cost when you'll actively use both cooking surfaces, not as a hedge against occasional variation.
### How do I protect a small gas grill from weather if it stays outside year-round?

A fitted grill cover is the baseline , it keeps rain and UV off the housing and grates, slowing corrosion on stainless and preventing rust on cast iron components. Beyond a cover, store the propane canister disconnected and away from the grill when it's not in use. Cast iron grates benefit from a light oil coat before winter storage. Stainless steel housing is the most weather-tolerant option for a grill that lives outdoors permanently, which is why it appears consistently across the better-built options on this list.
