Protecting a pellet grill is one of those purchases that feels unnecessary until the season you skip it. A good [traeger grill cover](/tools-and-grates/) keeps moisture, UV exposure, and debris off your investment between cooks , and on a machine with electronics, a hopper, and a controller board, that protection matters more than it does on a basic charcoal kettle. The options range from Traeger's own branded covers to well-regarded third-party alternatives built from heavier material.

The key variables aren't complicated, but they do require attention: fit, material weight, and how the cover handles sustained outdoor exposure. A cover that lifts in the wind or traps moisture underneath earns nothing except a false sense of security.

## What to Look For in a Traeger Grill Cover

### Material Weight and Construction

The number that matters most on a grill cover spec sheet is the denier rating , the measure of fabric thread density. Most budget covers run 210D or 300D polyester, which is adequate for occasional rain but degrades faster under sustained UV exposure and doesn't hold up well to wind abrasion against fence posts or deck railings. Covers rated 600D are meaningfully heavier, with tighter weave construction that resists tearing and holds waterproof coatings longer over multiple seasons.

Look for seam stitching and hem reinforcement alongside the base material rating. A 600D cover with poorly finished seams will fail at those points before the fabric itself gives out. Double-stitched seams and bound hem edges are signs that the manufacturer accounted for stress points, not just the flat panels.

### Fit: Model-Specific vs. Universal

Traeger grills vary significantly in footprint. A cover sized for the Pro 22 won't properly protect a Pro 34 or a Timberline, and a universal cover that claims to fit everything tends to fit nothing particularly well. Loose covers catch wind, pool water in fabric folds, and abrade the controller panel they're supposed to protect.

The safest approach is to match the cover to your exact model number, not just the series name. Pro 575 and Pro 34 are different physical sizes. A cover listed for "Pro Series" without specifying dimensions should be cross-referenced against actual grill measurements before purchase. Manufacturer-specific covers exist precisely because fit tolerances matter here.

### Waterproofing vs. Water Resistance

These are not the same claim. Water-resistant covers repel light rain but eventually allow moisture through under sustained exposure or pooling. Waterproof covers , particularly those with a polyurethane or PVC coating on the interior , maintain a barrier through extended rainfall. For a pellet grill left outside through a Midwest winter, the distinction is meaningful: the controller board and hopper seal are the components most vulnerable to moisture intrusion.

Check whether the cover's waterproofing claim extends to the seams. A waterproof panel with uncoated seams still allows water ingress at the stitching lines. Taped or welded seams close that gap. Not all covers at the mid-range carry this feature, but it's worth identifying before purchase.

### Hardware, Straps, and Securing Mechanisms

A cover that blows off in a moderate wind event provides protection only when you don't need it. Drawcords, velcro tabs, and side-buckle straps each solve the same problem with different trade-offs. Drawcords allow consistent tension adjustment but can loosen over time. Buckle straps are more secure but require two hands to fasten and unfasten before each cook. Zipper access panels , present on some covers , let you reach the hopper or controller without removing the entire cover.

Hardware quality matters as much as hardware type. Plastic buckles become brittle in sustained cold, and thin drawcord material frays at the adjustment point. Reinforced cord ends and heavier-gauge buckles are worth the slight premium, particularly if the cover stays on the grill through a full year of temperature swings. Browsing the broader range of [grill tools and accessories](/tools-and-grates/) alongside your cover choice can help you think through the full protection and maintenance picture at once.

## Buying Guide

### Match the Cover to Your Exact Model

The most common grill cover mistake is buying for the series rather than the specific model. Traeger's Pro series spans the 22, 34, 575, and 780 , and each has different physical dimensions. A cover listed as "fits Pro Series" without model-specific callouts is likely a loose universal fit that trades coverage consistency for broad listing appeal. Always cross-reference your grill's model number, not just the series name, before purchasing.

If you're unsure of your model, the grill's model number appears on a label typically located on the lower body panel near the legs. It's worth checking before you order, since returning a cover that almost fits is an annoying second trip through the process.

### Prioritize Material Weight for Year-Round Use

If the cover stays on the grill through all four seasons, material weight matters more than any other specification. A 210D or 300D polyester cover is adequate for mild climates or occasional use but degrades faster under UV exposure and sustained moisture. Owners in the Midwest, Pacific Northwest, or anywhere with real winters should be looking at 600D construction as a baseline. The thicker weave retains waterproof coatings better and resists the cracking and brittleness that lighter materials develop after two to three winters.

Year-round outdoor exposure is hard on any cover material. Spending a little more on material quality upfront extends the replacement cycle meaningfully.

### Official vs. Aftermarket , Know the Trade-Off

Traeger's own branded covers , the BAC380 for the Pro 34 and the BAC503 for the Pro 575/22 , offer guaranteed fit without dimensional research. For buyers who want the path of least resistance, that's a genuine advantage. Official accessories also carry Traeger's warranty structure, which aftermarket products typically don't.

The counter-argument is material. Third-party covers at the 600D tier often use heavier construction than the official accessories at a comparable or lower price band. If maximum weather protection is the priority and you're willing to verify dimensions before ordering, aftermarket covers at this material tier are a legitimate choice. The decision comes down to whether you're paying for fit certainty or material specification.

### Hardware and Securing in Wind-Prone Environments

Anyone leaving a cover on a grill in an exposed location , a deck without a windbreak, a patio at grade in open suburban terrain , should scrutinize the securing hardware before other features. A cover that lifts off in a sustained wind event isn't protecting anything. Buckle straps provide more positive retention than drawcords alone, and zipper access panels let you open the hopper or check the controller without pulling the entire cover off.

Reviewing the full range of [outdoor grill tools and accessories](/tools-and-grates/) alongside your cover choice is worth doing once, especially if your setup is missing a cover-on-the-fly workflow.

### Storage and Handling Between Uses

Grill covers don't require much maintenance, but they do require some. A cover folded wet and stored in a garage will develop mildew in the liner coating. Shake covers out after rain events and allow them to dry before folding if storage is necessary. Most covers are fine staying on the grill long-term , that's the intended use , but if you remove it seasonally, dry storage matters.

The inside surface of a coated cover will eventually oxidize. That process is slower with higher-quality coatings, but no cover lasts indefinitely. Inspecting seams and coating condition at the start of each season takes two minutes and tells you whether replacement is due before the cover fails during a rain event.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I buy the official Traeger cover or an aftermarket option?
Both are legitimate choices, but they optimize for different things. Official Traeger covers like the BAC380 and BAC503 guarantee model-specific fit without dimensional research, which is a real convenience advantage. Aftermarket options , particularly those using 600D construction , often provide heavier material at a lower price tier. If fit certainty is your priority, go official.
### Does my Traeger grill need a cover, or is it built to handle the weather?
Traeger grills are designed for outdoor use but are not weather-sealed appliances. The controller board, induction fan, and hopper components are vulnerable to sustained moisture exposure. A cover reduces that exposure significantly, particularly for grills stored outdoors year-round. UV exposure also degrades the exterior paint and plastic components over time.
### What's the difference between the Pro 22, Pro 34, Pro 575, and Pro 780 in terms of cover sizing?

These are four physically distinct grills despite sharing the Pro series name. The Pro 22 and Pro 575 share a similar footprint, which is why covers like the [Comnova Grill Cover for Traeger Pro 22 & 575 Series](product:comnova-grill-cover-for-traeger) and the Traeger BAC503 serve both. The Pro 34 and Pro 780 are larger and require different cover dimensions. Always match the cover to your exact model number , "Pro Series" listings without model callouts often produce poor fit results.
### Is 600D material meaningfully better than standard grill cover fabric?
Yes, in practical terms. Denier is a measure of thread density, and 600D fabric is roughly two to three times denser than the 210D, 300D polyester found in budget covers. That density produces better abrasion resistance, longer retention of waterproof coatings, and significantly better UV fade resistance. For a cover that's on the grill for most of the year, the durability difference between 300D and 600D becomes visible within two seasons.
### Can I leave my Traeger cover on while the grill is still warm after a cook?

No , place the cover only on a fully cooled grill. Residual heat from the burn pot and cooking chamber can reach temperatures that degrade coated fabric, and a warm grill creates a humidity environment inside the cover that accelerates mildew formation in the liner. Most Traeger grills cool to ambient temperature within 30 to 45 minutes of shutdown. Covering a cold grill is the intended use case; covering a warm one shortens cover lifespan and creates moisture problems.
