Natural gas grills solve a specific problem: you want to cook outside regularly, and you're tired of running out of propane at the worst possible moment. If you have a gas line at your house , or you're planning to add one , a natural gas grill converts that supply into reliable, on-demand heat with no tank-swapping required. The tradeoff is permanence. These grills stay put, which means choosing carefully matters more than it does with a portable setup. The [Gas Grills](/gas-grills/) hub has broader context on fuel types if you're still deciding.

What separates a good natural gas grill from a frustrating one usually comes down to burner quality, grate material, and how well the grill manages heat across zones. Raw BTU numbers are less useful than they used to be , the question is whether the heat lands where you need it, holds steady, and recovers quickly after you open the lid.

## What to Look For in a Natural Gas Grill

### Burner Count and Heat Zone Control

The number of burners matters less than what you can do with them independently. A three-burner grill lets you run a hot direct-heat zone and a cooler indirect zone at the same time , that's the minimum configuration for cooking a mix of food in a single session. Four burners extend that range, giving you more gradient options and more surface before things get crowded.

Look at the burner layout, not just the count. Grills where burners run front-to-back give you different zone control than side-to-side configurations. For most backyard cooks doing a mix of burgers, chicken, and vegetables, three well-spaced burners are genuinely sufficient. Four becomes meaningful when you're regularly feeding more than six people or cooking a brisket flat indirectly while searing something else.

Ignition systems are worth checking too. Crossover ignition , where lighting one burner ignites its neighbors , is more reliable in windy conditions than individual push-button systems. It's a small detail that becomes noticeable after the fifth time you're crouching in the dark trying to light burner three independently.

### Grate Material and Heat Retention

Cast iron holds heat better than stainless steel. That's not opinion , it's physics. If you want defined sear marks and a good crust on proteins, porcelain-enameled cast iron grates are worth prioritizing. The porcelain coating prevents rust without the seasoning maintenance that bare cast iron requires, which makes it more practical for outdoor use where grates get left in weather.

Stainless steel grates are lighter and easier to clean, but they shed heat faster. For general grilling , vegetables, fish, anything you're moving around frequently , the difference is less pronounced. Where it shows up is on a cold grill that's recovering after you've added cold food. Cast iron grates absorb and redistribute that heat more evenly.

Grate spacing matters for smaller foods. Wide gaps are fine for steaks and thighs but frustrating for shrimp or asparagus. A reversible grate , flat side for delicate foods, ridged side for sear marks , handles both without requiring additional accessories.

### Construction Quality and Weather Resistance

Natural gas grills live outside permanently. That changes the durability calculus relative to a grill you'd store in a garage. Stainless steel cabinet construction holds up to weather better than painted steel over a multi-year horizon , but stainless grade varies. Brushed 304-grade stainless resists surface rust well. Lower-grade stainless develops rust spots at weld points and around hardware within a couple of seasons.

Lid and firebox construction affects heat retention more than most buyers expect. A heavy, well-sealed lid holds temperature during indirect cooking and reduces flare-up recovery time. Thin lids lose heat quickly and make low-and-slow work impractical. Press the lid before you buy if you're shopping in person , the flex and weight tell you a lot about the construction underneath.

Cart and frame stability matters at size. A four-burner grill is heavy and wide. Side tables, storage shelves, and locking casters add utility but also add to overall footprint. Measure your patio or deck space before buying and account for clearance from fencing, overhangs, and combustibles , the installation requirements for natural gas grills are specific and non-negotiable.

### Natural Gas Line and Installation Basics

A natural gas grill requires a dedicated gas line run to your outdoor cooking area. If you don't already have one, you'll need a licensed plumber or gas fitter to install it , this is not a DIY project in most jurisdictions. The grill connects via a quick-disconnect fitting, and the line needs to be rated for outdoor use and buried to code depth if it crosses a lawn.

Budget the installation cost separately from the grill cost. For buyers weighing the full range of [outdoor gas grills](/gas-grills/), the long-term fuel savings and convenience of natural gas usually justify the upfront line cost if you cook regularly , but it's a real expense that belongs in the planning conversation. Convertible grills that support both natural gas and propane give you flexibility during that transition period, which is worth knowing before you finalize your purchase.

## Buying Guide

### How Many Burners Do You Actually Need?

The honest answer for most suburban households is three. Three burners give you enough zone separation to cook a full meal , one zone hot for direct heat, one zone low for finishing, one off for warming , without requiring the footprint of a four-burner grill or the maintenance surface of a larger cooking area.

Four burners make sense if you regularly cook for more than six people or if you run extended indirect cooks alongside direct-heat work simultaneously. The extra burner and cooking surface genuinely earn their keep in those situations. For households of two to four people cooking typical weekend meals, a four-burner grill mostly means maintaining more grate than you're using.

### Cast Iron vs. Stainless Steel Grates

Grate material shapes the cooking experience more than most spec sheets suggest. Cast iron grates, particularly porcelain-enameled versions, hold heat through the temperature swings of adding cold food to a hot grill. That means more consistent sear marks and better crust development on proteins without as much active management.

Stainless grates are lighter, easier to clean, and less prone to chipping than porcelain coatings if you're rough with tools. They lose heat faster and require more deliberate preheating to reach sear temperatures, but for grilled vegetables, fish, and anything where you're moving food frequently, the performance difference narrows. Choose cast iron if searing is a priority. Choose stainless if maintenance simplicity matters more.

### Convertible vs. Natural Gas, Only Models

Convertible grills support both natural gas and propane, usually through a conversion kit that swaps the orifice and regulator. This is valuable during the installation window , you can use the grill on propane while waiting for the gas line, then convert once the line is live.

If your natural gas line is already installed and you have no interest in propane backup, a natural gas, only model is simpler. Fewer fittings, no conversion hardware to manage, and the fuel system is purpose-built rather than adapted. The Weber Spirit and Genesis lines fall into this category. The Monument grills offer convertibility as a built-in feature, which serves a different buyer profile. Knowing which situation you're in before you buy saves a return.

### Cabinet Style vs. Open Cart Frame

Cabinet-style grills enclose the space beneath the cooking chamber with doors and shelving. Open cart frames leave that space accessible but exposed. Cabinet construction adds storage utility and a more finished appearance, but it also adds weight and cost.

For buyers integrating a grill into a permanent outdoor kitchen setup, an open cart frame often gives way to custom cabinetry anyway , the enclosed storage under the grill becomes redundant. For buyers who want a self-contained outdoor cooking station without a built-in surround, the cabinet-style Monument grills make more practical sense. Review the full range of options in [gas grills](/gas-grills/) before making a final decision based on footprint , dimensions on spec sheets sometimes differ from how the grill actually fits in a real patio layout.

### Warranty and Long-Term Cost

Weber's warranties on the Spirit and Genesis lines cover burners, cooking grates, and ignition systems for multiple years , the specifics vary by component and model, but the coverage is among the strongest in the residential grill category. Monument grills carry shorter warranty terms and a more limited service network, which matters if something fails after the first season.

Natural gas fuel costs less per BTU than propane over time, and natural gas prices are generally more stable. The savings accumulate meaningfully if you grill three or more times per week across a full season. Factor the gas line installation cost into the year-one math, but don't let it dominate a decision that plays out over ten-plus years of cooking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do natural gas grills require a special gas line, or can they connect to a standard home gas line?

Natural gas grills connect to a standard residential gas supply line, but that line needs a dedicated outdoor outlet installed in the right location with proper shutoff hardware. Most homes have gas routed to a furnace or water heater , extending that to an outdoor cooking area requires a licensed plumber or gas fitter to run new pipe and install a weather-rated quick-connect fitting. This is not a project to DIY unless you are a licensed tradesperson.
### Is the Weber Genesis E-325 or E-425 Spirit a better choice for a family of four?

For a family of four cooking typical meals, the [Weber Genesis E-325 Natural Gas Grill](product:weber-genesis-e-325-natural) is the more proportionate choice. Three burners cover the cooking surface you realistically need, and the Genesis line offers better build quality than the Spirit at comparable burner counts. The [Weber Spirit E-425 Natural Gas Grill](product:weber-spirit-e-425-natural) earns its four-burner footprint only if you're regularly feeding six or more people or running extended multi-zone cooks.
### Can I use a convertible grill on propane while I wait for my gas line to be installed?

Yes , the [Monument Grills Larger Convertible 4-Burner Natural Gas Grill](product:monument-grills-larger-convertible-4) is designed for exactly this situation. You run it on propane initially, then swap the conversion kit components once your natural gas line is live. This eliminates the gap between "grill arrives" and "line is installed" without requiring you to buy two separate grills or delay your purchase until the installation is complete.
### What is the practical difference between porcelain-enameled cast iron grates and stainless steel grates?
Cast iron grates retain more heat through temperature fluctuations, which produces better searing performance , particularly after adding cold food to a hot grill. Porcelain enamel coating prevents rust without requiring manual seasoning. Stainless grates are lighter, release food cleanly, and tolerate more aggressive cleaning without damage, but they shed heat faster and require longer preheating to reach sear temperatures. For searing-focused cooking, cast iron wins.
### How large a patio footprint does a four-burner natural gas grill require?

Most four-burner natural gas grills occupy roughly 55, 65 inches of width including side tables, and 25, 30 inches of depth at the cooking surface. Add the required clearance from combustible structures , typically 18, 24 inches on sides and rear , and a realistic patio footprint runs close to 7 feet wide and 5 feet deep for the grill area alone. Measure your available space against the manufacturer's specifications before purchasing, and account for the gas line routing path as well.
