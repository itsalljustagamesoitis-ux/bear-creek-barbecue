Flat top griddles have changed how a lot of backyard cooks think about weekend cooking , and Blackstone built most of that momentum. If you're looking at [griddles](/griddles/) for the first time or upgrading from a basic model, the line between a good purchase and a frustrating one usually comes down to surface size, burner count, and whether the unit is built to stay outside or travel.

I've spent more time than I'd like to admit reading spec sheets, watching seasoning tutorials, and comparing BTU ratings on griddles I'm only allowed to store on a concrete patio. Here's what I've found.

## What to Look For in a Flat Top Grill

### Cooking Surface Size

Surface area is the first number most buyers look at, and it genuinely matters , but not in the way most people assume. A 28-inch griddle feeds a family of four without issue. A 36-inch surface is where you start cooking for a group without rotating food off to the side to rest while the next batch finishes.

The real question is how you cook. If you're doing smash burgers, eggs, and bacon on a Saturday morning for your household, 28 inches is enough. If you're cooking for a neighborhood gathering or doing multiple proteins simultaneously with a separate vegetable zone, you want the larger surface. Measure your patio footprint before you decide , these units are bigger in person than they look in product photos.

### Burner Count and Heat Zones

Two burners give you a hot side and a warm side. That's functional for most cooks. Four burners give you genuine independent zone control , you can hold pancakes warm on low heat while searing sausage on high at the other end of the griddle.

The practical difference matters most during longer cooks. With two burners, you're managing temperature by moving food rather than by adjusting zones independently. That's fine if you've cooked on a flat top before. If you're new to griddle cooking, more burners give you more margin for error. Four-burner units are larger and heavier, so factor in storage space.

### Hood and Weather Protection

A hood does two things: it traps heat for faster cooking of thicker items, and it protects the cooking surface when the griddle isn't in use. Not every griddle ships with one. Some models offer a hard cover separately; others include it standard.

If your griddle lives outside year-round , and most of them do , a hood or hard cover is worth having. The steel cooking surface will rust if moisture sits on it. A cover reduces how often you need to re-season after rain. Exploring the full range of [flat top griddle](/griddles/) options before committing to a size is worth doing, because hood inclusion varies significantly across the product line.

### Portability and Setup

There's a real difference between a griddle that's theoretically portable and one you'd actually take camping. Full-size 36-inch units with casters are portable in the sense that you can move them around your patio. They're not what you'd load into a truck bed for a tailgate.

Convertible designs , units with removable carts that drop down to tabletop configuration , solve a problem that full-size griddles don't address. If you cook at home most of the time but want the option to bring the griddle to a family event, that flexibility has real value. Caster wheels on a full-size unit are primarily for repositioning, not transport.

### Seasoning and Maintenance

Flat top griddles require ongoing seasoning to build and maintain a non-stick surface. This is not optional. It's the same principle as cast iron cookware , thin layers of oil, heated until they polymerize, build up a protective surface over time.

The first seasoning session takes 45 minutes to an hour. After that, you're doing lighter maintenance sessions after each cook. The more you cook, the better the surface gets. Grease management systems , particularly rear-channel designs , simplify cleanup significantly and should factor into your choice if you plan to cook fatty proteins regularly.

## Buying Guide

### Surface Size to Actual Use

The most common mistake buyers make is sizing up out of aspiration rather than actual cooking habits. A 36-inch griddle cooking for two people most weekdays will spend most of its surface idle, which means more area to maintain and season without proportional benefit.

Map your typical cook to a realistic surface need. Four burgers side by side need roughly 12 inches of surface width. A full breakfast spread , eggs, bacon, hash browns, pancakes , fills 28 to 36 inches depending on how you stage the cook. If your typical use is family-scale, start at 28 inches.

### Portability vs. Station Cooking

These are genuinely different product categories wearing the same label. A full-size 36-inch unit with casters is a station griddle , it lives on your patio and moves a few feet when needed. A two-burner 28-inch unit without shelves is a portable griddle , it goes in the truck.

The convertible designs, like the Royal Gourmet PD4001, occupy a middle position. You get more cooking surface than a pure camping griddle, with enough portability to move between venues. If you only need one unit and it has to serve both roles, that's where to look. Browsing the full range of [griddles](/griddles/) in this category makes the distinction clearer when you can compare form factors side by side.

### Burner Count and Cooking Control

Two burners give you two independently controlled zones. Four burners give you four. The practical difference is how much you can cook simultaneously without compromising one item to help another.

A two-burner setup works well for sequential cooking , finish the eggs, move to the sausage. A four-burner setup supports simultaneous cooking where timing matters , sear the steak on high while holding the vegetables warm on low at the other end. If your style of cooking involves multiple proteins with different finishing times, more burners reduce the juggling.

### Hood and Cover Decisions

A hood serves active cooking and passive protection. During a cook, it traps heat and helps thicker items , chicken thighs, pork chops , cook through without burning the surface. Between cooks, it keeps moisture and debris off the cooking surface.

Not every unit ships with a hood. The Blackstone Omnivore and the Original 36" include one standard. The 1517 camping model does not. If you're buying a unit without a hood, budget for a fitted cover , an ill-fitting tarp is a poor substitute and won't prevent moisture damage to the cooking surface over time.

### Seasoning Commitment

Every flat top griddle on this list requires regular seasoning. This is the one maintenance requirement you cannot opt out of. The cooking surface is carbon steel, and carbon steel rusts when exposed to moisture without a protective oil layer.

The good news is that seasoning becomes routine quickly. After your initial session, you're adding thin oil coats after each cook while the surface is still warm. The surface improves with use , the first five cooks on a new griddle are categorically different from cook fifty. Buy the griddle you intend to use regularly. A griddle that sits in the garage loses its seasoning and requires starting the process over.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What size flat top grill is best for a family of four?

A 28-inch griddle handles a family of four comfortably for most meals. You have enough surface to cook proteins and a side simultaneously without crowding the cooking zone. The [BLACKSTONE 1883 Original 28" Griddle](product:blackstone-1883-original-28-griddle) is a well-rounded choice at this size , it adds an integrated hood and side shelves, which the bare-bones 28-inch models skip. If you regularly cook for six or more, step up to 36 inches.
### How often does a flat top griddle need to be seasoned?

A light seasoning pass after every cook is the standard practice , thin oil coat applied while the surface is still warm, then wiped down. A full re-seasoning session (multiple thin layers built up over 45 to 60 minutes) is needed after the initial purchase and any time the surface shows rust or dullness from moisture exposure. The more frequently you cook, the better the surface builds up over time.
### Is the Blackstone Omnivore meaningfully different from the Original 36" Griddle?

Both are 36-inch Blackstone units with hoods, but they differ in ignition system, shelf design, and surface coating. The [BLACKSTONE Omnivore](product:blackstone-omnivore-outdoor-flat-top-gas) ships with push-button ignition and folding side shelves as standard. The [BLACKSTONE Original 36"](product:blackstone-original-36-griddle-portable-outdoor) features extendable shelves and caster wheels. Neither is objectively better , the right choice depends on whether caster mobility or the specific shelf configuration matters more to how you set up your cooking station.
### Can I use a flat top griddle for camping, or do I need a dedicated portable unit?

Full-size station griddles are not practical camping equipment , they're heavy, bulky, and sized for fixed patio use. The [Blackstone 1517](product:blackstone-flat-top-gas-grill) is the purpose-built answer here: a two-burner 28-inch unit without integrated shelves, built lighter for transport. The [Royal Gourmet PD4001](product:royal-gourmet-pd4001-4-burner) with its removable cart offers a middle-ground solution if you want more surface than a pure camping griddle provides.
### Does a flat top griddle replace a traditional grill?
For most backyard cooks, it's an addition rather than a replacement , but some buyers do switch entirely. A flat top griddle excels at breakfast foods, smash burgers, stir-fry, and anything that benefits from full surface contact. It doesn't produce the char marks or smoke flavor that direct-flame grill grates do. If those flavors matter to your cooking, keep both.
