Clearance deals on gas grills move fast, and the products that show up in these windows vary wildly in quality. Browsing [Gas Grills](/gas-grills/) by price alone will get you a grill that disappoints , the right clearance buy means finding a unit whose specs and build quality justify the reduced price, not one that's been marked down because it wasn't selling. One detail separates confident buyers from regretful ones: knowing what the numbers on the box actually mean before you click purchase.

This category rewards research. BTU ratings, cooking area, grate material, and burner count all interact in ways that aren't obvious until you've cooked on a grill that's wrong for your situation.

## What to Look For in a Gas Grill

### BTU Output and Heat Distribution

BTU is the number manufacturers lead with, and it's also the number most buyers misread. Total BTU divided by cooking area gives you BTU-per-square-inch , that ratio matters more than the raw figure. A grill with 30,000 BTU across 472 square inches performs differently than one with 30,000 BTU across 280 square inches. The first has room to breathe. The second runs hot and leaves little margin for error.

Distribution across burners is the other half of this equation. Three independent burners let you run a two-zone fire , high heat on one side, indirect on the other. That setup handles everything from searing steaks to finishing a whole chicken without overcooking the outside. A single-zone grill with identical BTU output is a fundamentally different cooking tool.

Don't chase the highest BTU number on the shelf. Chase the right ratio for the cooking you actually do.

### Cooking Surface Area and Grate Material

Cooking area is measured in square inches and usually combines the main grate with any secondary warming rack. The main grate is where your food actually cooks , secondary area matters less for most cooks. A 400-to-500 square inch primary cooking surface handles a family cookout without crowding. Below 350 square inches and you're cooking in shifts.

Grate material affects both sear quality and maintenance commitment. Cast iron holds heat exceptionally well and produces better grill marks, but it requires regular seasoning and dries out if left uncovered in wet weather. Porcelain-enameled cast iron offers similar heat retention with lower maintenance , the enamel coating resists rust and cleans more easily. Stainless steel grates are the lowest maintenance option but don't hold heat as consistently.

### Build Quality and Weather Resistance

A clearance grill is only a deal if it lasts more than two seasons. Stainless steel construction resists corrosion better than painted steel, though not all stainless is equivalent , look for 304-grade references where specified. High-temperature paint coatings on steel bodies can perform adequately, but they require periodic touch-up as the coating chips and oxidizes.

Cart construction and stability matter more than they get credit for. A portable grill on wheels sounds convenient until you realize the side-to-side wobble makes it feel unsafe at high heat. Check that wheel quality and frame rigidity are addressed in product descriptions before committing.

### Side Burners and Secondary Features

A side burner is a genuine addition for anyone who grills and wants to keep sauces warm, par-cook vegetables, or run a pot of water alongside the main event. It's not a gimmick if you cook that way. If you don't, it's extra surface to clean and one more igniter that can fail.

Folding side tables are worth evaluating by what they actually hold , a flimsy shelf that flexes under a plate is worse than no shelf. Storage below the cart is useful for tank access and keeping basic tools organized. The [full range of gas grill configurations](/gas-grills/) worth considering includes models built around different feature priorities, and understanding which extras match your workflow prevents paying for options you'll ignore.

## Buying Guide

### How Many Burners Do You Actually Need?

Three burners is the functional minimum for versatile outdoor cooking. With three burners, you run a two-zone fire , one side direct, one side indirect , and still have a middle zone available for transitional heat. That setup handles most weekend cooking: seared steaks moved to finish indirectly, spatchcocked chicken, fish that needs gentle heat. Two burners limits your flexibility significantly. Four or more burners add cooking capacity and zone granularity, but the jump matters most when you're feeding a large group regularly.

Six burners, as found on the Captiva Designs model, are for a specific buyer profile , large families, regular entertainers, anyone cooking multiple proteins at different temperatures simultaneously. For most suburban cooks, three burners is the right call.

### Matching Grill Size to Your Space

A grill that's too large for your outdoor space creates daily inconvenience. Measure before you buy , not just the grill body, but the full footprint including side tables deployed and clearance required from structures. HOA guidelines in many communities specify setback distances from fences, walls, and overhangs. A grill that fits the patio but violates setback rules is a problem on its first use.

Smaller patios favor models with folding side tables, which collapse when not in use and restore walkable space. Cabinet-style grills like the GasOne GP-1030S solve storage by integrating it into the base , no separate shelving required around the unit.

### Fuel Logistics Matter More Than Buyers Expect

Every grill in this group runs on propane. That means a tank , usually a 20-pound standard exchange , that needs refilling or swapping. A 20-pound tank at moderate use lasts roughly eight to ten hours of cooking. A six-burner grill at full output shortens that considerably. Planning for a backup tank is practical, not excessive, for anyone who grills frequently.

Natural gas conversion is worth investigating before purchase if your outdoor space has a gas line available. Some propane grills are convertible; most require a separate conversion kit. The convenience of a direct gas line , no tank management, no mid-session refills , justifies the conversion cost for regular grillers. Check compatibility before assuming any clearance grill converts cleanly.

### Understanding Clearance Timing and What It Signals

Clearance pricing on gas grills follows a predictable seasonal pattern , late summer and early fall, when retailers move current inventory to make room for incoming models. A clearance grill isn't necessarily flawed; it's often last year's configuration at a reduced price. The relevant question is whether parts availability and manufacturer support continue for the model you're buying.

Before purchasing a clearance unit, verify that replacement grates, igniter components, and burner tubes are available for that specific model. Browsing the broader [gas grill market](/gas-grills/) before committing to clearance inventory helps you understand whether a deal is genuinely competitive or simply a price reduction on a model the market passed over.

### Maintenance Investment by Grate Type

Grate maintenance is where grill ownership either becomes routine or becomes a reason to use the grill less. Cast iron grates , bare, not enameled , require the most commitment: dry thoroughly after cleaning, apply oil before storage, never leave them wet. Porcelain-enameled cast iron is more forgiving; the coating prevents direct moisture contact with the iron, making rust less likely if the grill is occasionally left uncovered. Stainless steel grates are lowest maintenance but don't retain heat as evenly.

Match your grate type to your actual maintenance habits. Buying cast iron grates because they're technically superior and then failing to season them produces a worse outcome than buying stainless and keeping it clean.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which grill is the best option for a small backyard?

For a small backyard, the [MASTER COOK 3 Burner BBQ Propane Gas Grill](product:master-cook-3-burner-bbq) or the model with folding side tables are the strongest candidates. Both offer three-burner versatility without requiring a large permanent footprint. Folding side tables specifically help on compact patios by collapsing flat when prep space isn't needed. The six-burner Captiva Designs model is too large for most small spaces regardless of price.
### Is 30,000 BTU enough for searing steaks?

Thirty thousand BTU is adequate for searing if your cooking surface area is appropriately sized , 30,000 BTU across 472 square inches is a reasonable ratio. Running all three burners on high and allowing sufficient preheat time , at least ten minutes , produces a grill surface hot enough for a proper sear. If high-heat searing is your primary use, the G31 PRO's 34,000 BTU output provides additional headroom with the same three-burner configuration.
### How do I maintain cast iron grates on a gas grill?
After each cook, brush the grates while they're still warm to remove food residue. Once cooled completely, dry them with a cloth and apply a light coat of neutral cooking oil. Store the grill covered when not in use. Avoid soaking cast iron grates in water or leaving them exposed in wet weather , that accelerates rust regardless of how well they've been seasoned.
### What's the practical difference between a six-burner and a three-burner grill?

Three-burner grills handle most household cooking efficiently , parties of six to eight, multi-zone setups, standard weekend grilling. Six-burner grills like the [Captiva Designs](product:captiva-designs-6-burner-propane) add simultaneous cooking capacity for larger groups and allow finer temperature zone control across a 665 square inch surface. The trade-off is footprint, propane consumption, and cost. Unless you're regularly feeding twelve or more people, a three-burner grill will cover your needs without the additional space and fuel requirements.
### Can these grills be converted from propane to natural gas?

Conversion compatibility varies by model and isn't guaranteed for any of the grills in this group. Some manufacturers offer conversion kits as an accessory; others engineer the regulator and orifices specifically for propane and don't support conversion. Before purchasing any clearance grill with the intention of converting it, confirm with the manufacturer that a compatible conversion kit exists for that specific model. Attempting conversion with mismatched components is a safety risk and voids most warranties.
