Getting a pizza oven off the ground , literally , changes how the whole setup works. A dedicated [outdoor pizza oven table](/pizza-ovens/) moves your Ooni, Gozney, or Cuisinart off a folding card table and into a cooking station with real storage, real stability, and a work surface that earns its place on the patio. I've spent enough weekends improvising with whatever flat surface was nearby to know that the table is not a minor detail.

The options have expanded considerably, and the differences between them matter more than they first appear. Wheel placement, shelf configuration, weight rating, and whether the thing folds flat for off-season storage all affect how the station actually performs on a cook day.

## What to Look For in an Outdoor Pizza Oven Table

### Surface Area and Working Height

The oven takes up real estate. What's left determines whether you have an actual prep station or just a holding shelf. You want enough surface to land a peel, rest a cutting board, and keep a bowl of toppings within reach , all at the same time. The 47-inch-wide options solve this problem outright. Narrower tables work, but you'll be rotating tasks rather than running them in parallel.

Height matters almost as much. Standard outdoor tables sit around 30 inches, which works if you're standing over a griddle. Pizza ovens mounted there tend to put the fire mouth at a slightly awkward angle for launching. Some stands run taller , up to 35 inches , which puts the oven deck closer to a comfortable working position.

### Weight Capacity and Stability

A loaded Ooni Karu 16 with the gas burner attached weighs close to 50 pounds. Add a cast iron pan, a bag of flour, a peel, and the mental math changes fast. The table needs a meaningful rated capacity with room to spare , a tight margin between the oven weight and the table's limit is a problem waiting to surface.

Stability compounds with capacity. A table that rocks on an uneven patio surface is a hazard when you're pulling 900-degree dough from a live flame. Fixed installations eliminate this concern; wheeled carts shift it to a question of how well the locking casters hold on your specific surface material.

### Material and Weather Exposure

Powder-coated steel works. Stainless steel works longer. The distinction matters most if the table lives outside year-round, which most of these will. Stainless resists rust without the coating eventually chipping at weld points or under UV exposure. If you're in a region with serious winter weather or high coastal humidity, the material choice is worth the extra cost.

Shelf material often gets overlooked. Open wire shelving drains water and debris; solid shelving holds more but traps moisture underneath everything you store on it. Neither is wrong , the right call depends on what you're storing and how often you're pulling covers.

### Portability vs. Permanence

Wheels are convenient until they're not. A cart that repositions easily during setup becomes a liability if it drifts while you're mid-launch. Locking casters solve the problem in theory; the quality of the lock mechanism determines whether they solve it in practice. If your patio surface is level and you rarely move the station, a fixed-leg table eliminates the concern entirely.

Foldable designs trade rigidity for off-season convenience. If you're storing the table in a garage from November through March, the ability to fold flat is a meaningful feature. If the table lives under a cover year-round, the fold mechanism just adds potential flex points for no return. Browse the full range of [pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) and accessories before committing to a portability strategy , the oven's own footprint and storage profile should inform the table choice.

## Buying Guide

### How Much Surface Area Do You Actually Need?

The honest answer depends on how you cook, not how large your patio is. If the pizza oven is the only appliance running, a 32-inch-wide surface handles the oven plus a single peel. Add a second cook, a cutting board for toppings, or a dough station, and you'll run out of surface fast. The 47-inch-wide options exist for this reason , they're not oversized, they're correctly sized for how pizza production actually works when you're feeding more than two people.

### Fixed Height vs. Adjustable

Most outdoor pizza oven tables ship at a fixed height , 30, 32, or 35.5 inches depending on the design. The right height is the one that puts the oven mouth at a comfortable launch position for whoever is doing the cooking. Taller cooks generally want the higher end of that range. The oven model also matters: a domed oven like the Gozney Arc sits differently on a 30-inch table than a flat-topped Ooni Karu. Check the oven's own dimensions before assuming any table in this range will position it correctly.

### Wheels Are Not All Equal

Every wheeled cart in this group advertises locking casters. The locking mechanism varies considerably in how well it holds on real patio surfaces. On smooth concrete, most will hold adequately. On brick pavers with grout lines, or composite decking with surface texture, cheaper caster locks slip. If your outdoor surface isn't dead flat, prioritize casters with a positive-lock lever rather than a simple swivel brake.

### Weather Exposure and Storage Strategy

Stainless steel costs more than powder-coated steel and earns that difference over time , specifically over two or three outdoor seasons in a region with meaningful rain or humidity. If the table lives outside uncovered year-round, stainless is the right call. If you're pulling it under a covered area or using a quality furniture cover from October through April, powder-coated holds up fine with less maintenance concern. The [pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) hub covers oven storage separately; the table's material choice should match whatever storage commitment you're making for the oven itself.

### Assembly and Long-Term Durability

Every table in this group requires some assembly. That's not a complaint , it's just true. The durability question isn't usually about the steel; it's about the hardware used at the connection points. Thin bolts at leg joints and frame corners are where tables fail after a season or two of being loaded, repositioned, and exposed to weather cycling. Before assembly, check whether the supplied hardware is stainless or zinc-coated , zinc corrodes faster, and a corroded bolt at a leg joint eventually strips under load.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What size outdoor pizza oven table do I need for an Ooni Karu 16?

The Ooni Karu 16 has a base footprint of roughly 25 by 20 inches, so the oven itself fits on any table in this group. The real constraint is working space. A 32-inch top deck leaves you minimal room for a peel and nothing else; a 47-inch surface gives you a genuine prep zone alongside the oven. The [M Meterxity](product:m-meterxity-stainless-steel-pizza) is designed specifically around Ooni dimensions and handles the Karu 16 with room to spare.
### Can I use any of these tables with a Gozney Dome?

The Gozney Dome is heavier and wider than most portable pizza ovens, so table selection matters more. Check the Dome's base dimensions against the table's top deck before buying , the [Leteuke](product:leteuke-pizza-oven-table-grill) at 47 inches wide is the best fit here, with enough surface to accommodate the Dome's footprint and still leave working room. Weight capacity shouldn't be an issue with any table rated above 200 pounds.
### Is a foldable pizza oven table stable enough to hold a loaded oven?

A quality foldable stand holds a standard portable pizza oven without issue under normal use. The stability question becomes relevant if the table is on an uneven surface, if the oven is at the heavy end of the range, or if lateral pressure gets applied , resting weight on a corner, for example. The [Feasto](product:feasto-outdoor-grill-table-foldable) handles this well for compact-to-mid-size ovens; if you're running a heavier model, a fixed-leg or welded cart design removes the concern entirely.
### Do I need a table with wheels, or is a fixed stand better for a patio setup?

Wheels make setup easier and let you reposition during a cook. Fixed stands are more stable and have fewer components to fail over time. If your patio is level and the oven lives in one spot, a fixed stand is the simpler choice. If you're rolling the station out from a covered area before each cook or repositioning around furniture, wheels earn their place , just verify the locking casters are rated for your surface type.
### How much weight capacity should an outdoor pizza oven table have?

Most portable pizza ovens weigh between 20 and 55 pounds. A table rated to 200 pounds handles the oven with plenty of margin for tools, a gas canister, and incidentals. The [M Meterxity's](product:m-meterxity-stainless-steel-pizza) 650-pound rating is overkill by that measure , but excess capacity means the table is structurally overbuilt for the load, which translates directly to long-term rigidity and reduced flex under repeated use.
