A charcoal grill that doubles as a smoker gives you two cooking methods in one backyard footprint , and that's the appeal for most people reading this. You're not necessarily looking for a dedicated offset smoker. You want a grill that can run hot for burgers on Saturday and low-and-slow for ribs on Sunday. Browse the full range of [Charcoal Grills](/charcoal-grills/) and you'll see how much variation exists in that category.

The difference between a grill that smokes adequately and one that smokes well comes down to airflow control, lid seal quality, and how the design handles indirect heat. Those three factors separate a frustrating setup from a genuinely useful one.

## What to Look For in a Charcoal Grill with Smoker

### Airflow Control

Temperature in a charcoal grill is managed almost entirely through oxygen. Open the vents and the fire burns hotter. Close them and the fire slows. A grill that gives you precise, repeatable control over both the top and bottom dampers is one you can actually dial in for smoking , where the target range is 225°F to 275°F and drifting above 300°F ruins hours of work.

Look for dampers that slide smoothly and hold their position without creeping. Dampers that rattle loose or stick closed are not a minor inconvenience , they're a core failure for smoking applications. Kettles from established manufacturers tend to handle this better than cheaper alternatives because the tolerances are tighter.

### Lid Seal and Heat Retention

Smoke and heat escape through gaps. A kettle grill with a tight-fitting lid traps both, which means lower charcoal consumption and more even cooking temperatures across a long session. You can test a lid's fit before buying by looking at the contact point between the lid rim and the bowl , even spacing around the full circumference is what you want.

Porcelain-enamel coatings on the bowl and lid interior matter here too. They resist rust, hold heat evenly, and don't react with the acidic compounds in smoke. A bare steel interior will eventually corrode and develop hot spots.

### Cooking Surface and Indirect Zone

For smoking, you're using indirect heat , charcoal banked to one side or arranged in a ring, with the food sitting away from the flame. That configuration requires enough cooking surface that your food isn't sitting directly above coals no matter how you arrange them. A 22-inch kettle gives you roughly 363 square inches of grate space, which is enough for a whole brisket flat, two racks of ribs, or a spatchcocked chicken.

If you're consistently cooking for eight or more people, that footprint gets tight. But for most households, it's the practical limit before you're buying a dedicated offset smoker rather than a dual-purpose grill.

### Thermometer Placement

A built-in lid thermometer is convenient but frequently inaccurate at the grate level, because the dome of the lid sits several inches above where your food actually is. A dome thermometer reading 250°F may correspond to 230°F or 270°F at grate level depending on where the meat sits and how the vents are set.

That said, a built-in thermometer is still more useful than nothing , it tells you when the grill is cold, climbing, stable, or running away. For precise smoking, pair any built-in gauge with a probe thermometer placed at grate level. Exploring the full [charcoal grill options](/charcoal-grills/) available in each size class will help you understand which thermometer setups are standard versus which require aftermarket additions.

### Cleaning and Maintenance

Long smoking sessions produce significant ash accumulation. A grill that makes ash removal easy is one you'll actually use for multiple sessions in a weekend. One-Touch cleaning systems , where a rotating arm sweeps ash through the bottom grate into a catch bowl , are meaningfully better than scooping ash manually between cooks.

Check whether the ash catcher is enclosed and protected from wind, which can scatter ash and reignite embers. A hinged cooking grate that allows you to add charcoal mid-session without moving food off the grill is a smaller but real quality-of-life feature on longer cooks.

## Buying Guide

### Understanding the Grill-Smoker Overlap

Not every charcoal grill smokes equally well. The kettle design works for smoking because it creates a contained environment where airflow is controlled top and bottom, and indirect heat is easy to establish. What you're doing with a two-zone setup is turning a grill into a rough approximation of a dedicated smoker , and it works, within limits.

Those limits are session length and temperature stability. Longer smokes , brisket at ten to fourteen hours , require charcoal refills and more active management than you'd need on a dedicated offset. For ribs, chicken, pork shoulder in the six-to-eight-hour range, a kettle handles the job without compromise.

### Choosing Between Models in the Same Size Class

All five products on this list share the 22-inch bowl diameter, which means cooking surface is not a differentiator. What you're actually choosing between is the feature set around that bowl: built-in thermometer, lid holder, finish color, and portability configuration.

Start with the One-Touch cleaning system as a baseline , it's present on all of them and is the feature most likely to determine whether you actually use the grill for repeated smoking sessions. Then decide whether the built-in thermometer matters to you. If you have a probe thermometer, it's a convenience item. If you don't, it's a useful backstop.

### Fixed Versus Portable Setup

The Jumbo Joe Premium's portability has a specific use case , situations where the grill needs to move. For a permanent or semi-permanent backyard setup, the standard kettle height and weight give you a more stable platform. Wind, which is a real temperature variable during long cooks, affects a lighter, shorter grill more than a heavier one anchored by its own mass.

If you're buying one grill that lives on your patio, the full-height kettle models are the more practical choice. The Jumbo Joe earns its place as a second grill for travel rather than a primary smoking setup. Browse the [charcoal grill category](/charcoal-grills/) to see how fixed and portable designs compare across the full price range.

### Fuel Management During Long Cooks

Smoking on a kettle requires more charcoal management than grilling does. A full chimney of lit charcoal , roughly 80 to 100 briquettes , will hold temperature for two to three hours at 250°F with dampers dialed correctly. For longer cooks, you're adding unlit charcoal alongside the lit, a technique called the Minion method, which extends your burn time significantly.

A hinged cooking grate that lets you add charcoal without removing food is a meaningful advantage here. Not all kettle variants include hinged grates , check the product specifications before buying if this matters to your workflow.

### Weather and Seasonal Considerations

Cold weather and wind both pull heat out of the grill and force you to run dampers more open than you would in calm, warm conditions. Porcelain-enameled bowls help with heat retention because enamel holds and radiates heat better than bare steel. Lid fit becomes more important in cold weather too , any gap that's tolerable at 80°F becomes a real heat loss at 40°F.

If you cook year-round, the tighter-toleranced models are worth the additional consideration. Seasonal cooks who put the grill away in November have more flexibility, but a well-sealed, porcelain-enameled kettle will outperform a cheaper alternative every time the temperature drops.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between the Weber Original Kettle and the Weber Original Kettle Premium?

The Premium adds a built-in lid thermometer and a few finish details over the standard Kettle. The underlying bowl, grate, and damper system are the same on both. If you already own a probe thermometer and use it consistently, the standard Kettle delivers the same smoking performance for less. If you want a built-in reference gauge without reaching for a separate tool, the [Weber Original Kettle Premium](product:weber-original-kettle-premium-charcoal-grill) is worth the step up.
### Can a 22-inch kettle grill actually handle low-and-slow smoking?

Yes, with realistic expectations. A 22-inch kettle running a two-zone setup will hold 225°F to 275°F reliably for two to three hours per charcoal load, which covers ribs, chicken, and pork shoulder without difficulty. Long brisket cooks , ten-plus hours , require charcoal management and more attention than a dedicated smoker. The kettle is a practical dual-purpose tool, not a substitute for a full offset rig.
### How does the Weber Master-Touch differ from the standard Premium kettle?

The [Weber Master-Touch](product:weber-master-touch-charcoal-grill-22) adds a tuck-away lid holder, which changes how you interact with the grill during active cooking , adding charcoal, checking temperature, wrapping meat. The cooking geometry and damper system are identical to the Premium. It's an ergonomics upgrade rather than a performance one. Buyers who do long smoking sessions where the lid comes off repeatedly will notice the difference more than those who mostly grill.
### Is the Weber Jumbo Joe suitable as a primary home smoker?

It can function as a smoker, but its lower profile and lighter build make it better suited as a portable secondary grill than a permanent smoking setup. Wind affects temperature consistency more on a shorter, lighter unit. If your primary use is backyard smoking at home, one of the full-height kettle models gives you a more stable, controllable platform. The [Jumbo Joe Premium](product:weber-jumbo-joe-premium-charcoal-grill) makes the most sense for buyers who need one grill that travels and also smokes acceptably.
### Does the color difference between the black and copper Premium models affect performance?
No. The [copper Premium](product:weber-original-kettle-premium-charcoal-grill-2) and the [black Premium](product:weber-original-kettle-premium-charcoal-grill) are identical in construction, material, and cooking geometry. The porcelain-enamel finish is the same on both , color is a surface-level variation, not a material one. Heat retention, damper function, and thermometer placement are unchanged.
