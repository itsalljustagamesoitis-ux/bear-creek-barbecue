Clearance shopping for a gas grill is one of those situations where research actually matters more, not less. The discount creates urgency, and urgency is how buyers end up with a six-burner monument they can't fit on their patio or a portable unit that maxes out at tailgates but not much else. I've spent enough time in the [Gas Grills](/gas-grills/) category to know the clearance rack has genuinely good options , you just need to know which one matches your setup before the price disappears.

These four propane grills span the range from tabletop portable to full six-burner backyard anchor. Each one solves a different problem.

## What to Look For in a Gas Grill on Clearance

### Cooking Area and Burner Count

Cooking area in square inches tells you how many items you can run at once. Burner count tells you how much control you have over temperature zones. Both numbers matter, and they work together. A single-zone grill with 472 square inches is less useful than a four-burner grill with the same surface, because you can't move a chicken thigh off direct heat while the corn stays over a lower flame.

The practical floor for a backyard grill serving a family is somewhere around 400 square inches with at least two independently controlled burners. Below that, you're making compromises on volume or flexibility. Above 600 square inches with three or more burners, you're cooking for groups , which is great if that's your situation, but overkill if weekend cooking means you and two kids and the occasional neighbor.

Clearance models tend to cluster at either end of this range. That's not a flaw , it's just how inventory moves. Know your number before you browse.

### BTU Output and Heat Distribution

BTU figures get misread more than almost any other grill spec. Total BTU output is only meaningful relative to the cooking surface it covers. A grill producing 40,000 BTU across 600 square inches delivers similar heat density to one producing 30,000 BTU across 450 square inches. The ratio is what matters for searing capability, not the headline number.

Heat distribution matters more than raw output for everyday cooking. Burners that run hot in the center and cool at the edges force you to constantly rotate food, which interrupts the Maillard reaction you're trying to build. Porcelain-enameled cast iron grates help here , they retain and distribute heat more evenly than thin steel grates, which means less babysitting per cook.

If searing steaks is your primary use case, prioritize heat density over total BTU. If you're cooking chicken and vegetables for a crowd, even distribution across a larger surface wins.

### Construction and Weather Durability

Stainless steel exteriors are standard across most mid-range and above propane grills now, but stainless quality varies. Gauge thickness, grade of steel, and how well the frame handles moisture at the joints all determine how a grill weathers two or three Ohio seasons before you start seeing rust at the edges. The exterior finish matters less than the burner tubes and the grate material.

Porcelain-enameled cast iron grates outlast plain steel grates significantly in humid climates. They're heavier and require slightly more care , don't use wire brushes that chip the enamel , but they hold seasoning better and resist rust at the cooking surface where it would actually affect your food.

Clearance grills sometimes include floor models with minor cosmetic wear. That's fine. Check the burner tubes, ignition system, and grate condition rather than whether the side shelf has a scratch.

### Portability Versus Fixed Footprint

This is the decision that eliminates the most wrong choices. A tabletop portable grill and a full standing grill serve genuinely different use cases , they are not interchangeable with a size preference. Portable grills go where you go: campsites, tailgate lots, a friend's apartment deck. Standing grills stay where you put them and become part of your outdoor cooking setup.

Exploring the full range of [propane and gas grill options](/gas-grills/) before locking in a size is worth the time, especially on clearance where you're working with what's available rather than a custom order. The worst clearance buy is a grill that's the right price and the wrong category.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Grill Size to Your Actual Space

The most common clearance grill mistake is buying footprint first and thinking about space second. Measure your patio before you look at any specifications. Account for clearance from the house, deck railing, or fence , most manufacturers recommend at least three feet of clearance on all sides. A six-burner grill on a patio that can accommodate it is a genuine upgrade. The same grill on a 10-by-10 concrete pad is a fire hazard and a frustration.

Side tables and shelves add to the operational footprint even when they fold. A grill that lists a 50-inch body width may add another 24 inches of shelf on each side when open. That's the working footprint , the space you actually need during a cook.

### Burner Count and Zone Cooking

Zone cooking is the technique that separates confident grillers from anxious ones. Two zones , one direct, one indirect , give you the ability to sear and then finish proteins without burning the exterior before the interior is done. You need at least two independently controlled burners to do this. Three burners give you more gradation. Four let you run multiple proteins at different temperatures simultaneously.

If your clearance budget is pointing you toward a two-burner standing grill, that's still workable zone cooking. If it's pointing you toward a single-burner unit, understand that you're giving up zone control entirely and adjusting your expectations accordingly. The burner count is not a prestige feature , it's a functional cooking variable.

### Propane Logistics

Every grill on this list runs on propane, which means tank management is part of the ownership picture. A standard 20-pound propane tank lasts approximately 18 to 20 hours of grilling at medium heat , longer on smaller grills, shorter on six-burner units running at high output. If you grill frequently, having two tanks and a rotation system is more practical than running out mid-cook.

Tank storage needs a dry, ventilated outdoor location away from direct sun and heat sources. Most suburban patios accommodate this without issue, but it's worth confirming before you purchase a high-output grill that burns through propane faster than your storage situation can handle. Reviewing the full range of [gas grill options](/gas-grills/) , including natural gas models that eliminate tank logistics , is worthwhile if propane management sounds like a friction point for your situation.

### Clearance Timing and What It Signals

Gas grills go on clearance for predictable reasons: end-of-season inventory reduction, model-year transitions, and floor models being replaced by new stock. None of these reasons mean the grill has a problem. End-of-season clearance in particular often surfaces current-year models at meaningful reductions simply because retailers are managing warehouse space.

Model-year transitions are slightly different. An outgoing model may lack a feature added to the new version, or it may be identical except for a cosmetic update. Checking whether the clearance model's ASIN is the same as the current catalog version tells you quickly which situation you're in. If they're the same product at a lower price, that's a straightforward buy. If the new version added a meaningful feature, factor that into the decision.

### Ignition Systems and First-Cook Reliability

Ignition reliability is the easiest grill feature to undervalue until yours fails mid-setup with guests arriving in 20 minutes. Twist-start and push-button ignition systems both work well when they're new. They vary in long-term reliability based on how well the igniter is protected from moisture and grease buildup.

A grill with a reliable secondary ignition method , or one where the burners are easily lit manually with a long lighter , is a practical consideration, especially for clearance floor models that may have been exposed to showroom conditions. Test ignition before you commit if you're purchasing a floor model in person.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many burners do I actually need for a backyard gas grill?

For a family of four cooking on weekends, three burners is a practical minimum , it gives you real zone control without the footprint of a larger unit. Four burners expands your flexibility for mixed cooks. Six burners is appropriate for regular large-group cooking. Most buyers overestimate how much grill they'll use consistently, so honesty about your actual cook frequency matters more than aspirational capacity.
### Is a clearance gas grill likely to have something wrong with it?
Not typically. Most clearance grills are discounted for inventory or seasonal reasons, not functional ones. End-of-season sales, model-year transitions, and floor model clearances are the most common sources. For floor models, inspect the ignition system and burner tubes carefully before purchasing.
### What's the difference between the HUGROVE 4-burner and the MASTER COOK 3-burner for everyday backyard cooking?

The [HUGROVE 4-Burner](product:hugrove-4-burner-propane-gas) gives you an additional cooking zone, which matters most if you regularly cook mixed proteins at different temperatures simultaneously. The [MASTER COOK 3-Burner](product:master-cook-3-burner-bbq) covers most family cooking scenarios with less footprint and a slightly simpler configuration. If you cook chicken and burgers together frequently, the four-burner setup earns its keep. For straightforward weekend grilling, three burners is sufficient.
### Can the Cuisinart tabletop grill replace a standing grill for home use?

For a small household , one or two people, occasional grilling , the [Cuisinart Chef's Style Tabletop](product:cuisinart-chefs-style-tabletop-portable) handles the job with fewer logistics. For a family or anyone cooking for more than four people regularly, a standing grill with a larger surface and more burners is the better long-term choice. The tabletop unit excels in portability scenarios: camping, tailgating, apartment balconies. It's a purpose-built tool, not a scaled-down substitute.
### Does BTU output actually matter when comparing gas grills?
BTU matters most as a ratio to cooking surface, not as an absolute number. A grill with 40,000 BTU across 500 square inches delivers more heat per square inch than one with 65,000 BTU across 900 square inches. For searing, heat density is the relevant variable. For low-and-slow indirect cooking, it matters less.
