Choosing a Charbroil gas grill is simpler than it looks , once you know which version of "simple" you actually need. The brand covers a wide range, from four-burner cabinet grills built for weekend crowds to single-burner portables sized for a tailgate or a campsite. A [gas grill](/gas-grills/) from Charbroil fits suburban patios, balconies, and truck beds alike, depending on which model you pick.

The challenge is that "Charbroil" describes a product family, not a single product. Cooking surface, burner count, heating technology, and portability all vary meaningfully across the lineup. Getting clear on those variables before browsing will save you from buying more grill than your patio can hold , or less grill than your cookouts require.

## What to Look For in a Charbroil Gas Grill

### Heating Technology: Infrared vs. Convective vs. Standard

Charbroil offers three distinct heating approaches across its lineup, and they produce genuinely different results. Standard burner grills direct flame upward through grates , familiar, easy to understand, and straightforward to maintain. Infrared grills use a radiant emitter plate to distribute heat more evenly and retain higher temperatures with less fuel. Convective designs circulate heat around the cooking surface, reducing cold spots without requiring the higher surface temperatures that infrared produces.

For most suburban weekend grilling , burgers, chicken, occasional steaks , the differences matter less than marketing suggests. Where infrared earns its keep is with searing: steaks benefit from the consistent, intense surface heat. If your cooking skews toward longer, lower-temperature work (indirect chicken, bone-in cuts), convective or standard setups are easier to modulate and less prone to scorching.

### Burner Count and Cooking Zones

A single burner gives you one temperature. That's fine for camping or tailgating, where simplicity is the point. It becomes a problem on a busy Saturday when you need to hold sausages warm while finishing a steak. Two-zone cooking , one side hot, one side cooler , requires at least two burners, and three burners open up real flexibility for simultaneous cooking at different temperatures.

Four-burner setups add capacity more than they add capability. The jump from two to three burners is meaningful. The jump from three to four matters mainly when you're cooking for more than six people at once. Honest question worth asking: how often do I actually need that fourth burner? For most households, the honest answer is twice a summer.

### Portability vs. Permanence

Portable grills solve a specific problem: cooking somewhere that isn't your patio. Tailgating, camping, beach trips, a friend's roof deck. The trade-off is cooking surface , portable models typically run smaller, and you'll manage propane tank sizing differently than you would with a full cabinet grill.

If your grill lives on a concrete patio year-round and moves only for cleaning, portability features add weight and mechanical complexity without benefit. A cabinet-style grill with a side burner gives you more workspace and storage than a portable frame ever will. Knowing which life your grill is going to live is the first decision, not the last. Browsing the full range of [gas grills](/gas-grills/) with that question already answered saves time.

### Build Quality and Weather Exposure

Porcelain-enameled grates hold up well under regular use and resist rust better than bare cast iron. Stainless steel components on the exterior handle weather exposure more reliably than painted steel, which chips and oxidizes over a few seasons. Cabinet-style lower compartments keep propane tanks dry and out of UV exposure, which extends hose life.

A grill cover matters here , probably more than most buyers account for when they're choosing a grill. Even a well-built unit deteriorates faster when left uncovered through Ohio winters. The grates, the igniter assembly, and the burner tubes all benefit from dry storage between uses.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Grill Size to Actual Cooking Habits

The most common mistake in grill buying is sizing up based on occasional peak use rather than typical use. A four-burner cabinet grill handles thirty burgers simultaneously. If you're cooking thirty burgers twice a summer and twenty burgers the rest of the time, that grill is sized correctly. If you're cooking four burgers on a Tuesday night, a three-burner setup serves you just as well with less mass to heat and maintain.

Think about your most common cookout, not your biggest one. Buy for the Tuesday cook. Handle the annual big party with better technique on a smaller surface.

### Portability: How Often Does the Grill Actually Move?

Portable grills solve a real problem for people who actually move their grill. Tailgating, camping, travel , the portability feature earns its keep in those situations. For a grill that lives in one place and moves twice a year for cleaning, portability features add mechanical joints, latching hardware, and lighter-gauge materials that don't improve the cooking experience at all.

Before buying a portable model, honestly count how many times per year you'd cook away from home. If the answer is two or fewer, a stationary grill will serve you better at the same investment. If the answer is more than six, portability is a real functional requirement worth optimizing for.

### BTU Output: What the Numbers Actually Mean

BTU ratings describe fuel consumption rate, not cooking performance in isolation. A grill with a smaller cooking surface and lower BTU output can produce the same sear temperature as a larger grill running higher BTU numbers , because the energy is concentrated over less area. The meaningful metric is BTU per square inch of cooking surface, not raw BTU output.

For direct comparison, 80, 100 BTU per square inch is a reasonable target for a grill that achieves good searing temperatures. Below that, you may struggle to hit and hold the surface temperature searing requires. This is worth calculating if you're comparing two similarly-priced models where BTU marketing is front and center.

### Infrared vs. Standard: Who Actually Needs the Difference

Infrared technology reduces hot spots and improves heat consistency across the cooking surface. For a cook who does a lot of searing and high-temperature direct grilling, that consistency matters. Steaks benefit. Burgers benefit. Long indirect cooking sessions benefit less, because you're running lower temperatures where the infrared advantage narrows.

Standard convective burner designs are easier to control at lower temperatures and cost less to manufacture, which generally means a lower price for equivalent cooking surface. Neither technology is categorically superior. The right choice depends on what you cook most , and on whether you've ever actually been frustrated by hot spots on a conventional grill. Browse [gas grills](/gas-grills/) across both technologies before committing if you're genuinely unsure.

### Grill Covers and Long-Term Care

A grill cover isn't an accessory , it's maintenance equipment. Gas grills left uncovered accumulate moisture in burner tubes, which corrodes igniter assemblies and restricts gas flow. UV exposure degrades plastic components, handle grips, and painted exterior panels. Covering the grill when it's not in use extends the functional life of every component.

The cover should fit snugly without being difficult to remove. A cover that billows in wind gets blown off. One that fits too tightly tears at the edges after a season. Manufacturer-matched covers, sized for specific grill dimensions, tend to fit better than universal covers across multiple seasons of use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Charbroil a reliable brand for gas grills?

Charbroil is one of the larger mass-market grill brands in the U.S., with a long track record in propane and gas grills at accessible price points. Build quality varies across the lineup , the Pro Series and Performance lines use better materials than the entry-level models. Replacement parts and customer support are generally available, which matters more over a three-to-five-year ownership period than it seems at purchase.
### What's the difference between infrared and convective heating in a Charbroil grill?

Infrared heating uses a radiant emitter to distribute intense, even heat across the cooking surface , it excels at searing and reducing cold spots. Convective heating circulates hot air around food, producing more even ambient temperatures with better heat control at lower settings. The [Charbroil Pro Series](product:charbroil-pro-series-with-amplifire-infrared) uses infrared; the [Charbroil Portable Convective 1-Burner](product:charbroil-portable-convective-1-burner) uses convective design. Which you need depends on whether you prioritize searing performance or temperature control.
### How do I decide between a portable Charbroil grill and a full-size model?

Count how often you actually grill away from home each year. If the honest answer is fewer than three times, a portable grill's trade-offs , smaller cooking surface, lighter construction, limited zone cooking , likely outweigh the portability benefit for your situation. If you tailgate regularly or camp with family, the [Charbroil Grill2Go X200](product:charbroil-grill2go-x200-portable-gas-grill) or the single-burner convective model both solve the portability problem well.
### Do I need a grill cover for a Charbroil gas grill?

Yes, and this is less optional than it's typically treated. Moisture intrusion through uncovered burner tubes accelerates corrosion in igniter assemblies and burner hardware, regardless of how well the grill itself is built. The [Char-Broil Performance Grill Cover](product:char-broil-4965580p04-performance-grill) is sized for 3-4 burner grills and fits most standard models in the lineup. Using it consistently is one of the higher-return maintenance habits for grill longevity.
### How many burners do I actually need for a gas grill?

For two-zone cooking , the technique that gives you the most control over most cuts of meat , you need at least two burners. Three burners add flexibility for mixed cookouts where different foods need different temperatures simultaneously. The jump from three to four burners is meaningful mainly for cooking volume, not capability. Most households cooking for six or fewer people find three burners sufficient across nearly every situation they actually encounter.
