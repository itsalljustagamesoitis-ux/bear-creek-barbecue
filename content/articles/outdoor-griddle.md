Flat top cooking changes how you think about outdoor meals. Instead of juggling grill grates and losing half your breakfast through the gaps, you get one continuous surface that handles eggs, smash burgers, stir-fry, and bacon all at once. If you've been browsing [Griddles](/griddles/) trying to figure out which outdoor griddle actually fits your setup, this is where I land after putting real time into the research.

The category splits into standalone propane griddles and cast iron inserts that sit on your existing grill. Both work. Which one is right depends on how much space you have, how many people you're feeding, and how much setup complexity you're willing to manage on a Saturday morning.

## What to Look For in an Outdoor Griddle

### Cooking Surface Size

The single number that matters most is square inches of cooking surface. A two-burner, 28-inch griddle gives you room for a family breakfast without crowding. A six-burner, 44-inch station is feeding a backyard party. The gap between those isn't just square footage , it's a fundamentally different piece of equipment in terms of footprint, fuel consumption, and what you'll actually use it for.

For most suburban setups, the 28-to-36-inch range covers the realistic use case: weeknight dinners plus weekend cooking for four to six people. Anything larger than that requires genuine outdoor storage space and a reason to run six burners regularly. Buying more griddle than you'll use consistently is how equipment ends up sitting under a tarp.

### Burner Count and Heat Zoning

More burners mean more independent temperature zones. A two-burner unit gives you one hot side and one cooler side. A four-burner configuration lets you run a high-heat sear zone, a medium hold zone, and still have room to park finished food off direct heat. Six burners extend that logic further.

The practical question is whether you actually cook that way. If you're making pancakes and bacon, two zones is plenty. If you're running a full taco operation , meat searing, onions sweating, tortillas warming , four or more burners earn their keep. Think about your most complicated cook, not your average one.

### Surface Material: Rolled Steel vs. Cast Iron

Standalone outdoor griddles typically use rolled steel flat tops. Cast iron inserts sit on an existing burner setup. Both surfaces cook beautifully once properly seasoned, but they behave differently.

Rolled steel heats faster and more evenly across a large surface. It's lighter per square inch, which matters on a 44-inch griddle you're moving around. Cast iron holds heat longer once it gets there and transitions between indoor and outdoor cooking more naturally. The maintenance rhythms are similar , both need seasoning, both hate dish soap , but cast iron is less forgiving of neglect. Exploring the full range of [flat top griddle options](/griddles/) before committing to a surface type is worth the time, especially if you're new to the format.

### Portability and Storage Footprint

A griddle with a removable cart is a different object than one welded to a frame. Convertible designs that function as both tabletop and freestanding units give you genuine flexibility , camping trip one weekend, backyard setup the next. Fixed-frame units with side tables are more stable during cooking but harder to store and impossible to transport without a truck bed.

If your outdoor space has constraints , HOA rules, a covered patio with clearance limits, a garage with finite square footage , measure before you buy. The footprint of a 44-inch griddle with side tables deployed is wider than most people expect when they're looking at product photos.

### Grease Management

Nobody talks about grease management until cleanup day, and then everybody talks about grease management. Rear-drain systems channel grease away from the cooking surface without interrupting the cook. Front-drain setups work but require positioning a grease trap where it can get knocked over. Some cast iron inserts rely on a perimeter groove, which fills fast during high-fat cooks.

Look for a dedicated grease channel and a removable cup or tray. The difference between a thoughtful drain system and an afterthought is thirty minutes of cleanup time per session.

## Buying Guide

### Choosing Between Standalone and Insert Formats

The first decision is whether you want a standalone propane griddle or a cast iron insert that works on your existing grill. Standalone units are complete cooking stations , they bring their own burners, frame, and often side tables. Inserts convert a grill you already own into a flat top surface.

Standalone units give you more cooking surface area and independent heat control. Inserts cost less, store more easily, and don't require a second propane tank. If you already own a grill you're happy with, an insert is a meaningful upgrade without a significant footprint addition.

### How Many Burners Do You Actually Need

Two burners handles family-scale cooking with two heat zones. Four burners unlocks genuine multi-zone cooking , enough to run a taco night or a breakfast spread without managing everything in sequence. Six burners is crowd-scale equipment.

The honest calculation is this: think about the three cooks you do most often, not the ambitious one you're planning. If two-thirds of your outdoor cooking is weekend breakfast for four, two burners is sufficient. If you're regularly feeding eight or more people and managing multiple proteins, four is the practical floor. You can browse the [griddle options](/griddles/) by burner count to narrow the field before comparing specific models.

### Propane Management and Fuel Planning

Every standalone griddle on this list runs on propane. That's the right fuel for outdoor flat top cooking , no electrical connection required, consistent heat output, and the ability to cook anywhere. The management side is real, though.

Know your tank level before a long cook. A 20-pound tank lasts significantly longer on a two-burner unit than on a six-burner station running at moderate-to-high heat. Keep a backup tank if you're cooking for a crowd. Propane storage requires a safe, ventilated outdoor space away from the house , factor that into your setup planning.

### Surface Seasoning and Maintenance Expectations

Rolled steel and cast iron surfaces both need regular seasoning to stay nonstick and prevent rust. The process is the same in principle: thin coat of oil, heat to smoking, repeat. Where they differ is in how quickly they degrade when neglected.

Cast iron surfaces that dry out or get exposed to moisture rust faster than rolled steel. Rolled steel griddle surfaces on freestanding units are exposed to weather between cooks , a cover is essential, not optional. Build the seasoning routine into your post-cook workflow and it takes five minutes. Skip it consistently and you're dealing with remediation instead of maintenance.

### Portability and Space Constraints

A griddle with a convertible tabletop design travels. A fixed-frame six-burner station does not. If portability matters for your use case , camping, tailgating, cooking at a family member's house , that constraint should drive your decision before anything else.

Storage is the other dimension. Large outdoor griddles with deployed side tables can exceed six feet in total width. Measure your storage space with the unit in its stored configuration, not its deployed one. Most buyers who feel cramped made their purchase decision based on cooking footprint rather than storage footprint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a griddle and a flat top grill?

The terms are used interchangeably in most product listings, but there's a functional distinction worth knowing. A flat top grill typically refers to a restaurant-style setup with a large steel cooking surface directly over burners , what these outdoor propane units replicate. A griddle can refer to that same surface or to a cast iron insert that sits on existing grill grates. For practical purposes, expect the same cooking style from both: continuous flat surface, no gaps, full contact between food and heat.
### Is the Blackstone 28-inch a good choice for a small family?

For a household of four cooking breakfast, burgers, or simple weeknight dinners outdoors, the Blackstone 28-inch is a strong match. The two-burner configuration gives you enough surface for a complete meal without wasted capacity, and the rear grease management makes cleanup realistic on a weeknight. If you're regularly feeding six or more people at once, the cooking surface starts to feel constraining , that's when four burners becomes the more appropriate choice.
### Do I need to season my outdoor griddle before the first use?

Yes, regardless of whether the surface is rolled steel or cast iron. Pre-seasoned cast iron inserts like the [Utheer griddle](product:utheer-25-x-16-pre) have a head start, but they still benefit from an additional seasoning round before the first real cook. Rolled steel surfaces on propane griddles are typically shipped with a protective coating that needs to be burned off and replaced with your own seasoning layers. Plan for 30 to 45 minutes of seasoning before you cook anything you want to eat.
### How do I decide between the Royal Gourmet 4-burner and 6-burner models?
The core question is how many people you're regularly cooking for and whether portability matters. The [Royal Gourmet PD4001](product:royal-gourmet-pd4001-4-burner) convertible design travels , it detaches from the cart and functions as a tabletop unit, making it viable for camping and tailgating. The [Royal Gourmet GB6000](product:royal-gourmet-gb6000-6-burner) is a fixed-frame station built for high-volume backyard cooking. If your primary use is a permanent backyard setup feeding large groups, the six-burner capacity earns its footprint.
### Can I use a cast iron griddle insert on a gas grill?

Yes , that's exactly what the cast iron insert format is designed for. Both the [Utheer griddle](product:utheer-25-x-16-pre) and the [Lodge Double Play](product:lodge-double-play-reversible-cast) are built to sit on existing grill grates over gas or charcoal burners. Confirm the dimensions fit your grill's cooking area before purchasing, since grill grate sizes vary. Universal designs cover most standard configurations, but measuring your grate and comparing it to the insert's footprint takes two minutes and avoids a return shipment.
