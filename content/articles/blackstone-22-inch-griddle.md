Finding the right flat-top for a suburban patio means balancing cooking surface, power source, and how much counter space you're actually willing to give up. The [Griddles](/griddles/) category has expanded fast, and the 22-inch size sits at an interesting intersection , large enough to feed a family, compact enough to move around without pulling a muscle.

Most buyers come to this size with a specific constraint: limited outdoor space, a deck that won't support a full stand-alone unit, or a need to bring the griddle inside on rainy Ohio Saturdays. The power source question , electric versus propane , matters more than the brand name on the front.

## What to Look For in a 22-Inch Griddle

### Cooking Surface and Zone Layout

The stated cooking surface size and the usable cooking surface size are not the same number. Manufacturers measure edge-to-edge, but burner placement and frame design can reduce the effective hot zone by several inches on each side. A two-zone layout , where the surface can hold different temperatures in different areas , is more useful than raw square inches for anyone cooking a full meal at once rather than a single protein.

For the 22-inch class, that means paying attention to how many independent burners or heating elements the unit runs. A single heating element gives you one temperature across the whole surface. Two zones let you finish pancakes on low while searing sausage on high, which changes how practical the griddle actually is for weeknight cooking.

### Heat Source: Electric vs. Propane

Electric griddles plug into a standard outlet and heat without the logistical overhead of propane tanks, but they typically reach lower peak temperatures and recover heat more slowly after you add cold food. Propane units heat faster, hold higher temperatures, and run independent of outlet placement , but you're managing fuel, connection hardware, and a flame source.

For covered decks and garage setups, electric is the cleaner solution. For open-air cooking where outlet access is limited, propane makes more practical sense. The choice also affects cleanup: electric units with removable drip trays are generally easier to manage indoors, while propane units benefit from outdoor drainage flexibility.

### Surface Coating and Long-Term Maintenance

Nonstick coatings vary significantly in durability. Ceramic titanium coatings tend to be more scratch-resistant than basic PTFE coatings but still require non-metal utensils to preserve the surface. Bare steel surfaces , common on propane stand-alone units , require seasoning before first use and regular re-seasoning to prevent rust, particularly in humid climates.

Midwest humidity is not your friend if you leave a bare steel griddle uncovered between cooks. Powder-coated frames protect the body; the cooking surface itself is a separate maintenance question. If you're not planning to season and cover a griddle after every cook, a coated surface is worth the trade-off in peak heat capacity.

### Portability and Deployment Flexibility

A tabletop griddle requires a stable surface at the right height to be comfortable to use. A stand-alone or cart-mounted unit sets its own height but takes up more footprint when stored. Some units offer both configurations through removable cart systems, which is a meaningful advantage if your use case spans deck cooking and camping or tailgating.

Weight is a real factor for anyone moving a griddle regularly. Electric units tend to be lighter than comparable propane units with full accessory hardware. If storage means carrying the unit through a door and down stairs, check the published weight against what you're actually willing to carry , and whether the full [griddles](/griddles/) product lineup in this size class offers a case or cover for transport protection.

## Buying Guide

### Electric vs. Propane: The Decision That Drives Everything

Power source isn't a feature preference , it's a constraint filter. If your cooking location has reliable outlet access within cord range and you want indoor/outdoor flexibility, electric removes friction. If you're cooking away from structures, need higher peak temperatures, or already manage propane for other equipment, gas is the practical choice. Most buyers have one right answer before they start comparing models.

Electric units in the 22-inch class are almost always tabletop-only. Propane units in this class span tabletop and stand-alone configurations. That secondary difference in form factor compounds with the fuel question when you're deciding between categories.

### Tabletop vs. Stand-Alone Configuration

A tabletop griddle requires a stable, heat-tolerant surface to sit on. That sounds obvious, but it eliminates some deployment contexts , plastic folding tables at lower weight ratings, uneven ground, surfaces you don't want to expose to heat. If you don't have a dedicated outdoor table that works as a griddle station, that's a purchase you're adding alongside the griddle.

Stand-alone and cart-mounted units set their own working height, which matters for anyone cooking extended sessions. Hunching over a tabletop unit for forty-five minutes of smash burgers produces real back fatigue. Units with convertible configurations , like the Royal Gourmet PD4001's removable cart , give you both options and are worth the price premium if your use cases are genuinely mixed. For the full range of [flat-top griddle](/griddles/) configurations available in this class, comparing footprint dimensions against your actual storage space before purchasing is worth the five minutes.

### Surface Coating vs. Bare Steel

Coated surfaces are easier to maintain and more forgiving of inconsistent upkeep. Bare steel surfaces hold heat better, tolerate higher temperatures, and develop a more effective non-stick seasoning over time , but that seasoning requires consistent maintenance to build and keep. A well-seasoned steel surface from someone who cooks weekly is better than a degraded nonstick coating from someone who doesn't. A neglected steel surface develops rust spots that require significant remediation.

Be honest about your maintenance habits before choosing. If you're cooking once a week in a climate with high humidity, bare steel requires real commitment. If you're cooking three or four times a week and the griddle lives outside, seasoning becomes a natural part of the routine rather than a burden.

### Accessory Readiness

The griddle itself is only part of the system. Enlarged spatulas, a quality scraper, and a basting cover are functional requirements for most cooking scenarios , not optional add-ons. Buying a complete accessories kit alongside the griddle means your first cook isn't hampered by undersized tools or missing equipment. If you're already running a full tool set from a previous griddle, verify the spatula sizes and scraper compatibility before assuming your existing tools will work on a new surface width.

A cover for the griddle surface is worth noting separately. Most bare steel units benefit from a dedicated cover to protect against moisture and debris between cooks. Coated tabletop units that store indoors have less urgent need, but a cover still extends surface life.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Blackstone 8001 E-Series and the 1600W ceramic titanium model?

Both are 22-inch electric tabletop griddles from Blackstone in the 8001 product family, but the 1600W model adds LCD temperature display, EZ-Touch dial controls, and a ceramic titanium coating that offers better scratch resistance than the base model's standard nonstick surface. The 1600W unit provides more precise temperature management, which matters for recipes requiring a specific and consistent heat level. For straightforward family cooking, the base model handles the task competently. The 1600W is worth the step-up if repeatable temperature precision is a priority.
### Is a 22-inch griddle large enough to cook for a family of four?

For most family meal scenarios , breakfast, smash burgers, stir-fry sides , a 22-inch surface is sufficient. Four burger patties or eight pancakes fit simultaneously on a properly sized 22-inch surface, which covers a single cooking round for a family of four without batching. If you're cooking proteins and sides at the same time rather than sequentially, the surface feels tighter and you'll manage timing more carefully. Buyers cooking for five or more people regularly should evaluate the [BLACKSTONE 1883 Original 28" Griddle](product:blackstone-1883-original-28-griddle) before settling on 22 inches.
### Do I need to season the cooking surface before first use?

It depends on the surface type. Electric griddles with nonstick ceramic or PTFE coatings do not require seasoning , a wipe-down and a light oil coat before first use is sufficient. Propane griddles with bare steel or cast iron surfaces require a proper seasoning process: heat the surface, apply thin oil coats in stages, and build up the protective layer before cooking food. The Royal Gourmet PD4001 falls into the propane/bare-surface category and needs that initial seasoning session before it performs at its best.
### Can the Royal Gourmet PD4001 be used as a tabletop unit without the cart?

Yes , the cart is designed to be removable, which is the core of the PD4001's convertible design. The griddle body separates from the standing cart and can sit on a table, tailgate, or other stable surface independently. That flexibility is one of the main reasons the PD4001 earns consideration alongside the electric tabletop units in this comparison. Verify that your intended tabletop surface can support the weight and handle the heat clearance requirements before committing to a specific deployment setup.
### What accessories does a first-time griddle owner actually need?

A large flat spatula, a scraper, and a basting cover cover the majority of first-cook scenarios. The spatula handles flipping and moving food; the scraper manages surface cleanup between batches; the basting cover traps steam for thicker cuts and vegetables. The [35PCS Griddle Accessories Kit](product:35pcs-griddle-accessories-kit-flat) bundles all three along with supplemental tools that become useful as you expand your cooking repertoire. Buying the full kit upfront costs less than sourcing the three core items individually, and it eliminates the mid-cook discovery that you're missing a tool you needed.
