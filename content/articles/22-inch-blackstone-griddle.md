The 22-inch Blackstone griddle sits in a specific sweet spot , big enough to feed a small crowd, compact enough to move when you need to. Whether you're cooking on a patio, hauling it to a campsite, or setting it up for a tailgate, the format works because it doesn't demand a permanent installation. I've spent enough time with flat-top grills to know that the size difference between a tabletop and a standalone unit matters more in practice than it looks on paper.

Before you buy, it helps to understand what the [Griddles](/griddles/) category actually covers , because "22-inch" describes a cooking surface, not a product type, and these griddles vary significantly in design, portability, and intended use.

## What to Look For in a 22-Inch Griddle

### Cooking Surface Material and Finish

Cold-rolled steel is the standard surface material on most flat-top griddles, and Blackstone uses it across most of their lineup. It seasons like cast iron , the more you cook on it, the better it gets. The trade-off is that cold-rolled steel rusts if you neglect it. A weekend without a cover after a humid Ohio evening will find that out for you the hard way.

Some griddles in this size range use stainless steel cooking surfaces or housing instead. Stainless is easier to maintain and resists corrosion better, but it doesn't build seasoning the same way. If you're buying for portability and convenience over long-term patina, stainless has a reasonable case.

The quality of the surface also determines how evenly heat transfers from burner to food. Thin or poorly finished surfaces create hot spots. When you're cooking pancakes for six people, consistency matters more than peak BTU output.

### BTU Output and Burner Configuration

A 22-inch griddle typically runs two burners. The BTU rating tells you how fast the surface comes to temperature and how well it recovers heat after a cold batch of food hits the surface. More BTUs matter more at scale , if you're cooking a full flat of bacon after a cold start, you'll feel the difference.

Equally important is how the burners are arranged. H-burner designs distribute heat more evenly across the surface than single-tube burners. Independent zone control , meaning each burner adjusts separately , lets you keep one side hot for searing while the other runs lower for eggs or vegetables. On a 22-inch surface, that flexibility is worth having.

Don't chase the highest BTU number for its own sake. Adequate output with good distribution beats raw power with uneven results.

### Portability and Deployment Format

This is where 22-inch griddles split into distinct product types. Some are tabletop-only , flat base, no legs, sits on a table or cart you already own. Others come with scissor legs or removable carts that let them stand independently. A few convert between formats.

Tabletop models are lighter and easier to pack. Freestanding models are more convenient at home because you're not hunting for a surface to set them on. If you're buying for one primary use , camping, backyard, tailgate , match the format to that use rather than buying the more versatile option and using only half of it.

Wheel integration matters more than most buyers expect. A griddle that weighs 40-plus pounds with a full propane tank benefits from wheels, especially if you're rolling it between storage and the patio rather than carrying it. Browsing the full range of [flat-top grills and griddles](/griddles/) makes the format differences easier to visualize before committing.

### Frame Stability and Build Quality

A wobbly griddle is a safety issue, not just an annoyance. Scissor-leg designs can flex under load if the locking mechanism isn't solid. Fixed-leg frames with cross-bracing are more stable but reduce portability. When reading reviews, instability complaints are worth weighing heavily , a tilted cooking surface changes how grease and liquid move across the flat top, and not in your favor.

## Buying Guide

### Matching the Format to Your Primary Use Case

The single most useful question to answer before buying a 22-inch griddle is where you'll use it most. Backyard use favors a freestanding format with side shelves and counter height , you want a stable work surface that doesn't require a separate table. Camping and tailgating favor tabletop models that pack tightly and set up fast. Frequent transport favors wheeled freestanding options that don't require lifting.

Buyers who try to optimize for all three often end up with a product that performs adequately for each and excels at none. If your honest answer is "mostly at home, occasionally camping," buy for home use and accept the portability trade-off.

### BTU Output and Surface Recovery

For a two-burner 22-inch griddle, adequate BTU output means the surface comes to temperature in a reasonable time and recovers heat quickly after cold food loads. Underpowered griddles struggle when you add a full tray of frozen hash browns or a batch of room-temperature burgers , the surface temperature drops and you lose the sear.

The difference between adequate and high output matters most for large batches. If you're typically cooking for two to four people, moderate BTU output is fine. If you're regularly cooking for larger groups, prioritize higher output and confirm the burner design supports even distribution.

### Propane vs. Convenience Trade-offs

Every griddle in this category runs on propane. That means maintaining a supply of fuel, managing tank connections, and knowing when you're about to run out mid-cook. For backyard use with a fixed setup, a full-size propane tank on a regulator solves the problem reliably. For portable use, 1-pound canisters or a small travel tank are more practical , but smaller tanks deplete faster.

The propane adapter included with some travel bundles is worth noting because it expands your fuel options without requiring a separate purchase. If the griddle you're considering doesn't include an adapter, factor that into your setup cost.

### Seasoning and Maintenance Commitment

A cold-rolled steel cooking surface needs to be seasoned before first use and re-oiled after every cook. That process is straightforward but non-negotiable. A griddle that sits uncovered after cooking will rust. One that gets stored wet will rust faster. The maintenance rhythm isn't difficult , wipe down the surface while it's still warm, apply a thin coat of oil, cover it , but it does require consistent execution.

If that routine is a genuine barrier, look at griddles with stainless steel cooking surfaces or consider whether a different outdoor cooking format suits your habits better. The [full griddle category](/griddles/) includes options across surface materials and maintenance profiles.

### Accessories and First-Use Readiness

Most griddles ship without accessories beyond the griddle itself. Cooking on a flat top requires different tools than a grated grill , primarily wide spatulas, a scraper for cleaning the surface during and after cooking, and a squirt bottle for water-based surface cleaning mid-cook. A basting dome is worth having for melting cheese and finishing proteins.

Buying a kit at the same time as the griddle removes the first-use frustration of realizing you don't have what you need. Assess what the griddle package includes , some travel bundles come with covers and adapters , and fill the remaining gaps before you cook the first meal on it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a 22-inch griddle large enough to cook for a family of four?

A 22-inch cooking surface is workable for four people, but it requires cooking in batches for larger breakfast spreads or full dinner plates. For two to three people, it's comfortable. For four or more, plan to stagger food rather than cooking everything simultaneously. The scissor-leg and tabletop models in this size perform similarly in terms of surface capacity , the format choice doesn't affect how much you can cook at once.
### What's the difference between the scissor-leg and tabletop formats?

The scissor-leg griddle folds down and uses wheels for transport , it's a freestanding unit that can move. The tabletop model is lighter and more compact, but it requires a stable external surface to sit on. If you're setting up in locations where a folding table is always available, the tabletop format is more packable. If you want to roll it from your garage to your patio without carrying it, the scissor-leg design is more convenient.
### Should I buy the 22-inch or step up to the 28-inch Blackstone?

If you're cooking at home with a fixed outdoor space and regularly feed more than four people, the [BLACKSTONE 1883 Original 28" Griddle](product:blackstone-1883-original-28-griddle) is worth the size increase. The additional surface area makes zone cooking significantly more practical. If portability is a genuine requirement , camping, tailgating, travel , stay with the 22-inch format. The 28-inch unit isn't designed to move frequently.
### Do I need a griddle cover, and is one included?

A cover is essential for any cold-rolled steel griddle , leaving the surface exposed to weather accelerates rust and degrades the seasoning layer. Some bundles include a soft cover; others don't. Check the specific product listing before assuming one is included. If a cover isn't part of the package, it's a necessary additional purchase and should be factored into your decision.
### What accessories do I need for a first cook on a flat-top griddle?

At minimum, you need a wide spatula (preferably two), a metal scraper for surface cleaning, and a squirt bottle for water. A basting dome is useful but not critical for day one.
