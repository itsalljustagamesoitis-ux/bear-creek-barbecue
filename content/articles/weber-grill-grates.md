Replacing grill grates is one of those maintenance decisions that sounds straightforward until you're standing in front of three nearly identical listings, trying to figure out which one actually fits your cooker. Weber grills are some of the most popular backyard setups in the country, but the grate ecosystem is fragmented by model, material, and generation. Getting this wrong means a return trip or a grate that technically fits but performs worse than what you pulled off. The right starting point for any grate decision is understanding what you're replacing and why , which is exactly what the [Tools & Grates](/tools-and-grates/) hub is built to help with.

The good news is that Weber's replacement grate lineup is more organized than it looks once you know what to filter for. I've done the sorting below , covering kettle grates, Genesis 300, Genesis II 400, and a few material differences worth understanding before you buy.

## What to Look For in Weber Grill Grates

### Grill Model Compatibility First, Everything Else Second

Nothing else on this list matters if the grate doesn't fit your grill. Weber's lineup spans decades and several distinct product families , the kettle series, the Genesis 300 series, the Genesis II 400 series, the Spirit series, and more. Grates are sized and shaped for specific models. An 18.5-inch kettle grate is not interchangeable with a 22-inch kettle grate, and neither fits a Genesis gas grill.

Before you look at material or price, confirm your grill's model number. It's usually stamped on a sticker under the lid or on the side of the firebox. Weber's own parts lookup tool uses this number, and so do most third-party sellers. Buying a grate that "fits Weber kettles" without checking the inch size is the most common mistake in this category.

If you own multiple cookers or are buying a replacement for an older unit, write down the model numbers for each before browsing. One correct search takes thirty seconds and saves a week of shipping.

### Material Trade-offs: Cast Iron vs. Stainless Steel vs. Porcelain-Enameled

The three main materials in this category each solve a different problem. Cast iron holds heat exceptionally well and produces deep sear marks, but it requires maintenance , oiling after every cook, prompt drying after cleaning , to prevent rust. Left wet or stored outdoors uncovered, bare cast iron degrades quickly.

Porcelain-enameled cast iron adds a glass-fused coating that makes the surface easier to clean and more resistant to moisture. The heat retention advantage of cast iron stays. The trade-off is that the coating chips if you use metal utensils aggressively or drop the grate on a hard surface. Once the enamel chips at the cooking surface, rust can work in underneath.

Stainless steel , particularly 304-grade , is the lowest-maintenance option. It won't rust, cleans easily, and handles aggressive brushing without concern. It doesn't retain heat quite as well as cast iron, but for most weekend cooks that distinction isn't meaningful. If you don't want to think about grate maintenance at all, stainless is the right call.

### Replacement Parts vs. Full Systems

Some grate listings are replacement parts only , a single cooking grate, sized to drop into an existing grill without any hardware. Others come as sets that include flavorizer bars, cooking grates, and sometimes additional components. Knowing which you need before you shop saves money and avoids over-purchasing.

If your cooking grate is warped or rusted but the rest of the grill is in good shape, a single replacement grate is all you need. If your flavorizer bars are corroded and no longer protecting the burners properly, a combination set makes more sense than buying components separately. The product titles in this category are often long and cluttered with model numbers , look past the marketing language and check the parts list in the product description. A full breakdown of what fits what is available through the [grill tools and accessories](/tools-and-grates/) section if you need a broader comparison.

### Hinged vs. Standard Grate Design

This is a feature worth paying attention to if you cook with charcoal. A hinged grate has sections that fold up, giving you direct access to the charcoal bed without removing the entire grate and whatever is cooking on it. This matters most during long cooks where you need to add fuel.

For gas grill cooks, the distinction is less important , you're not adding fuel through the cooking surface. For kettle and charcoal cookers doing anything over an hour, a hinged design is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade that most people undervalue until they've scorched their forearm trying to add charcoal around a full rack of ribs.

## Buying Guide

### Know Your Grill Generation Before You Buy

Weber has sold grills under the Genesis name since the 1980s, and the Genesis 300 and Genesis II 400 are different product generations with different grate dimensions and mounting configurations. Buying the wrong generation is the single most common fitment error in this category. If your grill is from roughly 2011, 2016, you likely have a Genesis 300-series unit. If it was purchased from 2017 onward, it may be Genesis II. Check the model label , don't rely on visual memory.

The same logic applies to kettle grills. Weber sells kettle models in 18.5-inch and 22-inch diameters, and the grates are not interchangeable. The 22-inch is the most common backyard size, but the 18.5-inch remains popular as a portable or secondary unit.

### OEM vs. Aftermarket: When It Matters and When It Doesn't

Weber OEM (original equipment manufacturer) parts are machined to the exact tolerances of the grill they were designed for. For cooking grates, this usually means a confident fit with no gaps, wobble, or adjustment. Aftermarket grates , made by third-party manufacturers , vary more widely in tolerance. Some are excellent; some are close enough to be annoying.

For high-use primary grills, OEM is worth the premium. For a secondary cooker or a grill that sees occasional use, a quality aftermarket replacement in 304 stainless steel at a lower cost is a reasonable trade. Read reviews specifically for fit confirmation, not just overall satisfaction , a grate that people describe as "a bit loose" or "needed minor bending" is a different product than one where fit is never mentioned as an issue.

### GBS Compatibility: Do You Actually Use It?

The GBS (Gourmet BBQ System) is Weber's modular insert platform , a center section that can be swapped for a cast iron griddle, a pizza stone insert, a poultry roaster, and other accessories. GBS-compatible grates have a removable center piece that accepts these inserts.

If you already own GBS accessories, confirm that your replacement grate is GBS-compatible before buying. If you've never used GBS and don't plan to, a standard grate without the insert opening is simpler and often more durable since there's no center joint that can develop play over time. Don't pay for compatibility you won't use, and don't give up a feature you rely on.

### Cast Iron Maintenance Is a Real Commitment

Cast iron grates , porcelain-enameled or bare , require more care than stainless steel. After high-heat cooks, the surface should be brushed clean while still warm, then lightly oiled before the grill cools. Storing a cast iron grate in a humid environment without a cover accelerates surface degradation at any chips or worn spots in the enamel.

This isn't a reason to avoid cast iron , the sear quality and heat retention are genuinely superior , but it's a reason to be honest with yourself about your actual maintenance habits. The full [tools and grates](/tools-and-grates/) breakdown covers care routines by material type if you want to go deeper before deciding. If your current grates are rusted because you don't dry them after cleaning, a stainless replacement is probably more realistic for how you actually cook.

### Flavorizer Bars and Grates: Should You Replace Both at Once?

On gas grills, flavorizer bars sit between the burners and the cooking grates. They catch dripping fat, vaporize it for flavor, and protect the burners from grease buildup. When grates wear out from heavy use, flavorizer bars often do too , they take the same thermal cycling and grease exposure. Inspecting both at the same time and replacing both when needed prevents you from doing this job twice over the next two seasons.

Combination listings that bundle grates and flavorizer bars , like the Genesis II 400 option above , are worth considering if both components are showing wear. If only the grates are damaged and the flavorizer bars are still structurally sound, a grate-only replacement is fine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between Weber Genesis 300 and Genesis II 400 grates?

The Genesis 300 and Genesis II 400 are separate grill generations with different grate dimensions and configurations. They are not interchangeable , a Genesis 300 grate will not seat correctly in a Genesis II 400 grill. Check your grill's model label before ordering. The [Grill Parts for Weber Genesis 300 Series](product:grill-parts-for-weber-genesis) and the [Genesis II 400 series replacement set](product:grill-replacement-parts-for-weber) are each designed for their respective platforms and should not be swapped between them.
### Do I need to season porcelain-enameled cast iron grates the same way I season bare cast iron?

Porcelain-enameled grates don't require the full seasoning routine that bare cast iron does, but a light coat of cooking oil before the first use is still a good practice. The enamel layer handles most of the non-stick and rust-prevention work. The bigger care priority with enameled grates like the [Weber Porcelain‑Enameled Cast‑Iron Cooking Grates](product:weber-porcelain-enameled-cast-iron) is protecting the coating from chips , avoid metal scrapers and hard impacts on the grate surface.
### Will an 18.5-inch Weber kettle grate fit a 22-inch Weber kettle?
No. The 18.5-inch and 22-inch Weber kettles are different physical sizes, and their grates are not interchangeable. The [Outspark Charcoal Grate Cooking Grid](product:outspark-charcoal-grate-cooking-grid) is designed specifically for 18.5-inch models. If you own a 22-inch kettle , which is the more common backyard size , you'll need a grate sized for that diameter.
### Is 304 stainless steel meaningfully better than 430 stainless for grill grates?

For long-term outdoor use, yes. 304 stainless contains more nickel, which gives it significantly better corrosion resistance than 430 stainless , particularly in humid climates or if your grill isn't covered year-round. The [Genesis 300 series stainless replacement](product:grill-parts-for-weber-genesis) uses 304 grade specifically, which is the right spec for a part that will see repeated heat cycling and exposure to grease, moisture, and cleaning products over several seasons.
### When should I replace flavorizer bars at the same time as cooking grates?

If your cooking grates are warped or corroded from several years of use, there's a reasonable chance your flavorizer bars are in similar condition , they experience the same thermal stress and grease exposure. Inspect both components at the same time. If the flavorizer bars show heavy rust, physical warping, or significant grease buildup that won't clean off, replace them alongside the grates rather than doing two separate maintenance jobs within the same season.
