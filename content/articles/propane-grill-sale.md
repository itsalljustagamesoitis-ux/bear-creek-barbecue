Finding a propane grill on sale sounds simple until you're standing in front of five models with different BTU ratings, burner counts, and grate materials and no clear way to separate genuine value from a markdown on something you'll regret by August. I've spent more time than I care to admit reading spec sheets on [gas grills](/gas-grills/) and I'll say this plainly: the right grill for your patio isn't the one with the biggest number on the tag.

What separates a good buy from a bad one at any price point is whether the design matches how you actually cook , not how you imagine you'll cook.

## What to Look For in a Propane Gas Grill

### BTU Output and What It Actually Means

BTU ratings get thrown around like the highest number wins. They don't work that way. A burner's BTU output only tells you how much gas it can burn per hour , it says nothing about how efficiently that heat reaches your food. A well-designed firebox with tight lid seals will outperform a higher-BTU grill with a leaky lid every time.

What matters more than peak BTU is BTU-per-square-inch. Divide the total output by the primary cooking area in square inches. Anything in the 80, 100 range is solid. Below 70, you may struggle to sear properly. Above 110, you're likely burning more fuel than you're converting to usable heat.

Pay attention to burner construction as well. Stainless steel burners outlast porcelain-coated ones. If the spec sheet doesn't mention burner material, assume they're not worth bragging about.

### Cooking Area , Primary vs. Total

Manufacturers love to advertise total cooking area, which includes warming racks and secondary surfaces. The number that matters is primary cooking area , the main grate over the burners where actual grilling happens.

For two to four people, 400, 450 square inches of primary cooking area handles most weekend meals without crowding. Feeding larger groups or running low-and-slow indirect cooks alongside direct sears requires more room. Crowding a grill kills temperature management and leads to steamed food instead of grilled.

A side burner is a separate consideration entirely. It doesn't add to your grilling area, but it's genuinely useful for finishing sauces or keeping sides warm without running inside. Worth having if you actually use one , easy to ignore if you don't.

### Grate Material and Maintenance Trade-offs

Cast iron grates retain heat better than stainless steel, which translates to harder sear marks and more even cooking surface temperatures. The trade-off is maintenance: cast iron requires seasoning and careful drying to prevent rust. Skip that step twice and you'll be chasing rust spots all season.

Stainless steel grates are lower maintenance and still perform well for most cooks. Porcelain-coated grates sit in between , good heat retention, easy to clean, but prone to chipping if you're rough with metal tools. A chipped porcelain grate rusts fast.

If you're buying a budget grill and don't want to think much about maintenance, stainless grates are forgiving. If you want maximum sear performance and will commit to the upkeep, cast iron earns that extra effort. Before you settle on a grate type, it's worth browsing the full range of [gas grills](/gas-grills/) to see how grate choices line up across different price bands.

### Construction and Durability

Stainless steel construction is a legitimate selling point, but not all stainless steel is equal. Thin-gauge stainless dents, warps under high heat, and develops rust at welded joints within a season or two. Thicker panels and reinforced lid edges signal a grill built to last through Ohio winters stored outside.

Check the lid fit. A poorly fitting lid bleeds heat constantly and makes temperature management nearly impossible. If you can, open and close the display model a few times. A solid lid with minimal gap is more valuable than any feature on the spec sheet.

## Buying Guide

### How Many Burners Do You Actually Need?

The honest answer for most suburban backyard cooks is three. Three burners give you meaningful zone cooking , one zone hot for direct searing, one zone off or low for indirect finishing. That handles steaks, chicken, whole fish, and most of what lands on a weekend grill.

Five burners are worth it if you're regularly cooking for eight or more people, running multiple proteins at different temperatures simultaneously, or want the flexibility to dedicate a full zone to low-and-slow indirect cooking while still having room for direct-heat work alongside it. For anything smaller, five burners are infrastructure you're not using.

### BTU vs. Cooking Surface , Prioritize the Ratio

A grill with high BTU on a small cooking surface will overheat and create unmanageable hot spots. A grill with low BTU on a large cooking surface won't reach proper searing temperatures. The ratio between output and primary cooking area matters more than either number alone.

Before you finalize a purchase, do the math: divide total BTU by primary cooking square inches. Anything in the 80, 100 range is well-matched. If you're seeing a big cooking surface paired with a low BTU number, that grill will struggle to sear and take longer to preheat. That's a design compromise worth knowing about before it shows up at your first cook.

Browsing the range of available [propane and gas grills](/gas-grills/) by spec , not just by headline BTU number , will surface those mismatches faster than reading descriptions alone.

### Portability Versus Permanence

Some of these grills move. Some are designed to stay where you put them. That difference affects more than just logistics. A portable grill on a wheeled cart is easier to store for the off-season, easier to reposition when the sun changes, and flexible enough to leave the property for a tailgate or campsite.

A permanent station-style grill , like the five-burner Royal Gourmet , is more stable, usually has more storage underneath, and signals a different commitment to the setup. If your patio has a defined grill zone and the grill stays there year-round, portability adds nothing. If you're in an HOA with restrictions on permanent installations, a grill that moves matters.

### Grate Material and Long-Term Cost

Cast iron grates perform better but cost more time. Stainless grates are lower maintenance but don't hold heat as evenly. This isn't a trivial decision , it shapes every cook you do on the grill and the amount of work you put in at the beginning and end of each season.

Budget for grate replacements when buying any grill in the mid-range. Grates wear out. Cast iron grates on a well-maintained grill last longer, but if yours rust because the seasoning lapses, replacement cost is real. Stainless grates are cheaper to replace and require almost no off-season care.

### Construction Quality Signals to Check Before You Buy

Lid fit is the one thing you can evaluate from photos and reviews that the spec sheet won't tell you. A lid that doesn't seal tightly bleeds heat and makes temperature management a frustrating exercise. Read reviews specifically for heat retention complaints , that's usually a lid-fit problem masquerading as a BTU problem.

Check whether the side shelves are welded or bolted. Bolted shelves loosen over time, especially if the grill gets moved. The material gauge on the firebox matters too , thin stainless warps under sustained high heat and develops rust at seams faster than thicker panels. These details separate grills that hold up through five seasons from ones that start deteriorating in season two.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I choose between a three-burner and five-burner propane grill?

The deciding factor is how many people you're regularly feeding and whether you run multiple cooking zones simultaneously. Three burners handle most households comfortably , you get a hot zone and an indirect zone, which covers the majority of what a backyard cook needs. Five burners make sense when you're cooking for larger groups or want to run four distinct temperature zones at once. Most buyers landing on a three-burner like the MASTER COOK 3 Burner are making the right call for their actual cooking habits.
### Do I need a side burner on my propane grill?

Only if you'll genuinely use it. A side burner is useful for finishing sauces, heating beans, or keeping side dishes warm while proteins rest , tasks that otherwise require running inside. If your outdoor cooking is limited to proteins and vegetables on the grill grates, the side burner sits unused most of the time. The [34,000 BTU 3-Burner G31 PRO](product:34-000-btu-3-burner) and the Royal Gourmet both include one, which adds value for cooks who do full outdoor meals from start to finish.
### Is the Weber Spirit E-325 worth the premium over budget propane grills?

For most buyers who plan to use the grill regularly for several years, yes. Weber's parts availability, lid seal quality, and overall build mean the grill performs well in year four the way it did in year one. Budget grills can deliver strong results early but often degrade faster , warping grates, loosening hardware, and heat retention problems that compound over time. The boost burner sear zone on the Spirit E-325 also closes the sear-quality gap between gas and charcoal in a meaningful way.
### What's the difference between cast iron and stainless steel grates on a propane grill?

Cast iron retains heat better, produces harder sear marks, and creates a more even cooking surface temperature. The trade-off is maintenance: cast iron requires seasoning and thorough drying after each cook to prevent rust. Stainless steel grates are significantly easier to maintain , clean them and you're done , and still perform well for most cooks. The [Propane Gas Grill with Folding Side Tables](product:propane-gas-grill-with-folding) includes both cast iron grates and a stainless griddle, which lets you experience both surfaces before committing to a preference.
### How much propane does a typical three-burner grill use per cook?

A standard 20-pound propane tank lasts roughly 18, 20 hours of cooking at medium output on a three-burner grill. In practical terms, that's approximately eight to ten typical grilling sessions before you need a refill or swap. Higher-BTU five-burner grills running all burners on high burn through a tank faster , closer to 10, 12 hours. Keeping a spare tank on hand eliminates the frustration of running out mid-cook, which is genuinely more common than it should be.
