Cast iron grill grates are one of those upgrades that sounds simple until you realize how many compatibility variables are actually involved. Getting the material right matters , but so does getting the fit right for your specific grill. I've spent enough time digging through replacement part listings to know that the model number on your grill is the starting point, not the finish line.

Most grills in the mid-range category ship with porcelain-coated steel grates. They work, but they don't hold heat the way cast iron does, and they don't produce the same sear. If you're exploring your options across the [Tools & Grates](/tools-and-grates/) category, replacement cast iron grates are often the highest-return swap you can make without touching your burners.

## What to Look For in Cast Iron Grill Grates

### Model Compatibility

This is the one criterion that makes every other evaluation secondary. Cast iron grates are almost universally sold as model-specific replacement parts. A grate that fits a 4-burner Nexgrill will not drop into a Chargriller without modification, and even within a single brand's lineup, grate dimensions can shift between model years. Before anything else, locate your grill's model number , it's typically on a plate inside the lid or on the cart frame , and verify it appears on the product's compatibility list.

Replacement grate listings often cover a range of model numbers because manufacturers use common dimensions across product lines. That's useful if your model is listed; it's irrelevant if it isn't. The compatibility list is the document that matters, and cross-checking it against your model number takes about two minutes. Do that before you evaluate anything else.

### Cast Iron vs. Porcelain-Coated Steel

Cast iron holds heat at a level porcelain-coated steel can't match. That differential matters most when you're searing protein , the grate surface temperature drops when cold meat hits it, and cast iron recovers faster. The tradeoff is maintenance. Cast iron rusts if you leave it wet, and it requires periodic re-seasoning to maintain a non-stick surface and prevent corrosion.

Porcelain-coated steel is lower maintenance but more fragile at the surface level , chip the porcelain and you've created a rust initiation point anyway. For anyone willing to do the basic cast iron upkeep , dry the grates after use, apply a thin oil coating before storage , the cooking performance justifies the routine. It's maybe five minutes of work after a cook, and it extends the grate's life significantly.

### Grate Dimensions and Bar Spacing

Grate dimensions are the mechanical fit question , width and depth need to match your cooking box. But bar spacing is the cooking question. Narrower bar spacing supports smaller items like shrimp, thin fish fillets, and sliced vegetables. Wider spacing creates more direct flame contact and more defined sear marks on larger cuts.

Most replacement cast iron grates for standard gas grills fall into a spacing range that handles versatile cooking. If you're primarily doing burgers and steaks, standard spacing is fine. If you regularly cook fish or vegetables, evaluate bar spacing specifically rather than assuming it's adequate.

### Surface Seasoning and First-Use Preparation

Most cast iron grates arrive with a light factory coating , enough to protect during shipping, not enough to cook on without preparation. Before the first use, wash the grates with mild soap to remove any manufacturing residue, dry them completely, coat lightly with high-smoke-point oil (flaxseed, canola, or grapeseed all work), and run them through a seasoning cycle on a hot grill or in an oven.

That first seasoning layer builds the foundation of the cooking surface. Each subsequent cook adds to it. The more you use cast iron properly, the better it performs. If you're browsing the broader [grill accessories and components](/tools-and-grates/) available for your setup, grates are where seasoning discipline pays the most visible dividend.

## Buying Guide

### Start with Your Grill's Model Number

Every purchase decision in this category begins and ends with model compatibility. Cast iron replacement grates are manufactured to dimensional specifications, and those specifications are model-specific. Before comparing products, find your grill's model number and have it ready. Check the compatibility list on each listing against that number , not against the brand name, not against a general description of your grill's size or burner count.

Burner count is a useful filter but not a reliable final check. Multiple grill models share burner counts but differ in cooking box dimensions. A 4-burner grill from one production year may have different grate dimensions than a 4-burner from two years later.

### Evaluate the Compatibility List Carefully

A long compatibility list isn't automatically a positive signal. It can mean the grate dimensions are standard across many models, which is genuinely useful. It can also mean the seller is being optimistic about fit. Focus on whether your exact model number appears , not whether a similar model number appears, not whether the brand and burner count match.

If your model isn't listed explicitly, contact the seller before purchasing. A 17-inch grate that's a quarter-inch wider than your cooking box won't seat correctly, and returning cast iron grates after they've been used isn't always straightforward. The two minutes of verification upfront is worth it. For additional context on how replacement grates fit into your overall setup, the full [tools and grates](/tools-and-grates/) resource covers component compatibility more broadly.

### Understand What You're Maintaining

Cast iron grates require more active maintenance than porcelain-coated steel, but the maintenance is simple. After each cook, brush the grates while they're still warm, let them cool, and apply a thin oil coating before closing the lid. If you live in a humid climate or your grill is exposed to rain, the drying step is especially important.

Rust on cast iron is recoverable. Surface rust , orange discoloration with no deep pitting , can be removed with a stiff wire brush and re-seasoned. Deep pitting means the iron has degraded and the grate needs replacement. Catching rust early and re-seasoning is far cheaper than replacement. The maintenance routine takes five minutes and becomes habit quickly.

### Kit Purchases vs. Individual Grates

Some replacement listings bundle grates with heat plates, emitters, or other components. The bundle approach makes sense if multiple components in your grill need replacement simultaneously , you consolidate the sourcing and often reduce the total cost relative to buying parts individually. It makes less sense if only the grates are worn, because you're paying for components you don't need.

Assess each component in your grill before purchasing. Heat plates rust from the inside out and often fail before grates do. Grates wear on the surface and can be re-seasoned through moderate rust. Buying a full kit to replace only functional heat plates adds unnecessary cost without adding value.

### Infrared Grills Require Additional Verification

Charbroil's TRU Infrared system , and similar infrared designs from other manufacturers , uses an emitter panel between the burner and the cooking grate. On these grills, the grate and emitter are a matched system. Replacing the grate without replacing a damaged emitter defeats some of the purpose. Listings that include both components are the right choice for TRU Infrared owners.

Standard cast iron replacement grates are not compatible with TRU Infrared systems unless specifically listed as such. The emitter geometry and the grate's interaction with radiant heat are different from a conventional gas grill setup. Verify that any replacement grate listing explicitly names your Charbroil TRU Infrared model number before purchasing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know which cast iron grates are compatible with my grill?

Find your grill's model number , typically on a plate inside the lid or on the cart frame , and check it against the compatibility list on the replacement grate listing. Brand name and burner count alone are not reliable filters because grate dimensions can vary within the same brand across model years. Confirming your exact model number is the only reliable method.
### Do cast iron grill grates require seasoning before first use?

Yes. Most cast iron grates arrive with a light factory coating that protects during shipping but isn't adequate for cooking. Before first use, wash the grates with mild soap, dry them completely, apply a thin layer of high-smoke-point oil, and run a seasoning cycle on a hot grill or in an oven. That initial layer builds the foundation of the cooking surface.
### Can I use cast iron grates on a TRU Infrared Charbroil grill?

Only if the listing explicitly states compatibility with your TRU Infrared model. Standard replacement cast iron grates are not interchangeable with TRU Infrared systems because those grills require a matched grate and emitter panel. The [17" Grill Grates and Emitter for Charbroil TRU Infrared](product:17-grill-grates-and-emitter) is designed specifically for these grills and includes both components.
### Is it better to buy individual cast iron grates or a full replacement kit?

Buy a kit if multiple components in your grill need replacement at the same time , heat plates, emitters, and grates often wear on similar timelines. Buy individual grates if only the cooking surface is deteriorated. The Uniflasy kit for Dyna-Glo is a reasonable choice when heat plates and grates both need replacement, but it adds unnecessary cost if only the grates are worn.
### How do I remove surface rust from cast iron grill grates?

Surface rust , orange discoloration without deep pitting , is recoverable. Scrub the affected area with a stiff wire brush or steel wool, wash with mild soap, dry the grates completely, and re-season with a thin oil coating followed by a heat cycle. Deep pitting indicates structural degradation and replacement is the better option. Catching rust early and re-seasoning is always cheaper than buying new grates.
