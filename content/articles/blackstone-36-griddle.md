The 36-inch flat top has become the backyard workhorse that grills never quite managed to be , capable of breakfast for eight, smash burgers at noon, and stir fry at dinner without changing the setup. If you've landed here, you're probably sorting through the Blackstone lineup and wondering which version makes sense for your patio and your cooking habits. I've spent enough time with flat tops to know the details that matter and the specs that mostly don't. [Griddles](/griddles/) are a different cooking discipline than open-flame grilling, and picking the right one saves real frustration later.

The accessories matter too. A 36-inch cooktop represents a meaningful equipment investment, and how you protect and maintain it shapes how long it performs. This article covers the main griddle models alongside the protection gear worth pairing with them.

## What to Look For in a 36-Inch Griddle

### Cooking Surface Quality

The cooking surface is where flat top cooking either works or doesn't. Cold-rolled steel is the standard, and it's genuinely excellent once properly seasoned , it holds heat across the surface and builds the non-stick patina over time that makes flat top cooking so efficient. Look for surface thickness, which affects how evenly heat distributes. Thin surfaces develop hot spots that burn edges while leaving centers underdone. The better Blackstone models use steel gauges that handle high-volume cooking without warping under sustained heat.

Seasoning and surface care are non-negotiable with any flat top. The steel surface requires cooking oil applied in thin layers over heat to build protection , skip that process and the surface rusts. Once the seasoning is established, maintenance is straightforward and the surface improves with use rather than degrading.

### Hood Design and Weather Exposure

A hood on a 36-inch griddle serves two functions that are easy to confuse: it helps retain heat during cooking, and it protects the surface when the griddle is stored outside between sessions. Those are meaningfully different purposes, and not every hood design handles both equally well. A built-in hood that seals reasonably well when closed does real work for outdoor storage. A hood sized primarily for cooking convection may not close flat enough to block rain and debris effectively.

If your griddle lives outside year-round , which most suburban setups require , the hood design and a quality cover together determine how much prep work you face before each cook. Factoring both into the purchase decision is worth the time.

### Side Shelf Configuration

Side shelves on a 36-inch flat top are more than convenience. With a cooking surface this large, you're managing multiple food groups simultaneously, and having nowhere to stage ingredients, tools, or resting food slows the whole operation. Fixed shelves are sturdy but limit how the griddle positions against fences or walls. Folding or extendable shelves recover that flexibility without sacrificing workspace during the cook.

Shelf height relative to the cooking surface matters for workflow. A shelf significantly lower than the surface forces awkward tool movements under real cooking pressure. The better configurations keep prep surfaces within a few inches of griddle height.

### Portability and Footprint

A 36-inch griddle is large equipment. On a 16x14 concrete patio , or anything comparably sized , the difference between a compact caster-wheeled unit and a stationary installation is the difference between a griddle you can move to the corner and one that owns the space permanently. Caster wheel quality varies considerably across models; soft-rolling wheels on uneven concrete surfaces make repositioning much easier than rigid plastic alternatives.

Weight is the other factor. Heavier frames and thicker steel cook better but move less easily. Understanding which constraint matters more for your specific outdoor setup before purchasing saves post-delivery regret. Exploring the full range of [griddle configurations](/griddles/) before settling on a footprint is worth the homework.

## Buying Guide

### Which Blackstone 36 Model Is Right for Your Setup

The Original 36 with caster wheels is for patios where flexibility matters , small spaces, shared areas, or layouts that change. The Omnivore with hard cover is for buyers who want a complete package without sourcing accessories separately. The Iron Forged 2310 is for serious frequent users who prioritize construction durability over a lower initial commitment.

Knowing which constraint is most relevant to your situation , space, portability, or long-term durability , narrows the choice quickly. Trying to evaluate all three without that anchor leads to purchase regret in one direction or another.

### Gas Supply and Ignition Reliability

All three griddle models run on propane, which means tank logistics are part of the ongoing operating cost and planning. A 36-inch flat top running at full heat uses propane meaningfully faster than a smaller unit , factoring that into your tank storage and refill routine before the first large cook avoids the problem of running out mid-session.

Push-button ignition on the Omnivore models is a convenience that earns its value in cold weather and during back-to-back cooking sessions. Manual ignition isn't a dealbreaker, but it adds one more step in conditions where you'd rather not be fumbling with a lighter.

### Cover and Surface Protection as a System

Protection for a 36-inch griddle works as a two-layer system: a silicone mat on the cooking surface and a polyester cover over the full unit. The silicone layer addresses moisture and debris contact with the seasoned steel directly. The cover layer addresses weather, UV exposure, and general outdoor conditions against the frame, shelves, and exterior.

Buying both at the same time as the griddle is practical; retrofitting protection after rust or seasoning damage means extra re-seasoning work that's avoidable from the start. The manufacturer cover fits better than most third-party alternatives for the hooded 36-inch configuration.

### Outdoor Space Planning

A 36-inch flat top with extended side shelves occupies a meaningful footprint. With shelves deployed, figure approximately 60, 65 inches of linear space minimum, and that assumes clear access on the working side. On a typical suburban patio, that translates to a defined area that the griddle largely owns during a cook.

Caster wheels make the difference between a griddle that moves when you need it to and one that becomes a permanent fixture by inertia. For restricted spaces, measuring before purchasing rather than after delivery is the step most people skip and later regret. The broader [griddle](/griddles/) category includes smaller format options worth considering if the 36-inch footprint genuinely doesn't fit.

### Cleaning and Long-Term Maintenance

Flat top maintenance is more systematic than grate grill cleaning but not more difficult once the habit is established. Each cook session ends with a hot-surface scrape using a metal scraper, a wipe with a paper towel, and a thin layer of cooking oil applied while the surface is still warm. That process takes three minutes and protects the seasoning through storage.

The surfaces that fail are almost always ones that were stored without the final oil layer or left uncovered through wet weather. The steel itself is durable , the seasoning on top of it is what requires attention. Building the end-of-cook routine into the habit early prevents the re-seasoning sessions that take longer than all the maintenance they replace.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Blackstone Original 36 and the Iron Forged 2310?

The Original 36 prioritizes portability with caster wheels and an extendable shelf design that works on smaller or more flexible outdoor spaces. The Iron Forged 2310 emphasizes construction quality , heavier frame materials, powder-coated steel for rust resistance, and a more rigid X-braced hood structure. Both have a 36-inch cooking surface, so the choice comes down to whether portability or construction durability is the more important variable for your setup.
### Do I need both a silicone mat and a griddle cover?

They serve different functions, so yes , both are worth having for a griddle that lives outside. The silicone mat sits on the cooking surface and protects the seasoned steel from moisture and debris contact directly. The polyester cover protects the entire unit from weather, UV, and general outdoor exposure. The [BLACKSTONE 5482 Griddle Cover](product:blackstone-5482-griddle-cover-fits-36) pairs well with the [36" Silicone Griddle Mat](product:36-silicone-griddle-mat-for) for full protection.
### How long does it take to season a new 36-inch Blackstone griddle?

Initial seasoning typically takes 45, 60 minutes for the first session. You apply a thin layer of cooking oil to the full surface, heat it to the smoke point, let it cool slightly, and repeat the process three to five times. The surface darkens with each pass. That first session establishes the base layer; the seasoning builds and improves with every subsequent cook over weeks and months of use.
### Is the Blackstone Omnivore hard cover sufficient for outdoor storage without a separate cover?

The hard cover on the Omnivore protects the cooking surface during storage and adds weather resistance compared to an open griddle. For most climates and typical outdoor exposure, it handles short-term storage adequately. For extended outdoor storage, high-rainfall climates, or long winter storage, adding a quality polyester cover over the full unit provides more complete protection for the frame, shelves, and ignition components.
### How much propane does a 36-inch Blackstone griddle use during a typical cook?

Consumption depends on heat level and session length, but a standard 20-pound propane tank typically supports several full cooking sessions at moderate heat before needing a refill. Running all four burners at high heat for an extended period consumes propane meaningfully faster. Keeping a spare tank on hand before any large group cook is the practical approach , running out mid-session on a full griddle of food is a specific frustration worth avoiding.
