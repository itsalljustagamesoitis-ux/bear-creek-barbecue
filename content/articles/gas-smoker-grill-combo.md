Getting a gas grill and a smoker into the same footprint sounds like a compromise until you realize most backyards , and most Saturday schedules , aren't built for two separate rigs. A [gas smoker grill combo](/propane-smokers/) earns its place by collapsing two cooking methods into one unit without requiring you to choose a fuel type before you even know what you're cooking.

The trade-off is real, though. Dual-fuel and dual-surface designs involve more components, more maintenance, and more decisions. What separates a useful combo from a frustrating one is how well each side performs on its own terms.

## What to Look For in a Gas Smoker Grill Combo

### BTU Output and Heat Distribution

Raw BTU numbers are a starting point, not a verdict. A 20,000, 24,000 BTU two-burner system is enough for most backyard grilling, but what matters more is how that heat distributes across the grate surface. Hot spots on one side and cold zones on the other mean you're moving food constantly rather than cooking deliberately.

Look for burner configurations that give you independent zone control. Two burners that can be set independently let you run a two-zone fire , high heat on one side, indirect heat on the other , which is how most real grilling decisions get made. A single undifferentiated heat source is a step down in capability regardless of BTU rating.

### Cooking Surface Versatility

A combo unit sells you on versatility, so the question is whether the surfaces actually deliver different cooking experiences or just different temperatures on the same type of grate. A flat-top griddle surface and a raised grate surface genuinely behave differently , the griddle catches drippings and conducts heat through direct contact, while the grate creates radiant heat and char marks through open flame.

Cast iron surfaces , whether a grate or a griddle pan , hold heat better than steel, recover faster after cold food lands on them, and develop a cooking surface over time with proper seasoning. Porcelain enamel coating adds rust resistance and makes cleanup easier. Both are worth looking for.

### Charcoal and Gas Integration

Pairing charcoal and gas in a single unit isn't just a marketing angle , it reflects a real cooking decision that comes up regularly. Gas is fast, predictable, and easy to shut down. Charcoal adds flavor complexity and a higher sear temperature that propane alone rarely matches. A well-designed dual-fuel combo keeps these two systems genuinely independent rather than awkwardly overlapping.

The critical thing to evaluate is whether you can run both sides simultaneously without one interfering with the other. If the heat transfer between chambers affects your charcoal side when the gas burners are running, you've lost the independence that makes the dual-fuel concept useful.

### Build Quality and Stability

Combo units carry more mechanical complexity than a dedicated grill. There are more joints, more connections, and more surface area exposed to heat cycling. That means build quality , specifically the gauge of the steel, the quality of the welds, and the fit of the lids , matters more here than in a straightforward single-fuel grill.

Side shelves on a combo unit also do real work. When you're managing two cooking methods simultaneously, you need a place to rest food, hold tools, and stage ingredients without walking away from the cook. A shelf that wobbles or detaches under load isn't a minor inconvenience , it's a safety issue.

### Footprint and Storage

Combo units are almost always larger than either of their component grills would be individually. Before ordering, measure your patio or deck and account for clearance from the house, combustibles, and the path you use to move food from the grill to the table. The [range of propane smoker and combo configurations](/propane-smokers/) varies enough that footprint and weight should drive your shortlist as much as features do.

Also consider wheel placement and handle position. A unit that can't be repositioned by one person is going to stay in one spot permanently , which means that spot needs to be exactly right before it arrives.

## Buying Guide

### Choosing Between Dual-Fuel and Dual-Surface

The most important clarification in this category is the difference between a dual-fuel combo (gas and charcoal) and a dual-surface combo (griddle and grate, both on gas). They serve different needs, and confusing the two is the most common source of buyer regret.

A dual-fuel combo gives you charcoal smoke flavor and gas convenience in the same unit. A dual-surface combo gives you two cooking textures , flat griddle and open grate , powered by the same fuel source. Decide which gap you're actually trying to fill before you evaluate specific products.

### How Much Cooking Area Do You Actually Need

Combo units report total cooking area as a combined number across both chambers or surfaces. That number is useful for comparison but can obscure how usable each side is independently. A unit with 870 total square inches split unevenly might leave one side too small for a full cook.

Think about your largest typical cook. If you're feeding six people on a weekend, you need enough simultaneous cooking space to handle the whole meal , not enough space in theory if both sides were merged. A realistic assessment of cooking area per side matters more than the aggregate number.

### Propane Tank Compatibility and Run Time

Gas combo units need to connect to a standard 20-pound propane tank, and most do. The variable is how long a tank lasts given your BTU output and cooking habits. At 20,000, 24,000 BTU across two burners running full, a 20-pound tank delivers several hours of cooking time , enough for a weekend's worth of sessions before a refill.

Keep a spare tank if you cook often. Running out of propane mid-cook on the gas side of a dual-fuel combo is more disruptive than it sounds , you're not just adjusting one variable, you're managing two active cooking methods simultaneously. The [full range of propane smoker options](/propane-smokers/) can help you calibrate how gas consumption fits your cooking style.

### Side Shelf Utility

Side shelves on a combo unit are not optional extras. Two active cooking surfaces mean you're managing more food, more tools, and more timing decisions at once. A pair of solid side shelves , ones that lock level and carry real weight , changes how the whole cook flows.

Folding shelves that collapse for storage are useful if space is tight, but verify they lock firmly in the open position. A shelf that drops while you're holding a cast iron pan isn't just inconvenient. It is a problem you won't forget.

### Maintenance as a Selection Criterion

A combo unit that sits uncovered for a season and becomes difficult to operate is not a bargain at any price. Before buying, think honestly about how you maintain equipment. Dual-fuel combos have more components to inspect , gas connections, burner tubes, charcoal grates, ash management systems, and multiple cooking surfaces.

If you're the type who covers the grill, cleans the grates, and runs a brush through the burner tubes before winter, a full-featured dual-fuel combo will reward that discipline. If your current grill is already harder to clean than you'd like, a dual-surface gas-only combo may be the more realistic choice for your actual habits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a dual-fuel combo grill and a dual-surface combo grill?

A dual-fuel combo pairs two different heat sources , typically propane gas and charcoal , in one unit, letting you choose your fuel type based on the cook. A dual-surface combo uses one fuel source but provides two different cooking surfaces, such as a flat griddle and a raised grate. The Char-Griller and Royal Gourmet units here are dual-fuel; the Captiva Designs is a dual-surface gas unit. The distinction shapes how you cook, not just how you ignite.
### Can I run the gas side and the charcoal side of a combo grill at the same time?

Yes, most well-designed dual-fuel combos are built for simultaneous use , that's the practical value of the format. The [Char-Griller Dual-Function 2-Burner Combo](product:char-griller-dual-function-2) handles this with independently chambered cooking zones that limit heat crossover. In practice, managing two active fuel sources at once does require attention and some experience with each method individually before you combine them.
### Is a gas smoker grill combo harder to maintain than a dedicated grill?

It requires more attention, not harder attention. Two fuel systems mean two sets of components , gas connections, burner tubes, charcoal grates, and ash collection , all need to be inspected and cleaned. If you're already consistent about grill maintenance, a combo unit is manageable. If your current grill sits uncleaned between seasons, a dual-fuel combo will compound that problem faster than a single-fuel unit would.
### Which combo grill is better for someone who is new to dual-fuel cooking?

The [Royal Gourmet ZH3002N](product:royal-gourmet-zh3002n-propane-gas) is the most approachable starting point. Its layout is straightforward, the two sides are clearly separated, and the side shelves give you staging space while you're learning to manage both fuel types. It has less total capacity than the Char-Griller, which is a real trade-off, but the lower complexity is worth it if dual-fuel cooking is new territory for you.
### Does a cast iron griddle surface on a combo grill require special care?

Cast iron requires seasoning , a thin layer of oil applied and heated to bond with the surface , and should be dried thoroughly after cleaning to prevent rust. The Captiva Designs griddle uses porcelain-enameled cast iron for the grate, which reduces but doesn't eliminate maintenance requirements. A properly maintained cast iron surface becomes more effective over time, developing a naturally non-stick layer. Neglected cast iron corrodes and becomes harder to rehabilitate the longer it sits untreated.
