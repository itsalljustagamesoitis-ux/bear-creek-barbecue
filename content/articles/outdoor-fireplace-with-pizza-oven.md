Finding an outdoor fireplace that doubles as a pizza oven sounds like a weekend project that ends well , until you realize most products are built for one job or the other. The [pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) category has expanded enough that a few systems now bridge both functions, and the fire pit market has followed with designs serious enough to anchor a backyard cooking setup.

What separates a useful combination from a compromised one is understanding where the engineering trade-offs land. Heat retention, portability, fuel type, and cooking surface size all pull in different directions, and the right answer depends on how you actually use your outdoor space.

## What to Look For in an Outdoor Fireplace with Pizza Oven

### Heat Output and Temperature Range

A pizza oven needs to reach temperatures most backyard fire pits never approach. Neapolitan-style pizza wants 700°F or above at the cooking surface , wood-fired and propane-fed ovens with proper dome architecture can get there. A fire pit repurposed as a cooking surface runs substantially cooler, which shifts your results toward flatbreads and grilled pizza rather than true Neapolitan.

If your priority is pizza quality, look for a dedicated oven with a cordierite stone or ceramic deck. These materials store heat efficiently and release it evenly into the bottom of the crust. A fire pit is a fine fire pit, but it won't replicate that heat profile regardless of how much wood you add.

### Fuel Type and Fuel Management

Wood-burning units produce the smoke, char, and ambient experience most people picture when they imagine an outdoor fireplace. They also require sourcing, storing, and managing fuel , and if your setup is on a patio in a suburban neighborhood, smoke management becomes a practical concern, not just an aesthetic one.

Propane and gas-powered ovens trade some of that ambiance for convenience and precision. You can dial in a temperature and hold it, which matters if you're cooking four or five pizzas in sequence for a group. If you're choosing between fuel types, consider how often you'll actually manage a wood fire versus how often you'll want to light up quickly without the prep.

### Portability and Footprint

Permanent outdoor fireplaces and pizza ovens are built-in projects , masonry, permits, and significant yard space. Portable stainless steel units occupy a different category: they travel, they store, they don't require contractor work, and they work on patios where a permanent structure isn't an option.

The trade-off is mass. A built-in oven holds heat longer because it has more thermal mass. A portable unit heats quickly and cools quickly, which suits weekend cooks who don't want a two-hour preheat. For anyone working within HOA restrictions or on a rental property, the portable category is often the only realistic path into outdoor fire cooking. The [full range of pizza ovens](/pizza-ovens/) worth considering spans both permanent and portable formats, and understanding your site constraints before browsing saves time.

### Cooking Surface and Capacity

A 15-inch fire pit accommodates two people comfortably. A 27-inch fire pit can handle a group of six or more. Pizza ovens are measured differently , the deck size and opening width determine what pan or stone fits, and whether a 12-inch pizza can rotate freely inside the chamber.

Match capacity to your actual use. If you're cooking for a family of four on Saturday evenings, a mid-size portable setup handles it. If you regularly host larger groups, size up , the frustration of batching pizzas on a too-small deck compounds quickly when everyone is standing around waiting.

## Buying Guide

### Decide Whether You Want Fire or Pizza , Then See If You Can Have Both

The central tension in this product category is that fire pits and pizza ovens are engineered for different things. A fire pit optimizes for ambiance, warmth, and gathering. A pizza oven optimizes for high, sustained heat at the cooking surface. Products that attempt to serve both functions generally compromise one.

If pizza is the priority, buy the Pi Prime and accept that it's not a campfire experience. If fire atmosphere is the priority, buy a fire pit and manage your expectations about cooking capability. The buyers who end up most satisfied are usually the ones who picked a lane.

### Match Size to Your Actual Group

Sizing errors in both directions create frustration. Too small, and you're managing a fire that doesn't heat the group; too large, and you're burning through wood to maintain a fire that overwhelms a small patio or a two-person setup.

Solo Stove's lineup gives you three clear size points: Ranger for two to four people, Bonfire for four to six, Yukon for six or more. Use those guidelines as a starting point and adjust based on your patio dimensions. A 27-inch fire pit on a 12-foot patio is a different experience than the same unit in an open yard.

### Fuel Type Changes the Experience More Than the Specs Suggest

The difference between wood-burning and propane isn't just operational convenience , it's sensory. Wood fires smell different, sound different, and produce a different visual flame than gas. For some buyers, that distinction is the whole reason for a backyard fire setup.

If you want the wood-burning experience, plan for fuel storage and management. If you want repeatable, fast ignition without fuel logistics, propane is the cleaner solution. The [outdoor pizza oven options](/pizza-ovens/) that run on propane are particularly well-suited to frequent weeknight use where setup time matters.

### Consider Portability Against Permanence

Portable stainless steel units work on patios, decks, and rental properties where permanent installations aren't viable. They heat up faster than masonry, they move when you need them to, and they don't require permits. The trade-off is thermal mass , a built-in brick oven holds heat longer and more evenly than any portable unit.

For most suburban buyers cooking on a concrete patio, portable is the only realistic option. For buyers with the yard space and permanence to invest in a built-in structure, the performance ceiling is higher. Know which situation you're in before comparing specs across categories that aren't actually competing for your purchase.

### Cleanup and Maintenance Are Part of the Weekly Calculus

A unit you dread cleaning is a unit you use less. The removable ash pan across the Solo Stove lineup is a deliberate design choice that pays off in sustained use , pull the pan, dump it, replace it. The Sunjoy's chimney setup requires more effort.

Think about where you'll store your unit between uses and what your cleanup routine actually looks like. Stainless steel is weather-resistant but benefits from a cover in wet climates. The Ranger includes a carry case; other units in this lineup benefit from aftermarket covers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Solo Stove fire pit actually be used for cooking pizza?

No Solo Stove fire pit in this lineup is a pizza oven , they're fire pits. You can cook food over them with the right accessories, including flatbreads and grilled items, but you won't reach the deck temperatures a pizza oven produces. If pizza is your primary goal, the [Solo Stove Pi Prime](product:solo-stove-pi-prime-pizza) is the only unit here built specifically for that purpose, with a cordierite stone deck and dome architecture designed for it.
### What's the difference between the Bonfire and the Yukon for a backyard patio?

The core difference is size and heat output. The [Bonfire](product:solo-stove-bonfire-19-5) works well for four to six people and suits mid-size patios where a 27-inch unit would feel oversized. The [Yukon](product:solo-stove-yukon-27-inch) is built for larger groups of six or more and produces substantially more heat, which also means burning more wood per session. Both use the same secondary combustion design for smoke reduction , the choice comes down to how many people you're typically hosting.
### Is propane or wood-burning better for an outdoor pizza oven setup?

For pizza specifically, propane offers a meaningful advantage in temperature control and repeatability. You can hold a consistent 700°F across multiple pies without managing a wood fire between bakes. Wood-burning produces more atmospheric smoke and char character, which some cooks prefer for flavor. If you're running a backyard pizza night for a group, propane's faster recovery time between pizzas is a practical advantage worth considering.
### How do I manage smoke from a wood-burning fire pit near neighbors?

The Solo Stove models in this lineup are specifically engineered to reduce smoke through secondary combustion , they burn significantly cleaner than an open campfire or traditional fire pit. The [Ranger 2.0](product:solo-stove-ranger-2-0) and [Bonfire](product:solo-stove-bonfire-19-5) both carry this feature. Wind direction still matters, and no portable fire pit is completely smokeless, but the reduction is substantial enough to make a real difference in close-quarters suburban settings.
### Can I leave a portable stainless steel fire pit outside year-round?

Stainless steel is weather-resistant, but leaving any portable unit exposed through extended wet seasons accelerates surface oxidation and degrades the finish over time. Most manufacturers recommend using a cover when the unit isn't in use. The [Sunjoy](product:sunjoy-wood-burning-fireplace-matte) includes a PVC cover; Solo Stove covers are available separately. Bringing units under a covered patio or storing them in a garage during extended off-seasons extends the life of the finish and the components meaningfully.
