Choosing a propane gas grill that actually fits your patio, your cooking style, and the number of people you're feeding is harder than it sounds. The options range from stripped-down three-burner models to six-burner workhorses with side burners and cabinet storage , and the spec sheets don't tell you much about how any of them actually cook. I've spent enough time researching [Gas Grills](/gas-grills/) to know that BTU numbers and square-inch claims are the beginning of the conversation, not the end of it.

The real question is fit. A grill that works perfectly for a four-person household on a 12-foot deck is a different machine than one suited for feeding a neighborhood. Below are five propane grills worth considering, ranging from a manageable three-burner setup to a full six-burner configuration.

## What to Look For in a Propane Gas Grill

### BTU Output and Heat Distribution

BTU output is the number most manufacturers lead with, and it's the one that's most frequently misunderstood. Raw BTU tells you how much fuel the grill can burn , it doesn't tell you how efficiently that heat reaches your food. A three-burner grill putting out 30,000 BTU across 472 square inches is burning roughly 63 BTU per square inch. A six-burner grill pushing 65,800 BTU across 665 square inches runs about 99 BTU per square inch. That difference shows up in searing performance and recovery time after you open the lid.

What matters alongside the raw number is how evenly those burners distribute heat. A grill with one hot spot and two dead zones is going to frustrate you every time you try to cook chicken thighs alongside a couple of steaks. Multi-burner setups help here because you can adjust zones independently , high heat on one side for searing, lower heat on the other for holding or finishing.

### Cooking Surface and Grate Material

The advertised cooking area almost always includes a secondary warming rack. For practical grilling purposes, focus on the primary grate. Cast iron grates , especially porcelain-enameled cast iron , hold heat better than stainless steel rod grates and produce better sear marks. They're also more forgiving when food has any natural sugars. The tradeoff is that cast iron requires a bit more maintenance; leaving it wet invites rust even through the enamel coating.

Stainless steel grates are easier to clean and stand up to neglect better, but they release heat faster and don't give you the same thermal mass for searing. Neither is wrong. The question is whether you're willing to do a bit of post-cook maintenance in exchange for better crust development.

### Burner Count and Zone Management

Three burners give you a reasonable amount of flexibility for a family of four. Four burners open up indirect cooking zones without crowding. Five and six burners become meaningful when you're cooking for a crowd or want to run dramatically different temperature zones simultaneously , low and slow on one end, screaming hot on the other.

Side burners are genuinely useful if you're someone who makes compound butters, sauces, or sautéed sides to go with what's on the grill. If you're primarily a steak-and-burgers cook who doesn't want to walk inside for anything, a side burner earns its footprint. If you mostly grill and finish inside, it's extra surface area you won't use. Exploring the full range of [gas grill](/gas-grills/) configurations before you commit to a burner count is worth the time , the footprint differences between a three-burner and a six-burner are substantial.

### Build Quality and Weather Resistance

Stainless steel construction is the standard claim across almost every grill at this level, but stainless steel grades vary. Look for rolled edges rather than sharp-cut sheet metal, and check how the side shelves are attached , flimsy shelf brackets are a consistent weak point on mid-tier grills. Porcelain-coated fireboxes protect the interior from grease buildup and moisture; bare steel fireboxes require more diligent cleaning to avoid rust.

Cabinet-style bases with doors outperform open-cart designs for keeping propane tanks contained and protecting burner components from weather exposure. If your grill is going to sit outside year-round, that cabinet matters more than it might seem on first inspection.

## Buying Guide

### How Much Space Do You Actually Have?

The footprint question should come before almost everything else. A six-burner propane grill with a side burner and cabinet base can run four feet deep and five-plus feet wide. On a 16×14 patio with outdoor furniture, that's a significant commitment. Measure your available space with the grill at full operational size , burner knobs require clearance behind, and side shelves extend the width when deployed.

Three- and four-burner configurations occupy meaningfully less space. If your outdoor setup is more modest, the cooking capacity difference between a four-burner and a six-burner matters less than whether the grill actually fits.

### Matching Burner Count to How Many People You Feed

The most common mistake in propane grill buying is buying for your biggest cooking day rather than your typical cooking day. If you grill for four people four nights a week and host twenty people twice a year, optimizing for the big cookout means using an oversized grill constantly.

Three burners handles four to six people without issue. Four burners handles six to eight people with zone flexibility to spare. Five and six burners earn their footprint when you're regularly cooking for ten or more, or when you want to run dramatically different cooking modes simultaneously.

### Grate Material and Your Maintenance Tolerance

Porcelain-enameled cast iron grates are the better cooking surface. They hold heat through the lid openings that come with any active grill session, and the sear quality is noticeably better than stainless rod grates at equivalent temperatures. The maintenance requirement is real but manageable: dry the grates after cleaning, apply a light coat of oil if you're storing the grill for more than a few days, and don't leave food residue sitting on them.

Stainless steel grates are more forgiving if maintenance isn't your priority. They clean easily, they don't rust if you leave them damp, and they'll outlast neglect. The tradeoff is cooking performance. For a buyer who grills occasionally and doesn't want to think about grate care, stainless is the right call.

### Propane Tank Logistics

Every grill on this list runs on propane, which means you're managing a fuel supply. A standard 20-pound propane tank runs approximately 18, 20 hours on a medium-burner setup. A six-burner grill running all burners on high will work through a tank considerably faster. Keep a second tank on hand if you're cooking for a crowd , running out of fuel mid-cook is avoidable.

Tank storage matters too. Propane tanks should be stored upright, outside, away from heat sources , not inside a garage or shed. Grills with enclosed cabinet bases keep the active tank contained and protected during cooking, which is a meaningful safety and convenience advantage over open-cart designs. Reviewing the broader landscape of [propane and gas grill options](/gas-grills/) before you buy is worthwhile if tank logistics are a concern.

### Side Burners , Useful or Wasted Space?

A side burner is worth the footprint if you cook in a way that generates sides or sauces simultaneously with the main grill. Compound butter while the steak rests, sautéed peppers and onions for fajitas, a pot of beans alongside a rack of ribs , these all make the side burner earn its place.

If your grilling style is put meat on, take meat off, eat , the side burner adds footprint without adding value. The grills that include side burners on this list tend to be the larger configurations anyway, so the incremental space cost is lower. But it's worth being honest with yourself about how you actually cook before counting it as a feature.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many BTU do I need for a propane gas grill?

For most backyard grilling, 80, 100 BTU per square inch of primary cooking surface is a reasonable target. Below that range, you'll notice slower recovery times after opening the lid and reduced searing performance on thick cuts. Above it, you gain headroom for high-heat cooking but also burn through propane faster. A three-burner grill at 30,000 BTU across 472 square inches sits at the lower end and is adequate for everyday grilling, while the [Captiva Designs 6-Burner](product:captiva-designs-6-burner-propane) at 65,800 BTU across 665 square inches gives you substantially more thermal headroom.
### What is the difference between a 3-burner and a 5-burner propane grill?

The practical difference is zone flexibility and total cooking capacity. A three-burner setup gives you a basic two-zone configuration , one side hot, one side cooler for indirect cooking. A five-burner setup lets you run three distinct temperature zones simultaneously, which matters when you're cooking different proteins that need different heat levels at the same time. The [Royal Gourmet GA5401T](product:royal-gourmet-ga5401t-5-burner) illustrates what five burners enables: enough coverage to run a legitimate multi-zone session without crowding the grate.
### Are porcelain-enameled cast iron grates better than stainless steel grates?
For cooking performance, yes. Porcelain-enameled cast iron retains heat more effectively than stainless steel, which means it recovers faster after you place cold food on it and produces better sear marks. The tradeoff is maintenance , cast iron grates need to be dried and lightly oiled to prevent rust, even through the enamel coating. Stainless steel grates are more maintenance-forgiving and resist neglect better.
### Is a side burner worth it on a propane gas grill?
It depends entirely on how you cook. A side burner adds real value if you regularly make sauces, sauté vegetables, or prepare sides outdoors while the grill is running. It adds wasted footprint if you put protein on the grill and walk away. Grills like the [ROVSUN 4 Burner](product:rovsun-4-burner-propane-gas) skip the side burner and focus that space on the primary cooking area, which is the right call for some buyers.
### Can I convert a propane gas grill to natural gas?

Some grills support a conversion kit that swaps out the orifices and regulator for natural gas operation. Most grills in the mid-tier range do not include this option or officially support it. If running a permanent natural gas line is an option for your outdoor space and you want to eliminate propane tank management, the right move is to shop specifically for grills marketed with a natural gas conversion option rather than attempting an unsupported conversion.
