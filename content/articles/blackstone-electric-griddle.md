Electric griddles have carved out a real place on the patio and the kitchen counter, and Blackstone's move into the electric category has a lot of weekend cooks paying attention. If you've been browsing the full range of [griddles](/griddles/) and wondering whether electric makes sense for your setup, that question is worth taking seriously before you buy. The cooking surface size, heat control, and coating all matter more than the brand name on the front.

The difference between a griddle that earns counter space and one that ends up in a cabinet usually comes down to how the heat behaves and how hard the cleanup is. This round-up covers four electric griddle options, three from Blackstone's own lineup and one from outside the brand, to help you figure out which one fits the way you actually cook.

## What to Look For in an Electric Griddle

### Cooking Surface Area

Square inches of cooking surface determine whether you're feeding two people or eight. A 22-inch griddle gives you room to run bacon down one side while eggs cook on the other without crowding either. Smaller surfaces force you into batches, which means food sitting and cooling while the rest finishes.

For a family or a regular crowd, anything under 20 inches starts to feel cramped fast. The math is straightforward: every inch you lose in width is a row of pancakes you can't cook simultaneously. Think about your most common cooking scenario, Sunday breakfast for the household, and size from there.

### Heat Output and Temperature Control

Electric griddles are measured in watts, and that number is the ceiling on how fast the surface recovers after you load cold food onto it. A 1600W unit recovers meaningfully faster than a 900W unit after you lay down six burgers. Recovery time is what separates a griddle that sears from one that steams.

Temperature control matters just as much as raw power. A dial that gives you five broad settings is harder to work with than one with a fine-grained range or a digital readout. You want to hold 275°F for pancakes and push to 400°F for smash burgers without guessing where the dial needs to land.

### Nonstick Coating Type

Ceramic titanium and standard nonstick coatings behave differently under high heat and repeated use. Ceramic titanium coatings tend to hold up better over time and are generally considered more durable against metal utensils, though you should still avoid anything sharp. Standard nonstick is fine at lower temperatures but can degrade faster if you regularly cook at the high end of the range.

For everyday use, the coating is often the thing that determines how long the griddle stays useful. A surface that starts sticking after a year of use means you're either replacing it or fighting eggs every morning. Don't overlook it as a spec.

### Lid Design and Steam Control

A lid isn't just a cover, it changes what the griddle can do. A vented lid lets steam escape while keeping heat in, which is useful for cooking proteins through without drying the surface. A glass lid lets you monitor food without lifting and breaking the cook. Some lids rotate or remove entirely, which affects how you manage the cooking process mid-meal.

If you plan to cook anything thicker than a pancake, chicken thighs, thick-cut pork chops, smash burger stacks, a lid is the difference between finishing on the griddle and finishing in the oven. Worth factoring into the buying decision before you assume you don't need one.

### Indoor vs. Outdoor Suitability

Not all electric griddles are built for both environments. A unit designed strictly for indoor use typically lacks the weather resistance you'd want on a patio, and running a long extension cord to a wet surface is a safety problem. Conversely, some griddles marketed for outdoor tabletop use aren't great at managing smoke and grease in a kitchen without a range hood.

Before buying, be honest about where this griddle will actually live. Exploring the broader [griddle](/griddles/) category is worth doing if you're still deciding between indoor-only and dual-use setups, the differences in build and design are more significant than they appear in product listings.

## Buying Guide

### Indoor Use vs. Outdoor Tabletop

The most important question before buying an electric griddle is where it will actually be used. A kitchen counter placement means smoke management becomes a real concern, electric griddles don't produce the open flame of a gas burner, but they do produce smoke at high heat, and a range hood makes the difference between a comfortable cook and a triggered smoke alarm. The Ninja Sizzle Pro XL is explicitly designed for indoor use, with ventilation built into the lid design. The Blackstone E-Series units are marketed as indoor and outdoor, but their large tabletop footprint makes outdoor use more natural.

If the griddle is going on a patio table, weather exposure and power cord management become the relevant variables. An outdoor circuit or a properly rated extension cord is not optional, it's a safety requirement.

### Wattage and Heat Recovery

A 1600W griddle and a 1000W griddle are not the same tool. The difference shows up most clearly during recovery: the time it takes the surface to return to target temperature after you load cold food. For pancakes and eggs at moderate temperatures, the gap between wattage levels is small. For smash burgers or anything you want a proper sear on, recovery speed matters, and lower wattage units tend to steam food rather than sear it when the surface can't recover fast enough.

Match the wattage to the cooking you actually do. High-heat cooks need more watts. Moderate-temperature batch cooking, breakfast, quesadillas, grilled cheese, is well served by units in the mid-range.

### Coating Durability and Maintenance

Ceramic titanium coatings outlast standard nonstick under regular high-heat use, and that difference compounds over two or three years of weekly cooking. The BELLA, both Blackstone units, and the Ninja all use ceramic or ceramic titanium surfaces, which is a meaningful upgrade over basic PTFE coatings. That said, all nonstick surfaces benefit from the same basic care: no metal utensils with sharp edges, no stacking other cookware directly on the surface, and occasional seasoning with a thin layer of oil after cleaning.

Submersible cooking surfaces, available on some BELLA models, make cleanup significantly easier. If you cook messy things regularly, the ability to fully submerge the cooking surface is worth factoring into your choice.

### Surface Size and Batch Cooking Needs

A 22-inch cooking surface and a 20-inch cooking surface sound close, but in practice the extra two inches is a meaningful row of food. For solo cooks or couples, either size is more than sufficient. For a family of four or five cooking a full breakfast, the 22-inch models let you finish everything in one pass rather than two. Running two batches means the first round is cold by the time the second finishes.

If you're comparing options across the wider [griddle](/griddles/) category, including gas-powered tabletop models, surface area comparisons hold the same way. More square inches mean fewer batches. The math doesn't change by fuel type.

### Lid Type and Cooking Versatility

Not every electric griddle ships with a lid, and the ones that do vary considerably in how useful the lid actually is. A flat lid that traps steam without a vent forces you to choose between keeping heat in and releasing moisture. A vented or glass lid with rotation capability, like the patented hood on the Blackstone 1600W, gives you active control over the cook environment. For anything beyond flat-cooking pancakes and eggs, lid quality determines how far the griddle's range actually extends.

A lid that lets you steam chicken, melt cheese, and finish thick proteins on the griddle surface means the unit replaces more equipment. A lid that just sits on top is better than no lid, but it's not the same capability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Blackstone E-Series base model and the 1600W model?

The 1600W model adds ceramic titanium coating, an LCD temperature display, and the patented rotate-and-remove glass hood. The base E-Series uses a standard nonstick surface and simpler controls. Both share the same 22-inch cooking surface. The 1600W's higher wattage also means faster surface recovery after loading cold food, which matters most if you cook at high heat regularly.
### Is an electric griddle suitable for outdoor use year-round?

Outdoor electric griddle use requires a weatherproof power source and appropriate extension cord rating, never run an indoor-rated cord to an outdoor outlet in wet conditions. In cold weather, electric griddles take longer to reach target temperature and may struggle to maintain heat against wind. The Blackstone E-Series units are rated for indoor and outdoor tabletop use, but performance in cold or windy conditions will be noticeably reduced compared to gas alternatives.
### How does the Ninja Sizzle Pro XL compare to the Blackstone electric models for indoor cooking?

The [Ninja Sizzle Pro XL](product:ninja-sizzle-pro-xl-20-inch) is purpose-built for indoor use, with grease control and a ventilated lid designed specifically for kitchen environments. The Blackstone models are usable indoors but aren't designed with kitchen smoke management as a primary feature. If the griddle will live on a kitchen counter full-time, the Ninja's indoor-first design is a practical advantage. If you plan to move it between inside and outside, the Blackstone E-Series is the more flexible option.
### What nonstick coating is most durable for regular high-heat cooking?

Ceramic titanium coatings, used on the Blackstone 1600W and the BELLA XL, hold up better than standard nonstick under frequent high-heat use. They resist degradation at upper temperature ranges and are more tolerant of minor contact with non-sharp utensils. No nonstick surface is indestructible, but ceramic titanium extends the useful life of the cooking surface meaningfully compared to basic coatings when you cook regularly at or near maximum temperature settings.
### How do I decide between the BELLA XL and the Blackstone electric griddles?

The BELLA XL is the better choice for high-volume, moderate-temperature cooking, large family breakfasts, batch meal prep, situations where surface area and easy cleanup matter most. The Blackstone models, particularly the 1600W, are better for cooks who want higher heat performance, better temperature control, and lid-assisted cooking versatility. If searing is part of your routine, the Blackstone 1600W is the stronger tool. If you primarily want to cook eggs, pancakes, and simple meals in volume, the BELLA holds its own.
