A grill cover is one of those purchases that feels optional right up until the moment you lift off a cover full of standing water and find rust eating through your burner housings. For anyone running a Char-Broil three or four burner setup, the stakes are real , these grills represent a meaningful investment, and the right cover is the cheapest insurance you can buy. The [tools and grates](/tools-and-grates/) that support a grill setup are only as useful as the gear they're protecting.

Choosing a cover sounds simple until you realize that "fits 3, 4 burner grills" can mean a spread of eight inches in actual width, and that not all waterproofing holds up the same way after a full Ohio winter. What separates a cover that earns its keep from one that cracks, shrinks, or blows off in a wind gust comes down to a few specific factors worth understanding before you buy.

## What to Look For in a Grill Cover

### Fit , Width Matters More Than Burner Count

Burner count is a starting point, not a guarantee. Two grills labeled "4-burner" from different manufacturers can differ by six inches or more in overall width. Before you commit to any cover, measure your grill from handle to handle at its widest point , include side tables if they don't fold flat. A cover that gaps at the sides or pulls tight at the corners isn't doing the job. Most covers in this category are designed for grills up to 58 to 62 inches wide, but the lower end of that range can be just as important. A cover too large for your grill collects wind like a sail and traps moisture underneath.

### Material and Waterproofing Construction

Not all heavy-duty claims are equal. Look for covers made from 600D polyester or comparable Oxford weave fabric , the denier rating gives you a baseline for abrasion resistance. Waterproofing is typically either a PVC backing or a polyurethane coating; PVC tends to be heavier and more rigid in cold weather, while polyurethane stays more flexible when temperatures drop below freezing. Seam construction matters here too. A cover with welded or double-stitched seams will outlast one where the stitching acts as a water entry point after the first hard rain.

### UV and Fade Resistance

A grill cover spends every day in direct sun. Fabrics that aren't UV-stabilized will chalk and crack within a season or two, and once the outer surface degrades, waterproofing follows. Fade resistance isn't just cosmetic , it's a signal about how the material will hold up over time. This is especially worth considering if your grill is in an exposed southern or western exposure where it takes afternoon sun year-round.

### Ventilation and Moisture Trapping

A fully sealed cover sounds like the safest option, but it can create a humidity problem. Without some airflow, condensation builds inside the cover , particularly through temperature swings , and that trapped moisture accelerates rust just as reliably as rain. Some covers include side vents specifically to address this. If yours doesn't, pulling the cover slightly off-center on mild days helps. The [full range of grill tools and grates](/tools-and-grates/) worth protecting under a good cover becomes worthless faster when moisture is locked in rather than out.

### Storage and Ease of Use

A cover you don't use consistently isn't protecting anything. Consider how the cover stores when you're cooking , some have elastic hems or toggle straps that make removal and replacement quick, while others take both hands and a wrestling match to get back on. If you grill three or four times a week through the summer, a cover that's frustrating to handle will end up staying on the ground more than on the grill.

## Buying Guide

### Brand-Matched vs. Third-Party Covers

The simplest version of this decision: if you own a Char-Broil grill and you want the cover to fit the way the grill was designed to be covered, buy a Char-Broil cover. The brand-specific options in this category are built around Char-Broil's own cabinet dimensions, and that precision shows in how cleanly the cover sits. Third-party covers , including excellent ones like the Unicook , are designed to fit a range of grills, which means the fit is adequate but rarely exact. Neither approach is wrong. Brand-matched is better for fit; third-party is often better for material features.

### Size and Width Measurement

Measure your grill before you buy any cover. Write down the width at the widest point , handles included, side tables included if they stay extended while cooking. Most 3-burner grills run 48 to 54 inches wide; most 4-burner models run 55 to 62 inches. A cover undersized by even two inches will pull across the lid and fail at the seams faster. A cover oversized by six or more inches will flap in wind and trap moisture at the hem. The right size is one that fits with an inch or two of drop , not six. This is also worth cross-referencing with the broader [tools and grates](/tools-and-grates/) resources if you're outfitting a new grill setup from scratch.

### Climate and Material Matching

A cover in coastal South Carolina faces different demands than one in suburban Ohio. If your grill is in a high-UV environment, fade resistance matters more than it would in a shaded or northern setup. If you're in a climate with hard freezes, PVC-backed covers can stiffen and crack at the folds , polyurethane-coated fabrics stay more pliable in cold. Humid climates with temperature swings benefit from vented covers that allow airflow. Match the material to your actual conditions rather than buying on weight alone.

### How Often You Grill

Cover convenience is underrated as a purchase factor. If you cook four nights a week through summer, you're taking the cover on and off constantly. In that scenario, a cover with an easy hem toggle and a shape that comes off cleanly with one hand matters , friction in the process means the cover ends up on the deck more than on the grill. If you grill twice a month and the cover mostly lives on the grill, a tighter, more protective fit is worth the occasional effort of removing it.

### Storage Between Seasons

Some buyers store their grill indoors through winter, which means the cover is redundant for the hardest months. Others leave the grill outside year-round, which means the cover needs to survive ice, snow load, and sustained wind. If you're in the latter situation, invest in a heavier construction regardless of brand. A basic cover will technically work for one or two seasons, but in a climate with genuine winters, a heavy-duty waterproof option will outlast it significantly and protect the grill more reliably across freeze-thaw cycles.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a Char-Broil brand cover, or will any 3, 4 burner cover fit?

A Char-Broil branded cover is designed around Char-Broil's own cabinet dimensions, so the fit tends to be cleaner and more precise on their grills. Third-party covers sized for 3, 4 burner grills will work, but the fit depends heavily on your grill's actual width , measure before buying. Options like the [Unicook Grill Cover 60 Inch](product:unicook-grill-cover-60-inch) are built to span multiple brands and fit well on most standard cabinets.
### What's the difference between the Performance and Basic Char-Broil cover?

The [Char-Broil 3-4 Burner Performance Grill Cover](product:char-broil-3-4-burner) is built with heavier construction and more durable material than the [Char-Broil 3-4 Burner Large Basic Grill Cover](product:char-broil-3-4-burner-2). Both fit the same grill footprint and carry the same brand-matching advantage for Char-Broil owners. The Basic is a solid option for protected patios or mild climates; the Performance is worth the upgrade if your grill is exposed year-round.
### My grill is 60 inches wide , which cover do I need?

At 60 inches, you're at the upper edge of what most standard 3, 4 burner covers accommodate. The [Performance Cover for Char-Broil 3-4 Burner Gas Grill](product:performance-cover-for-char-broil) is rated for grills and smokers up to 62 inches wide and is the right choice for cabinets in that range. Forcing a shorter cover over a 60-inch grill creates stress at the seams and a poor seal , sizing up is always the right call when you're near the edge.
### How do I keep my grill cover from blowing off in wind?

Look for covers with drawstrings, toggle straps, or elastic hems rather than a simple slip-on design. A cover that can be cinched down at the base is significantly more wind-stable than one that relies on weight alone. If your current cover lacks these features, placing a bungee cord around the lower hem at the grill's base handles most wind situations without requiring a replacement purchase.
### How long should a quality grill cover last?

A well-constructed heavy-duty cover used consistently should last three to five years in most climates. UV exposure is the primary driver of degradation , the outer fabric chalks and weakens before the waterproofing fails, which is why fade-resistant options like the [Unicook Grill Cover 60 Inch](product:unicook-grill-cover-60-inch) tend to maintain their integrity longer in high-sun environments. Storing the cover properly when not in use and avoiding folding it when wet extends its lifespan further.
