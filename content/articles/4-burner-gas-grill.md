Four burners means something specific on a gas grill: the ability to cook chicken thighs over direct heat on one side while a beer can bird sits low and slow on the other, or to sear steaks and rest them in the same session without shuffling food around a single flame. If you're looking at [gas grills](/gas-grills/) for a patio setup that handles real meals , not just hot dogs at noon , a four-burner configuration is where versatility actually starts to matter.

The tricky part is that "four burner" describes a layout, not a quality tier. The gap between an entry-level cast iron grill and a name-brand model with engineered grates and a sear zone is significant, and which one serves you better depends almost entirely on what you're cooking and how often.

## What to Look For in a 4-Burner Gas Grill

### BTU Output and Heat Distribution

BTU ratings get thrown around in grill marketing like they're the whole story. They're not. Thirty-four thousand BTUs spread across four burners still averages out to roughly 8,500 BTUs per burner , enough for solid searing on a properly designed grate, but only if the burner tubes are sized correctly and the heat shields distribute evenly. A grill with 40,000 BTUs and poor diffuser design will have hot spots that scorch edges while the center runs cool.

What matters more than the peak BTU number is how the grill manages heat zones. Look for burners that run independently so you can drop one or two to low while the others run high. That's the core of two-zone cooking, and without it, a four-burner layout is mostly theoretical.

### Grate Material and Cooking Performance

Grates are where the heat becomes food. Cast iron holds temperature better than stainless and creates better sear marks, but it requires more maintenance , oil after every use, dry storage in wet climates. Porcelain-enameled cast iron splits the difference: it holds heat like bare cast iron but resists rust and cleans up faster. Stainless steel grates are the lowest-maintenance option but shed heat faster and don't produce the same crust.

For weekend grilling that includes thicker cuts , brisket flat, pork shoulders, spatchcocked birds , cast iron or porcelain-enameled cast iron is the right call. If your grilling is mostly weeknight chicken and vegetables, stainless does the job without the seasoning ritual.

### Build Quality and Frame Construction

A four-burner grill is a bigger piece of equipment than a two-burner. That means more leverage on the frame, more surface area catching weather, and more potential for wobble at the joints over time. Stainless steel construction resists rust but varies in gauge , thin stainless dents and warps in heat cycles. Powder-coated steel is heavier-duty in thicker gauges but more vulnerable to rust if the coating chips near a burner.

Side tables deserve a look beyond "does it have them." Fixed side tables brace the frame; foldable ones add mobility but introduce hinge joints that loosen with use. If the grill stays in one spot, fixed is sturdier. If you move it for storage or to accommodate a crowd, fold-down makes real sense.

### Warming Racks and Secondary Cooking Space

A warming rack above the main grates isn't luxury , it's functional space that changes what you can cook at once. Buns while burgers finish. Corn while steaks rest. Sauced wings held at temperature while you pull together the rest of the plate. On a four-burner grill, where you're likely cooking for six or more, that secondary surface earns its space every session.

Not every grill in this category includes one, and the ones that do vary in how much clearance they allow between the grate and the rack. Too little clearance and the rack just traps heat; enough clearance and it becomes a legitimate second cooking tier. Exploring the full [range of gas grill options](/gas-grills/) before committing to a configuration is worth the time, especially if your cooking regularly involves large mixed loads.

## Buying Guide

### How Many BTUs Do You Actually Need?

The floor for a four-burner gas grill that performs well is around 30,000 BTUs total. Below that, you're fighting to hold searing temperature on a cold day or with a grill that hasn't fully preheated. Above 40,000 BTUs, gains for most backyard cooks are marginal , the limiting factor becomes technique and food placement, not available heat.

What matters more than the number is burner independence. Four burners you can run at four different levels gives you genuine cooking flexibility. Four burners with only two control knobs gives you a four-burner layout with two-zone reality.

### Grate Material: Matching the Material to Your Cooking Style

Porcelain-enameled cast iron is the highest-performing low-maintenance grate option. It holds heat consistently, produces good sear marks, resists rust without regular oiling, and cleans up with a brush and water. For most buyers who want good results without a maintenance program, it's the right default.

Bare cast iron performs at the same level or slightly better on heat retention, but it requires oiling after every use and dry storage in humid climates. If you're disciplined about grill maintenance, bare cast iron is excellent. If the grill sits outside between uses and gets cleaned when you remember, skip it.

### Portability vs. Stability: Which Configuration Fits Your Setup

Fixed-frame grills with solid side tables are more stable and typically more durable at the joints. They belong in one place. If your patio has a dedicated grill area and the grill doesn't need to move for storage or guests, a fixed configuration is the right call.

Folding-table and portable configurations add flexibility at the cost of some rigidity. The hinge points on folding tables are the first thing to loosen on cheaper models. For buyers who move the grill seasonally , into a garage for winter, or between a deck and a driveway , a portable configuration with fold-down tables makes practical sense.

### Warranty and Brand Support

The spread between entry-level brands and established names is widest in the warranty and parts ecosystem. Weber supports its products with replacement parts, customer service, and warranties that actually hold up. Unknown-brand grills are sold as hardware , what you receive is what you have, and parts availability after twelve months is uncertain.

This matters more the heavier you grill. A grill that runs three times a week through a six-month season accumulates wear faster than one used on occasional weekends. For high-frequency grilling, brand support is insurance worth paying for. Browsing the full range of [reviewed gas grills](/gas-grills/) with warranty terms in mind can save a significant headache in year two.

### Warming Racks and Secondary Cooking Capacity

A warming rack changes the rhythm of a cook. The ability to move finished food off direct heat without pulling it from the grill entirely , keeping it warm while you finish the rest of the plate , is a practical advantage that shows up every session you're cooking for more than two or three people.

Four-burner grills cooking for a crowd need that buffer. Not every model in this category includes a warming rack, and the ones that do vary in clearance height between the main grates and the rack surface. More clearance means more usable secondary space. Less clearance means the rack functions more as a heat trap than a holding zone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the Weber Spirit E-425 compare to the Royal Gourmet in day-to-day use?

The Weber Spirit E-425 outperforms the Royal Gourmet on grate quality, sear zone capability, and long-term durability , but the gap in daily cooking results is smaller than the gap in build quality suggests. For weekend grilling of standard cuts, the Royal Gourmet produces solid food. The Weber earns its premium over the long haul: the grates hold up longer, the igniter stays reliable across more seasons, and the sear zone opens up techniques the Royal Gourmet simply can't match.
### Is 34,000 BTUs enough for a four-burner gas grill?

Yes, for most backyard cooking scenarios. At 34,000 BTUs across four burners, you're averaging roughly 8,500 BTUs per burner , sufficient for searing steaks, grilling chicken, and running a two-zone setup. Where lower BTU output shows its limits is on cold days when the grill struggles to hold temperature, or if you're cooking very large cuts that need sustained high heat for an extended session.
### Do I need cast iron grates, or will stainless steel work?
It depends on what you're cooking. Cast iron , bare or porcelain-enameled , holds heat more consistently and produces better sear marks on thicker cuts. Stainless steel grates are easier to maintain and still produce good results for thinner cuts, vegetables, and fish. If your grilling is mostly chicken thighs, burgers, and sausages, stainless is adequate.
### Can I use these grills year-round, or should I store them seasonally?

All of the grills listed here can operate year-round in most climates, but output drops in very cold weather and fuel efficiency decreases with propane below freezing. Stainless and porcelain-enameled construction handles freeze-thaw cycles better than bare steel. A grill cover is more important than seasonal storage for most buyers , protecting the igniter and the burner control valves from moisture extends reliable operation significantly more than moving the grill indoors.
### Which grill in this group is best for someone who grills three or more times per week?

The [Weber Spirit E-425](product:weber-spirit-e-425-liquid) is the right answer for high-frequency grilling. The porcelain-enameled cast-iron grates, engineered sear zone, and Weber's documented parts and warranty support make it the most durable choice under regular use. Entry-level and unknown-brand grills in this category are built for occasional use , components that hold up fine for twenty sessions a year show wear faster under weekly cooking through multiple seasons.
