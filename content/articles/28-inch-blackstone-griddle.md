Finding the right 28 inch Blackstone griddle means cutting through a surprisingly crowded field , several configurations, bundle options, and even a non-Blackstone competitor all competing for the same patio space. The size is popular for good reason: it's large enough to feed a family or a small gathering, compact enough that most patios can handle it, and compatible with the [Griddles](/griddles/) accessories ecosystem that's grown up around the Blackstone platform.

What separates a genuinely useful griddle from one that frustrates you after three cooks is rarely the cooking surface itself. Burner count, hood design, workspace organization, and how the unit manages grease will shape your experience every weekend for years. Those are the factors worth understanding before you pick a model.

## What to Look For in a 28 Inch Flat Top Griddle

### Burner Configuration and Heat Zone Control

A 28-inch cooking surface gives you enough room to run multiple temperature zones , but only if the burner layout supports it. Two-burner models divide the surface roughly in half, which works well for most family cooks: hot side for searing proteins, cooler side for eggs or vegetables. Three-burner configurations give you more granular control, letting you stage food across three distinct heat levels without the zones bleeding into each other.

What matters here isn't just the number of burners but the BTU rating per burner and how evenly that heat distributes across the flat top. A poorly designed burner can leave cold spots at the edges or scorching hot zones in the center, which forces you to shuffle food constantly rather than cook with intention.

If your typical cook involves multiple proteins or feeding more than four people at once, a three-burner model earns its keep. For two to four people with simpler menus, two burners is adequate and the lower complexity has genuine appeal.

### Hood and Weather Protection

Not every 28-inch griddle ships with a hood, and that gap matters more than it looks on a spec sheet. A hood lets you trap heat for thicker proteins , chicken thighs, pork chops , that benefit from ambient heat surrounding them rather than just contact heat from below. It also shields your cooking surface from wind, which can disrupt heat distribution on breezy days in a way that flat top cooking is particularly susceptible to.

An integrated hood is structurally cleaner than an aftermarket cover and stays with the unit regardless of where you set up. If you cook in a region with variable weather, or if you regularly cook anything thicker than a smash burger, a hood isn't optional equipment.

### Surface Material and Seasoning Requirements

The standard Blackstone cold-rolled steel surface requires seasoning , multiple thin coats of oil cooked in before first use, and periodic re-seasoning as the surface wears. This isn't a burden for anyone who cooks regularly, but it is a maintenance commitment that casual users sometimes underestimate.

Ceramic-coated cast iron, which appears on some competitors in this size class, changes that calculus. Cast iron holds heat more aggressively and the ceramic coating reduces the seasoning burden, but it also adds weight and can crack if thermally shocked. Neither surface is universally better , they reflect different priorities. If you want low-maintenance performance, ceramic-coated cast iron has advantages. If you want the traditional griddle experience with maximum control over the surface character, cold-rolled steel is the standard.

### Workspace and Grease Management

Side shelves and grease management aren't glamorous features, but they determine how much you enjoy the actual cooking process. Counter-height side shelves keep your prep surfaces at a comfortable working height , a detail that matters over the course of a long cook. Folding shelves that lock flat make transportation easier if this griddle will move.

Grease management systems route rendered fat away from the cooking surface into a catch cup. Rear-exit systems keep the front of the unit clean and the catch cup accessible without leaning over a hot surface. Front-exit systems are more common on older or simpler designs. If cleanup is your least favorite part of griddle cooking , and it usually is , pay attention to where and how the grease exits the system. Browsing the full range of [flat top griddles](/griddles/) will show you how much grease system design varies across price tiers.

## Buying Guide

### Two Burners or Three?

The honest answer is that two burners handle most home cooks' needs without the added complexity. Side-by-side heat zones , hot and moderate , cover the overwhelming majority of flat top cooking scenarios: breakfast spreads, smash burgers, stir-fried vegetables, fried rice. Two burners also mean two igniter points, two valves, and two potential failure modes rather than three.

Three burners earn their value when you cook for crowds or when your typical session involves multiple proteins at different stages simultaneously. The 2080's three-zone setup genuinely changes how you can sequence a meal. If you regularly cook for six or more, the upgrade is worth it.

### Hood vs. No Hood

A hood increases what the griddle can cook, full stop. Thicker proteins , chicken thighs, bone-in cuts, dense sausages , benefit from the retained ambient heat a hood provides. Wind protection is a secondary benefit that becomes primary depending on where you live and how exposed your outdoor cooking area is.

The cost of a hood is weight, complexity, and slightly more surface to clean. If your cooking is primarily thin-cut or smash-style, a hood adds less. If you want a single outdoor cooking station that handles everything, buy the hood.

### Cold-Rolled Steel vs. Ceramic Cast Iron

Standard Blackstone griddles use cold-rolled steel. It responds quickly to burner adjustments, makes seasoning intuitive, and is simple to repair if the surface gets damaged. The maintenance rhythm , season, cook, clean, re-season as needed , becomes automatic for regular users.

Ceramic-coated cast iron holds heat longer and requires less frequent seasoning, but it adds weight and the coating can chip under rough handling. For a stationary backyard station, cast iron's advantages are real. For anything that moves, cold-rolled steel's lighter weight and durability edge out.

Exploring the broader [griddle category](/griddles/) shows that surface material is one of the most consequential decisions in this product class , more so than BTU ratings or brand.

### Bundle Value vs. Feature Additions

The Omnivore bundle's appeal is immediate usability. The soft cover that comes in the box matters , an unseasoned or improperly covered griddle surface can rust between uses, particularly in humid climates. Getting the cover at purchase rather than sourcing it separately is a practical advantage.

As griddles add features , hoods, shelves, third burners , the bundle's value proposition shifts. At the upper end of the 28-inch lineup, the features themselves define the value and the bundle concept becomes less relevant. Match the purchase to what you'll actually use immediately rather than paying for features you'll grow into over six months.

### Footprint and Storage Reality

A 28-inch griddle requires more outdoor real estate than the cooking surface dimensions suggest. Side shelves add width. The propane tank needs a position. You need working clearance on at least two sides. Before buying the most featured model in the class, measure your intended setup area with those additions accounted for.

Storage matters differently depending on your climate. Covered patios reduce the urgency of indoor storage. Exposed setups in regions with harsh winters mean either a heavy-duty cover investment or finding interior space for a unit that doesn't fold flat. Plan the storage before the purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Blackstone 1517 and the Blackstone 2080 at the 28-inch size?

The primary difference is burner count and feature set. The [Blackstone 1517](product:blackstone-flat-top-gas-grill) runs two burners with a clean, minimal design optimized for portability and simplicity, while the [Blackstone 2080](product:28-inch-blackstone-griddle-2080) adds a third burner, an integrated hood, and side shelves for a more capable station setup. Both cook on the same 28-inch surface. Choose the 1517 for camping or simple family cooking; choose the 2080 when you want maximum control and a permanent patio station.
### Do I need to season a Blackstone griddle before first use?

Yes , all cold-rolled steel Blackstone surfaces require seasoning before cooking on them for the first time. The process involves applying thin coats of high-smoke-point oil and burning them in across multiple rounds until the surface develops a dark, non-stick patina. Skipping this step leads to food sticking and accelerated surface rust. The process takes about 30 to 45 minutes total and needs to be repeated periodically as the surface wears.
### Is the Captiva Designs griddle a good alternative to Blackstone at 28 inches?

For buyers who prioritize surface heat retention, the [Captiva Designs 27-inch griddle](product:captiva-designs-27-in-flat-top-griddle) is a legitimate option , its ceramic-coated cast iron holds heat more aggressively than Blackstone's cold-rolled steel. The trade-off is a significantly smaller accessories ecosystem and less community support compared to Blackstone. If you plan to add accessories over time or want easy access to replacement parts, the Blackstone platform's depth is a real advantage that the Captiva can't match.
### Does a 28-inch griddle have enough cooking surface for a family of six?

A 28-inch surface provides roughly 470 to 500 square inches of usable cooking area, which is enough for a family of six if you cook in batches rather than all at once. Breakfast for six , eggs, bacon, pancakes , is manageable with a two-zone setup. For larger groups or when you want everything ready simultaneously, the three-burner configuration of the [Blackstone 2080](product:28-inch-blackstone-griddle-2080) gives you more staging flexibility across heat zones.
### Can I use a Blackstone 28-inch griddle for camping?

The [Blackstone 1517](product:blackstone-flat-top-gas-grill) is the most practical option in this size for camping , the two-burner design keeps weight and bulk manageable, and the rear grease management system handles cleanup without running water. The 28-inch format is on the larger end for camp cooking and requires a stable, level surface and a standard propane tank. If vehicle camping with a truck bed or trailer, the size is workable. For backpacking or kayak camping, it's too large.
