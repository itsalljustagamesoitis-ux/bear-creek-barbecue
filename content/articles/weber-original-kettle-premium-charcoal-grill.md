The 22-inch Weber kettle is one of those products that shows up on every patio in the neighborhood for a reason , it works, it lasts, and it doesn't require a manual to operate. If you're researching [charcoal grills](/charcoal-grills/) before pulling the trigger on one, the Original Kettle Premium line deserves a serious look. The question most buyers actually face isn't whether the Weber kettle is good. It's which version fits their situation.

That answer depends on a few variables , color preference, whether you want the built-in thermometer, and whether protecting your investment with a quality cover matters to you. This guide covers all five products in the Weber kettle lineup so you can make the call without second-guessing it.

## What to Look For in a Charcoal Kettle Grill

### Cooking Surface and Capacity

The 22-inch cooking grate is the standard to understand before you buy anything else. At 363 square inches of primary cooking surface, you're looking at roughly four to six burgers side-by-side, a spatchcocked chicken with room to spare, or a bone-in pork shoulder set up for indirect heat. That covers most household needs , weekend cookouts, family dinners, the occasional larger gathering. Where it starts feeling tight is if you're regularly feeding more than eight to ten people at once, or if you want to run multiple large cuts simultaneously.

The kettle's round footprint also shapes how you manage your fire. Two-zone cooking , coals banked to one side, food on the other for indirect heat , is straightforward on a 22-inch grate, but it does reduce your direct-heat zone. Know your cooking style before you assume more grate space is automatically better.

### Airflow and Temperature Control

Dampers are the primary temperature control mechanism on a charcoal kettle, and the quality of those dampers matters more than most buyers realize until they've fought a stuck or leaky one on a cheaper grill. The Weber kettle uses a one-touch ash catcher assembly on the bottom and a simple top vent , both operate smoothly and hold their settings under heat. Learning to use them together is what separates a frustrating charcoal experience from a predictable one.

The built-in lid thermometer on the Premium models gives you a rough ambient reading without lifting the lid. It's not a substitute for a probe thermometer placed near your food, but it tells you whether you're in the ballpark for searing, roasting, or low-and-slow work. If you're new to charcoal, that reference point reduces a lot of the guesswork while you're still developing feel for fire management.

### Build Quality and Longevity

Weber's porcelain-enamel bowl and lid are the reason these grills appear on garage sales after fifteen years and still clean up well. The coating resists rust, doesn't absorb grease the way raw steel does, and handles thermal cycling without cracking at normal cooking temperatures. The plated steel grates are not the weak point they are on discount kettles , they hold seasoning and clean easily with a brush.

The legs, ash catcher, and hardware are where off-brand kettles typically cut corners. Weber's aren't flimsy, but they're also not overbuilt , they're engineered to last a normal use cycle rather than a lifetime. Exploring the full range of [charcoal grills](/charcoal-grills/) options will confirm that the Weber kettle hits a durable middle ground that most competitors at a similar tier don't consistently match.

### Cover and Storage Considerations

A grill cover isn't an optional accessory if you're leaving the grill outside year-round. UV exposure fades the enamel finish and dries out gaskets and plastic components faster than temperature swings do. Rain pooling on the lid, if your cover doesn't vent properly, creates the moisture conditions that accelerate rust on the hardware even when the bowl itself is protected.

A fitted cover designed specifically for the 22-inch kettle shape , rather than a generic round cover , sits lower at the sides, stays put in wind, and doesn't trap the condensation that breathable materials are designed to release. This is worth thinking about at purchase time rather than after your first winter.

## Buying Guide

### Standard Kettle vs. Premium , What You're Actually Deciding

The only functional differences between the standard Original Kettle and the Premium are the built-in lid thermometer and the hinged lid holder. Neither changes the grill's core performance. What they change is how much attention the grill requires during a cook. New charcoal cooks benefit from the thermometer as a reference point while they're learning to read fire by vent position and sound rather than temperature readout. Experienced cooks often ignore it. The lid holder is a small convenience that earns its keep across hundreds of cooks. If you're buying your first charcoal grill, start with the Premium.

### Which Color to Choose

Black is the most forgiving finish for a grill that stays outside and gets used regularly. Scuffs, carbon deposits, and cleaning marks disappear against a dark surface in a way they don't on copper or green. If the grill lives on a covered patio and is primarily a feature of a thoughtfully designed outdoor space, the copper and green finishes are worth considering , they're distinctive without being flashy, and they hold up well under normal use. Choose black if you're optimizing for durability of appearance; choose copper or green if you're optimizing for aesthetics and the trade-offs on visible wear don't concern you.

### Sizing: Is 22 Inches Enough?

For most households, yes. The 22-inch grate handles four to six burgers simultaneously on direct heat, a whole chicken or pork butt on indirect, or two racks of ribs standing upright with a rib rack accessory. Where it reaches its limits is large gatherings where you need to turn out food continuously for twelve or more people, or if you regularly cook multiple large cuts at the same time. If that's your typical situation, a larger kettle or a barrel-style grill is worth comparing , the [charcoal grills](/charcoal-grills/) category has options at larger formats. For everyone else, 22 inches is the right size.

### The Case for Buying the Cover at the Same Time

A grill cover purchased separately after a season of outdoor exposure is covering a grill that has already taken UV and moisture damage. Buying the cover at the same time as the grill means the enamel and hardware get protected from the first cook forward. The Weber Premium cover is not expensive relative to the grill, and the fitted design for the 22-inch kettle means it actually stays on and does its job rather than collecting water in a loose drape around the bowl. Treat the cover as part of the grill purchase, not an optional accessory to think about later.

### Charcoal Management: What the Dampers Actually Do

The bottom damper controls oxygen to the fire , open it and the fire burns hotter, close it and it drops. The top damper controls heat and smoke exhaust , running it nearly closed traps heat and concentrates smoke, running it open moves air through more quickly and burns cleaner. Learning to use both together is the skill that separates predictable charcoal cooking from reactive, frustrating temperature chasing. The Weber kettle's dampers are well-machined and hold their position; cheaper grills often have loose dampers that creep open or closed under heat, which makes this learning curve harder. Start with both fully open and work down from there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Weber Original Kettle and the Weber Original Kettle Premium?

The Premium adds two features the standard Kettle lacks: a built-in lid thermometer and a hinged lid holder that keeps the lid off the ground when you're working the fire. The grate size, bowl construction, One-Touch ash system, and dampers are identical between the two models. For new charcoal cooks, the thermometer is a useful reference while building fire management instincts , experienced cooks often find it redundant if they already own a reliable probe thermometer.
### Does the 22-inch Weber kettle have enough cooking space for a family?

For most families of four to six, yes. The 22-inch grate gives you roughly 363 square inches of primary cooking surface , enough for a full spatchcocked chicken, two racks of ribs with a rib rack, or six to eight burgers at once. If you're regularly cooking for ten or more people and need to keep food moving continuously, you may find the grate limiting. For typical weekend family cooking and occasional larger gatherings, 22 inches handles the workload without issue.
### Does it matter which color I choose between the Black, Copper, and Green Premium models?
Performance is identical across all three finishes , the color choice is entirely about aesthetics and how the grill ages visually. Black is the most forgiving finish for wear, scuffs, and carbon marks. The [Copper](product:weber-original-kettle-premium-charcoal-grill-2) and [Green](product:weber-original-kettle-premium-charcoal-grill-3) finishes are more distinctive but show wear more readily. If the grill lives in a visible outdoor space and the finish is part of a deliberate design choice, either non-black option is worth it.
### Do I need to buy the grill cover, or is any cover sufficient?

A cover fitted specifically to the 22-inch kettle shape performs better than a generic round cover. The Weber Premium cover sits snugly around the bowl and doesn't leave the gap at the base that generic covers create , that gap lets wind lift the cover and allows water to pool against the lower bowl. The breathable polyester construction also prevents the moisture buildup inside the cover that fully sealed materials can create. A properly fitted cover like the [Weber Premium 22-Inch Cover](product:weber-premium-22-inch-charcoal-grill) is worth buying alongside the grill rather than as an afterthought.
### Is the built-in thermometer on the Premium accurate enough to rely on?

The lid thermometer reads ambient dome temperature rather than grate-level temperature, so it runs somewhat hotter than what your food is actually experiencing , especially during low-and-slow indirect cooks. It's a useful directional indicator: you can tell whether your fire is climbing, holding, or dropping without lifting the lid and losing heat. For precise cooking, a separate probe thermometer placed near the food is the right tool. Think of the lid thermometer as a quick reference, not a precision instrument, and it will serve you well.
