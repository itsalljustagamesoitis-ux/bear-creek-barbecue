Picking a charcoal BBQ grill with smoker capability means choosing between genuine versatility and a box of compromises , and most buyers figure that out only after the first cook. The right setup handles both searing burgers and running low-and-slow ribs without requiring you to buy two different pieces of equipment. A look through the full range of [charcoal grills](/charcoal-grills/) shows just how wide that spectrum runs.

What separates a capable dual-purpose grill from a frustrating one is airflow control. Temperature management on charcoal is a skill, and the grill either helps you develop it or fights you every step.

## What to Look For in a Charcoal BBQ Grill with Smoker

### Airflow and Damper Design

The dampers , top and bottom , are the throttle of a charcoal grill. They control how much oxygen reaches the coals, which directly determines your cooking temperature. Cheap dampers that stick, warp, or don't seal properly make smoking nearly impossible. You need smooth, precise adjustment and a reasonably airtight fit to hold the 225, 275°F range that low-and-slow cooking demands.

A bottom damper that opens fully for high-heat searing and closes down to a sliver for smoking is the baseline requirement. If a grill can't maintain a stable temperature range across a two-hour cook without constant intervention, it's a searing tool with smoking pretensions , not a genuine dual-purpose grill.

### Lid Fit and Heat Retention

A loose lid bleeds heat and smoke. On a kettle-style grill, the dome lid is what creates the oven environment that makes indirect cooking and smoking work. The lid should seat firmly on the bowl without rocking, and the damper on the lid needs to position correctly for chimney draw , pulling air through the coals rather than letting it leak around the rim.

Porcelain-enamel construction on the bowl and lid isn't just aesthetic. It holds heat more evenly than bare steel, resists corrosion better, and doesn't interact with smoke flavor the way raw metal can over time.

### Cooking Grate Configuration

For smoking, the ability to set up a two-zone fire is non-negotiable. One side holds charcoal and wood, the other holds the meat. The food cooks indirectly, absorbing smoke without sitting over direct flame. A grill with a single fixed grate and no access to the coals mid-cook makes this harder , you're fighting the setup every time you need to add fuel.

Hinged grates or removable sections that allow charcoal access without disturbing the food are meaningfully better for long cooks. This isn't a luxury feature; it's the difference between a grill you'll actually use for smoking and one you'll avoid.

### Thermometer and Temperature Monitoring

Built-in lid thermometers matter more on a smoker-capable grill than on a pure searing setup. When the lid is down for an hour-plus cook, you want a read on dome temperature without lifting the lid and losing heat. A built-in thermometer won't replace a probe thermometer for meat temperature, but it gives you the ambient temperature signal you need to manage damper position.

That said, most built-in thermometers read dome temperature, which runs 20, 30°F hotter than grate level where the food actually sits. Knowing that offset and accounting for it is part of learning the grill.

### Capacity and Fuel Access

The 22-inch kettle size appears across most of the options worth considering here, and for good reason , it's large enough for a full rack of ribs, a spatchcocked chicken, or a pork shoulder without being unwieldy to manage. If you're regularly cooking for large groups, the 22-inch form factor still works; you just cook in stages.

Fuel access during a long cook is the underrated criterion. A grill that requires you to remove the grate and all the food to add charcoal every 90 minutes is genuinely tiring over a six-hour cook. Looking through the broader category of [charcoal BBQ options](/charcoal-grills/) before committing, with fuel access specifically in mind, is worth the time.

## Buying Guide

### Smoking vs. Grilling Ratio

Before buying, be honest about what percentage of your cooks will be long smoking sessions versus quick searing. If you're planning to smoke once a month and grill three times a week, the feature set that matters most is everyday cooking performance, with smoking as a secondary capability. If you're buying primarily to smoke and grilling is the occasional use, the access to coals mid-cook and built-in thermometer become your primary criteria rather than nice-to-haves.

The kettle form factor handles both well, but it handles everyday grilling slightly better than purpose-built smokers handle everyday grilling. That's actually a reasonable trade for most suburban cooks.

### Charcoal Access During Long Cooks

A six-hour pork shoulder requires fuel additions , charcoal doesn't last indefinitely. On a standard Kettle grill, adding charcoal means moving the grate or using the hinged sections carefully. The Master-Touch's removable center section is a genuine advantage here. If most of your smoking is under three hours , ribs, chicken thighs, fish , this matters less. If you're running briskets or large pork cuts regularly, consider it a primary decision criterion rather than a secondary one.

### Portability as a Factor

Most buyers overweight portability at purchase and underweight it in actual use. The Jumbo Joe is the right answer when portability is genuinely required , camping, tailgating, a house without a permanent outdoor setup. For a fixed patio situation, the standard Kettle height and stability make for a better daily cooking experience. A grill that lives on your patio doesn't benefit from being portable.

The full range of [charcoal grills](/charcoal-grills/) includes purpose-built portable options beyond the Jumbo Joe if size is a constraint , worth checking if your situation is specific.

### The Built-In Thermometer Question

The Premium models include a built-in lid thermometer. The base Kettle does not. For experienced cooks running an external probe thermometer setup, this is irrelevant , they're already monitoring grate-level temperature precisely. For newer charcoal cooks managing a long smoke for the first time, the built-in thermometer reduces one variable and provides a real-time feedback signal without requiring a second piece of equipment.

The counterargument: the built-in thermometer reads dome temperature, not grate temperature. Learning the 20, 30°F offset becomes part of the skill development anyway. The Premium's thermometer is a legitimate convenience, not a substitute for probe thermometry.

### Same-Brand Comparison Trade-offs

All five options here come from Weber, which means the quality baseline is consistent across the board. The meaningful differences are form factor, feature set, and finish , not reliability or build quality variation. Weber's porcelain enamel construction, damper precision, and One-Touch cleaning are consistent across the Kettle line. The decision between models is about which specific features solve your specific cooking situation, not about which manufacturer you trust more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Weber Original Kettle and the Weber Original Kettle Premium?

The Weber Original Kettle Premium adds a built-in lid thermometer to the standard Kettle's feature set , that's the primary functional difference. Both use the same One-Touch cleaning system, the same damper design, and the same 22-inch cooking capacity. For smoking, the built-in thermometer provides continuous dome temperature feedback without lifting the lid, which makes managing a long cook meaningfully easier if you're not already running an external probe setup.
### Can a 22-inch kettle grill actually function as a smoker?

Yes , with the right setup. A two-zone charcoal fire, dampers controlled to maintain 225, 275°F, and wood chunks added to the coals produces genuine low-and-slow smoked results on a 22-inch kettle. The technique requires more attention than a dedicated offset smoker, but a Weber Kettle with proper airflow management can produce competition-quality ribs or chicken. The limitation is capacity , you can't fit a full brisket flat and a pork shoulder simultaneously.
### How does the Weber Master-Touch differ from the Weber Original Kettle Premium?

The Master-Touch includes a hinged grate with a removable center section for mid-cook charcoal access, a tuck-away lid holder, and a slightly elevated feature set compared to the Premium. The Premium has the built-in thermometer; the Master-Touch requires an external thermometer. For long smoking sessions where you need to add charcoal, the [Weber Master-Touch](product:weber-master-touch-charcoal-grill-22) grate access is a practical advantage. For occasional smoking with emphasis on temperature visibility, the Premium is easier to manage.
### Is the Weber Jumbo Joe suitable as a primary home grill?

It can serve as a primary grill for one or two people with limited outdoor space or no fixed patio setup. The 22-inch cooking surface is adequate, and the Weber build quality is identical to the full Kettle line. The trade-off is working height , the Jumbo Joe sits lower than a standard Kettle on legs, which makes longer cooking sessions physically less comfortable. For a fixed home setup with regular cooking, the standard Kettle models provide a better daily experience.
### Which Weber kettle grill is best for someone new to charcoal smoking?

The [Weber Original Kettle Premium in black](product:weber-original-kettle-premium-charcoal-grill) is the strongest starting point. The built-in thermometer reduces the monitoring variables during the learning curve, the One-Touch cleaning system lowers the barrier to regular use, and the standard Kettle form factor has the most instructional content available online for new charcoal cooks. Starting with a grill that gives you real-time temperature feedback lets you focus on fire management technique rather than guessing what's happening inside the dome.
