## What to Look For in a Smoke Vault Cover or Propane Smoker Setup

### Fit and Model Compatibility

A cover that doesn't fit precisely is worse than no cover at all. Loose fabric pools water at the base, channels moisture toward seams and burner hardware, and eventually creates the exact corrosion problem you were trying to prevent. Camp Chef's Smoke Vault line comes in two primary footprints , the 18-inch and the 24-inch , and covers designed for one will not properly protect the other.

Before buying any cover, confirm the exact model number on your smoker. The SMV18 and SMV24 designations are stamped or labeled on the unit itself. Aftermarket covers typically list compatible models explicitly; if a product listing doesn't name the specific Smoke Vault variant it fits, treat that as a red flag. Precision fit pays for itself in protection.

### Material and Waterproofing

Not all outdoor covers use the same fabric construction. The difference between a cover that degrades in a season and one that lasts five years often comes down to two things: the denier count of the outer shell and the quality of the waterproof coating or laminate bonded to the interior. Higher denier means a heavier, more puncture-resistant weave. A polyurethane or PVC-backed interior layer keeps water from seeping through the fabric itself.

UV resistance matters equally. Sunlight breaks down cover fabric faster than rain does, especially in climates with hot summers. Look for covers that specify UV-stabilized material rather than assuming any outdoor cover handles sun exposure adequately.

### Ventilation and Condensation Management

A fully sealed cover traps moisture inside rather than keeping it out. This is a counterintuitive failure mode , the smoker gets wetter from condensation than it would from light rain. Quality covers include ventilation points, often at the top, that allow air movement while the exterior sheds water.

If you're storing your smoker through a wet season, the ventilation design matters more than any other single feature. A cover without vents can accelerate rust on burner components and door hinges even when the exterior stays dry. This is worth checking explicitly in product descriptions before purchasing.

### Capacity and Rack Configuration for Complete Units

If you're buying a propane smoker rather than a cover, the rack count and configuration determine what you can realistically cook at one time. A three-rack vertical unit handles a full brisket and two pork shoulders simultaneously, which covers most backyard cooking scenarios. Rack spacing matters too , tight spacing limits the cuts you can work with, regardless of total square inches.

Vertical propane smokers concentrate heat at the bottom, which means temperature varies somewhat between the lowest and highest rack positions. This isn't a flaw so much as a characteristic you manage by rotating positions during a long cook. Understanding how the unit handles heat distribution helps you plan accordingly. For anyone evaluating the full category of [vertical propane smoker options](/propane-smokers/), rack configuration is consistently one of the top decision points.

## Buying Guide

### First-Party vs. Third-Party Covers

The strongest argument for buying a Camp Chef-branded cover is fit certainty. The manufacturer designed both the smoker and the cover, so the dimensions are verified rather than approximated. For a product whose entire job is precise coverage, that verification has real value.

Third-party covers trade some of that certainty for price flexibility. Stanbroil has earned a reasonable reputation in the cover category by focusing specifically on model compatibility , their products are defined by the units they fit. Generic unbranded covers require more due diligence on the buyer's part to confirm that stated dimensions actually match the smoker's footprint.

### Understanding Your Smoke Vault Model Number

The Camp Chef Smoke Vault line uses a straightforward naming convention: SMV followed by the width in inches. The SMV18 is the 18-inch unit; the SMV24 family includes the SMV24S and SMV24B variants. Covers are not interchangeable between these sizes.

Check your smoker's label before purchasing. The model number is typically on a sticker near the burner assembly or on the back panel. Ordering a cover based on memory or assumption is the most common source of returns in this category. Two minutes with the label saves a week of shipping.

### Cover Material: What Actually Matters in Practice

Outdoor cover marketing overloads on specification language. The two numbers that actually predict durability are denier (the weight of the fabric weave) and the waterproofing rating. Anything marketed as waterproof without a rating or coating specification is often water-resistant at best , a meaningful difference after a heavy rain.

For smokers stored outdoors year-round, UV resistance is the second critical factor. A cover that repels water but degrades in sunlight becomes brittle and cracks at the seams within a season or two. Both properties should be present. For a deeper look at how cover quality factors into the total cost of owning outdoor cooking equipment, the [propane smoker buying guides](/propane-smokers/) at this hub cover material durability in more detail.

### When Buying a Complete Unit Makes More Sense

A bundled propane smoker with an included cover makes more financial and practical sense than buying a smoker and cover separately in two separate transactions. The cover is sized and designed for the unit it ships with, and the total package cost is typically lower than the sum of independent purchases.

The case for buying a cover-only product is strongest when you already own a Smoke Vault and need replacement coverage. If the original cover wore out, a precise-fit replacement extends the smoker's usable life at a fraction of the cost of a new unit.

### Propane Logistics for Vertical Smokers

Vertical propane smokers consume fuel at a rate that varies with ambient temperature and cook duration. A long brisket cook on a cold morning burns through a tank faster than a summer afternoon chicken cook. Most backyard cooks keep two tanks on hand and swap mid-cook rather than risk running dry.

The propane requirement also means planning around fuel availability. For weekend cooks that happen spontaneously, having a full backup tank removes one potential source of frustration. This is less of a concern in suburban areas where exchange tanks are accessible, but it's a logistics variable worth building into your routine from the start.

### Do Camp Chef Smoke Vault covers fit all Smoke Vault models?
No. Camp Chef's Smoke Vault covers are model-specific. The PC18 cover is sized for the SMV18, and covers designed for the SMV24S and SMV24B are not interchangeable with the 18-inch unit. Confirm your model number before purchasing , the designation is labeled on the smoker itself.
### Is the Stanbroil cover compatible with both the SMV24S and the SMV24B?

Yes. The [Stanbroil Patio Cover for Camp Chef 24" Smoke Vault](product:stanbroil-patio-cover-for-camp-chef) is listed as compatible with both the SMV24S and SMV24B variants, which are the same footprint with different door and finish configurations. Stanbroil's product line is organized around model-specific compatibility, so the dimensions account for the handle and hardware placement on those specific units.
### How do I manage temperature variation between racks in a vertical propane smoker?

Vertical smokers run hotter near the burner at the bottom and cooler toward the top. The practical fix is rotating rack positions midway through a long cook , move the bottom rack to the top position and vice versa. It adds five minutes of attention during a multi-hour cook and largely equalizes the finished result across whatever you're running simultaneously.
### What should I look for in a waterproof smoker cover to ensure it actually keeps water out?

Look for a specified waterproofing treatment , a PVC or polyurethane backing on the interior fabric , rather than a general "waterproof" claim without detail. Seam construction matters as much as fabric: taped or heat-welded seams resist water penetration better than stitched seams alone.
