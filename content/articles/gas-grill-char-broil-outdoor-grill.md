Choosing a Char-Broil gas grill is a more specific decision than it might first appear. The brand covers everything from compact single-burner portables to full-size four-burner cabinet grills, and the right choice depends on how you cook, where you cook, and how often you move. If you're browsing [gas grills](/gas-grills/) and keep landing on Char-Broil, there's a reason , the brand offers genuine range at multiple price bands without making you sort through a dozen manufacturers.

The gap between a good grill and a frustrating one usually comes down to heat management and cooking surface, not brand recognition. Understanding those factors first makes the product comparisons below much easier to follow.

## What to Look For in a Char-Broil Gas Grill

### Heat Technology: Infrared vs. Convective vs. Standard

Char-Broil sells grills across three distinct heat delivery systems, and the difference is meaningful in practice. Standard gas grills use open burners that heat air, which heats the cooking grate. That works, but it's susceptible to flare-ups and uneven hot spots across the surface.

Infrared systems , used in Char-Broil's higher-end lines , place a radiant emitter between the flame and the grate. That emitter absorbs heat and radiates it evenly across the cooking surface, which reduces flare-ups significantly and cuts preheat time. The trade-off is that infrared runs hotter by default, which takes some adjustment if you're used to conventional gas.

Convective systems sit in the middle. They use airflow patterns inside the grill body to distribute heat more evenly than open burners without requiring the emitter hardware of true infrared. For most casual cooks, convective heating is a practical improvement over standard systems with less learning curve than infrared.

### Burner Count and Cooking Zones

A single burner means a single heat zone. You can control temperature across the grill, but you cannot run a hot sear zone alongside a low indirect zone at the same time. For anyone cooking proteins and vegetables simultaneously , or trying to do a reverse sear without equipment gymnastics , a single burner is a real constraint.

Four burners change what's possible. You can run two burners at high and two at low, put a piece of meat over the cool side to finish slowly, and have a sear station ready when it's time to crust the exterior. That's not theoretical; it's the practical reason experienced cooks gravitate toward multi-burner configurations when the cooking surface allows it.

For portables, single-burner limitations are usually accepted as part of the format. The question to ask is whether you're buying portable because of genuine mobility needs, or buying portable because of price. Those are different problems with different right answers.

### Cooking Surface Area

Cooking surface is measured in square inches, and the numbers can be misleading if you're not careful. Manufacturers often list total surface area, which includes upper warming racks. The primary grate , where food actually sits over direct heat , is the number that matters for capacity planning.

A full four-burner cabinet grill typically delivers 450, 500 square inches of primary cooking surface, enough for a family cookout without juggling batches. A compact portable runs closer to 150, 200 square inches, which is sufficient for two to four people if you're not cooking sides simultaneously.

Overestimating how much grill you need creates storage and portability problems. Underestimating means you're cooking in shifts, which defeats the point of an outdoor grill for any group larger than two.

### Portability vs. Permanence

If you cook exclusively in one backyard, a cabinet-style grill with storage, a side burner, and a larger footprint is almost always worth it over a portable. The side burner alone adds meaningful versatility , sauce work, boiling corn, warming buns without crowding the main grate.

Portable grills earn their place for tailgating, camping, and apartments with balcony restrictions. They're also sensible for anyone who travels for work and wants a reliable outdoor cooking option that fits in a truck bed or cargo area. The full range of [gas grill options](/gas-grills/) spans both ends of this spectrum, but it's worth deciding which category you're actually shopping in before comparing features across the two.

## Buying Guide

### Propane as a Fuel System

All three grills in this comparison run on propane, which is worth understanding before purchase rather than after. Propane delivers on-demand heat with no warmup period, performs well in cold weather better than natural gas at low pressure, and is available at virtually every hardware store and many grocery chains. That accessibility makes it practical for portables especially , a 1-pound disposable cylinder runs a small grill for several hours without needing a home tank connection.

The management requirement is the trade-off. You need to track fuel level, store tanks safely, and decide whether you're running a 20-pound tank through a hose or using disposable cylinders. For stationary cabinet grills, a 20-pound tank with a gauge is almost always the better system. For portables, the disposable cylinder convenience is part of what makes the format work.

### Matching Grill Size to Your Actual Cooking Habits

The most common buying mistake is sizing up for a scenario that rarely happens. A four-burner cabinet grill is genuinely useful for a family that cooks outside regularly, hosts cookouts, or runs longer weekend sessions. For someone who grills twice a month for two people, it's storage space and maintenance overhead that doesn't pay off.

Be honest about frequency before deciding on size. A portable used consistently twice a week will deliver more value than a full-size grill used six times in a summer. The right answer is the grill that matches your actual cooking cadence, not your aspirational one.

### Infrared vs. Convective: Choosing the Right Heat System

The heat technology in a Char-Broil grill is a real differentiator, not just marketing language. Infrared, as found in the Pro Series, produces higher, more even heat with reduced flare-up risk , it rewards cooks who want restaurant-style searing and don't mind a learning curve on temperature feel. Convective, found in the portable unit, is more forgiving and more familiar to anyone who's used a standard gas grill before.

If you're new to gas grilling or primarily cook for convenience rather than technique development, the convective system is easier to work with out of the box. If you're already comfortable managing heat zones and want to improve your sear quality, infrared is the technology worth paying into. You can find both systems across the range of [outdoor gas grill options](/gas-grills/) at different price bands.

### Cabinet Grills vs. Open-Cart vs. Portable Format

The physical format of a grill shapes the cooking experience more than buyers typically anticipate. Cabinet-style grills offer enclosed storage, better weather protection for the interior components, and usually more primary cooking surface. Open-cart models sacrifice some storage for a lower price and a lighter footprint. Portables sacrifice cooking capacity and zone flexibility for mobility.

The side burner on a cabinet grill is a feature that sounds minor until you've spent a season without one. Having a dedicated burner for sauce, corn, or prepping a compound butter without crowding the main grate changes what's possible in a single outdoor cook session.

### Assembly and Long-Term Maintenance

Char-Broil grills at all levels require assembly out of the box. The Pro Series four-burner will take longer and require more attention than the portable single-burner models , factor that into your purchase timing if you're buying for a specific event. Plan for an hour to two hours for a full cabinet grill, less for the portables.

Long-term, the maintenance routine for a propane grill is manageable: brush the grates after each cook, check the burner tubes periodically for blockage, and cover the grill when not in use. The infrared emitter on the Pro Series is one additional component to inspect occasionally , food debris trapped in the emitter affects heat distribution over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Char-Broil Pro Series four-burner worth it over a portable for backyard use?

For anyone cooking at home regularly, yes. The four-burner Pro Series gives you cooking zone flexibility, a substantially larger primary surface, and a cabinet with a side burner , features that make a real difference in weekly use. The portables make sense when mobility is the actual requirement. If your grill is going to live in one spot more than three-quarters of the time, the Pro Series is the right investment.
### Can I use disposable propane cylinders with the portable Char-Broil grills?

The portable models are designed to accept standard 1-pound disposable propane cylinders, which is one of the main practical advantages of the format. A 1-pound cylinder runs a small portable for approximately one to two hours of cooking time, making it practical for a cookout without a large tank. For extended sessions, connecting a larger tank via an adapter hose is possible on most portable models , confirm compatibility before purchasing the adapter.
### How does Amplifire infrared heat differ from a standard gas burner in practice?

Infrared heat radiates from an emitter rather than heating the air around the food. The result is a faster sear, reduced flare-ups from fat dripping onto open flames, and more even surface temperature across the grate. The adjustment for new infrared users is that the system runs hotter than it feels , standard gas grill instincts for "medium" don't transfer directly. Give yourself a cook or two to calibrate before expecting the same results you'd get from a conventional burner.
### Which Char-Broil grill is best for tailgating?

The [Charbroil Portable Convective 1-Burner Propane Gas Grill](product:charbroil-portable-convective-1-burner) is the stronger choice for tailgating between the two portables, primarily because the convective heat distribution handles varied loads on a single burner more evenly than a standard open burner. Both portables will fit in a vehicle easily and run on disposable cylinders. If you're cooking for a larger tailgate crowd, neither portable fully substitutes for a full-size grill , but the convective model is the better of the two for the format.
### What maintenance does a Char-Broil propane grill require between seasons?

Before storing for winter or pulling out after a long break, check the burner tubes for spider webs and debris blockage , this is the most common cause of uneven ignition on gas grills that have sat unused. Clean the grates thoroughly, inspect the igniter battery if applicable, and run a visual check on the propane hose for cracks or brittleness. A grill cover extends the life of the exterior finish significantly. For the Pro Series infrared emitter specifically, clean the emitter surface of built-up residue before the first cook of the season.
