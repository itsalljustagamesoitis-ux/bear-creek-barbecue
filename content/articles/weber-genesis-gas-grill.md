The Weber Genesis line has defined what a serious backyard gas grill looks like for decades, and the current lineup is the best it's ever been. If you're sorting through burner counts, fuel types, and finish options across five different models, this guide cuts through the noise and tells you which one fits your situation. For broader context on what separates a capable gas grill from a frustrating one, the [gas grills](/gas-grills/) hub is worth a read before you commit.

The Genesis lineup isn't one-size-fits-all. The differences between models matter more than the marketing suggests, and buying the wrong configuration is an expensive mistake to correct.

## What to Look For in a Weber Genesis Gas Grill

### Burner Count and Cooking Zones

Three burners or four , that's the first decision, and it's more consequential than it sounds. A three-burner Genesis gives you indirect and direct heat, which covers most backyard cooking scenarios competently. Where the three-burner setup starts to show its limits is when you're running multiple proteins at different temperatures simultaneously, or when you need a large dedicated indirect zone while still maintaining a screaming-hot sear side.

Four burners don't just add cooking surface , they give you more zone granularity. You can run a low-and-slow section on the left, a medium zone in the middle, and high heat on the right, all at once. For someone cooking for a crowd regularly, that flexibility is the whole argument for the step up.

Most weekend cooks don't need four burners. Most cooks who think they don't need four burners eventually wish they had them.

### Fuel Type: Propane vs. Natural Gas

This decision is largely determined by your setup, not your preference. If your patio has a natural gas line or you're willing to have one run, the natural gas models eliminate the tank-refill routine entirely. That convenience compounds over time , no driving to the exchange, no mid-cook discoveries that the tank is empty, no off-season storage logistics.

Propane is the right call if you don't have a gas line and don't want to deal with permanent installation. It's also more portable in theory, though a Genesis isn't a grill you're moving around the patio frequently. The BTU output and cooking performance between propane and natural gas Genesis models is functionally identical.

The natural gas conversion, once done, is permanent. Make sure you're not planning to move before you commit to a fixed-line installation.

### Grate Material and Sear Performance

Every Genesis in this lineup ships with either porcelain-enameled cast-iron grates or stainless steel grates, and the choice affects daily cooking more than most buyers anticipate. Cast-iron grates retain heat more aggressively, which produces better sear marks and better crust development on steaks and chops. The tradeoff is maintenance , they need to be kept seasoned and dry to prevent rust under the enamel coating.

Stainless steel grates are lower maintenance and resist corrosion more reliably in humid climates or if you're not religious about cover usage. They heat quickly but shed heat faster, which matters if you're searing thick cuts. For most grilling tasks , chicken, vegetables, burgers , the difference is marginal.

### The Sear Zone Feature

The E-325 models include a dedicated sear zone that the E-315 omits. This is a higher-BTU burner section designed to produce restaurant-level crust development on steaks. Whether it matters depends on how often you're cooking protein that genuinely benefits from intense, rapid searing.

If most of your grilling is burgers and chicken thighs, the sear zone is a feature you'll use occasionally and appreciate when you do. If you're regularly cooking thick ribeyes or putting a crust on a butterflied leg of lamb, it moves from nice-to-have to a genuine differentiator. The full range of [gas grill options](/gas-grills/) worth considering covers this feature across multiple brands if the sear zone is your primary decision criterion.

### Finish and Weather Resistance

The E-series Genesis models use porcelain-enameled steel for the lid and firebox. The S-415 steps up to stainless steel construction, which handles UV exposure, humidity, and temperature cycling more gracefully over a long ownership period. Porcelain enamel is durable, but it can chip if the grill is bumped hard , once chipped, the exposed steel underneath is vulnerable to surface rust.

If you store your grill under a cover year-round and aren't rough with it, the E-series finish holds up well. If the grill lives outdoors exposed to weather, or you're in a coastal climate with salt air, the stainless construction of the S-415 earns its premium over time.

## Buying Guide

### Matching Burner Count to Your Cooking Frequency

The three-burner versus four-burner question deserves honest self-assessment before you commit. Count how many people you regularly cook for , not your aspirational dinner party, your actual Saturday night. If the answer is four to six people, a three-burner Genesis handles that comfortably. If you're regularly feeding eight or more, the four-burner layout gives you the surface area and zone control to do it without running two consecutive batches.

Burner count also affects how you cook, not just how much. Four zones let you manage indirect cooking more precisely. Three zones work well but require more active management when running complex cooks.

### Fuel Infrastructure First

Before comparing any other feature, confirm your fuel situation. Propane requires storage, refills, and the discipline to check tank levels before you fire up. Natural gas requires an existing line or a professional installation , which has a real cost and commits the grill to a fixed location.

Neither is wrong. Natural gas wins on convenience; propane wins on flexibility. The mistake is buying a natural gas grill when you don't have a line, or buying propane when a perfectly good natural gas connection is already at your patio. The rest of the buying decision flows from this one.

### Grate Material and Maintenance Commitment

Cast-iron grates , the standard on E-series Genesis models , perform excellently but ask something of the owner. After each cook, brush them clean. Before storage, apply a thin coat of oil. Keep the grill covered. Do those things, and cast-iron grates last for years and sear better than most alternatives at this price level.

Stainless grates, as on the S-415, are more forgiving. They don't rust, they clean easily, and they don't require seasoning. The tradeoff is that they release heat faster, which slightly blunts sear performance on thick cuts. For most cooking tasks the difference is minor, but it's real.

### Finish and Long-Term Durability

The E-series porcelain enamel finish is durable under normal conditions. It handles heat cycles, cleaning, and reasonable weather exposure without issue. Where it fails is chipping , a hard impact on the lid or firebox can break the enamel seal, and the exposed steel underneath will rust.

The S-415's stainless construction doesn't have that failure mode. Stainless takes impacts, salt air, and weather cycling without developing rust. If you're in a coastal environment or your grill lives outside without consistent cover use, that matters significantly over a five-to-ten year ownership period. Buyers browsing the full [gas grill category](/gas-grills/) will find this finish question comes up across multiple brands , it's a universal durability consideration, not a Genesis-specific one.

### Sear Zone: Feature or Noise?

The sear zone on the E-325 models adds a higher-BTU output section that generates more intense surface heat than a standard burner at full throttle. It produces better crust formation on thick steaks and dense proteins. If that's a regular part of your cooking, it's worth having.

If your grill rotation is primarily burgers, chicken, sausages, and vegetables, a standard burner at high heat covers those tasks without the dedicated sear zone. Be honest about what you actually cook before treating the sear zone as a mandatory feature. The E-315 omits it and loses nothing for the majority of backyard cooks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Weber Genesis E-325 and E-315?

The E-325 adds a dedicated sear zone that the E-315 doesn't include. Both grills have three burners, porcelain-enameled cast-iron grates, and the same fundamental Genesis build quality. The sear zone produces higher surface heat for crust development on thick steaks and dense proteins. If you regularly sear thick cuts, the E-325 is the right choice; if your cooking skews toward chicken, burgers, and vegetables, the E-315 covers it completely.
### Should I buy the propane or natural gas version of the Weber Genesis E-325?

The decision comes down to your patio infrastructure. If you have a natural gas line or plan to have one installed, the natural gas model eliminates tank refills and mid-cook fuel anxiety permanently. If you don't have a line and don't want to deal with installation costs or the fixed location commitment, the propane version is the practical choice. Cooking performance between the two versions is functionally identical.
### Is the Weber Genesis S-415 worth the upgrade over the E-415?

For most buyers, the upgrade case rests on two factors: stainless steel construction and climate. The S-415's stainless finish and grates handle outdoor exposure, humidity, and temperature cycling more durably over time than the E-415's porcelain enamel. If your grill lives outside in a harsh or coastal environment without consistent cover use, the stainless construction earns its premium. If your patio is covered and you maintain the grill consistently, the E-415 delivers four-burner performance at a more accessible level.
### How many burners do I actually need for weekend family grilling?

For a family of four to six people cooking on weekends, three burners handle the workload without issue. A three-burner Genesis gives you genuine indirect and direct zones, enough surface area for a full meal, and the temperature control to manage different proteins simultaneously. The four-burner models are worth considering if you regularly host larger groups , eight or more people , or if you frequently run complex cooks that require more distinct heat zones running simultaneously.
### Can I convert a Weber Genesis propane grill to natural gas?
Weber does not support field conversion between fuel types on Genesis models. The propane and natural gas versions are separate products with different regulators, orifices, and valve configurations. Attempting a DIY conversion is unsafe and voids the warranty. If you want natural gas, purchase the natural gas model from the start , the [Weber Genesis E-325 Natural Gas](product:weber-genesis-e-325-natural) is available directly.
