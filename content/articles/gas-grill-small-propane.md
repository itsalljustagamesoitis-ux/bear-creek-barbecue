Small propane grills occupy a specific and underserved corner of the [Gas Grills](/gas-grills/) category , compact enough to move, capable enough to cook a real meal, and ready to run without a natural gas line or a backyard full of space. Whether you're cooking on a concrete patio with HOA restrictions, setting up at a tailgate, or just trying to feed two people without firing up a full-sized rig, the right small propane grill does the job without demanding more space or setup than the situation calls for.

The challenge is that this category runs from true one-burner portables to multi-burner setups that blur the line with full-size grills. BTU output, cooking surface, and how the unit actually stores and travels matter a lot more here than they do with a permanent backyard grill.

## What to Look For in a Small Propane Grill

### BTU Output and Cooking Surface

BTU ratings describe how much heat a burner can produce, but the number only means something relative to the cooking surface it's working with. A single 10,000 BTU burner covering 150 square inches will cook hotter than 20,000 BTU spread across 400 square inches. What you're actually looking for is whether the grill can reach searing temperature , roughly 450, 500°F at the grate , within a reasonable preheat time.

For a small propane grill, that usually means a minimum of 8,000, 10,000 BTU per burner. Two-burner models in this category typically run 15,000, 20,000 BTU combined, which is more than adequate for cooking burgers, chicken, or fish for two to four people. Three-burner models push past that and start to offer genuine zone cooking.

Don't let a high aggregate BTU number distract you from asking whether the grill can hold a steady temperature once it gets there. Consistency matters more than peak output.

### Portability vs. Stability Trade-off

Tabletop grills are genuinely portable , they fold flat or pack small, and you can put them in a truck bed without thinking twice. The trade-off is that you need a stable surface to cook on, and you're working lower than you would with a standing grill. Leaning over a tabletop grill for thirty minutes while you manage a flare-up gets old quickly.

Grills with foldable legs solve part of this problem. They set up at a workable height and store compactly. The structural trade-off is that folding legs are lighter by necessity, which means the grill may shift on uneven ground if you're not careful about placement.

Full-size portable grills , models with a true cart or fixed legs , offer the best stability but sacrifice the easy-transport advantage. If you're buying primarily for a fixed outdoor space, that's a reasonable trade. If you actually need to move the grill regularly, weight and packed dimensions matter as much as cooking specs.

### Burner Configuration and Zone Cooking

Single-burner grills are simple and lightweight, but they cook everything at the same temperature. That works fine for burgers and sausages , things that want consistent direct heat throughout. It doesn't work well for thicker cuts that benefit from searing over high heat and then finishing over a cooler zone.

Two burners change the math entirely. You can run one side hot and one side moderate, which lets you build a crude two-zone setup on a small cooking surface. That's genuinely useful for bone-in chicken thighs, thick pork chops, or anything you'd otherwise be fighting against over a single flame.

Three-burner models in this size category push into territory that starts to resemble a small full-size grill. The zone control is meaningful, and the total cooking area typically crosses 300 square inches , enough for a family meal rather than just a quick cook for two. For a broader look at how these configurations fit into the full landscape, the [gas grill category](/gas-grills/) is worth exploring before you commit.

### Build Quality and Weather Resistance

Stainless steel grates and exteriors hold up better than coated alternatives over time, especially if you're leaving the grill outside between uses or using it near salt air. The caveat is that stainless steel requires cleaning and occasional maintenance , it spots, and surface rust will develop if standing water is left on it after rain.

Cast iron grates retain heat better than stainless and produce more pronounced sear marks, but they require seasoning and careful drying to prevent rust. Porcelain-coated grates split the difference , easier care, decent heat retention , but the coating can chip if you're rough with the cleaning brush.

For a grill that travels, lighter materials make sense. For a grill that sits in one place, prioritize grate material and weather-resistant construction over packed weight.

## Buying Guide

### How Many Burners Do You Actually Need?

The honest answer for most buyers is two. A single burner is enough for simple, direct-heat cooking , hot dogs, brats, thin burgers , but it removes zone cooking entirely. Two burners let you create a hot side and a cooler side, which covers the full range of what most backyard cooks actually make. Three or four burners add real capability for zone management and indirect cooking, but they also add size, weight, and cost. If you're feeding two to four people and your menu stays mostly in the direct-heat lane, two burners is the practical floor.

### Propane Tank Size and Run Time

Small propane grills typically run on one-pound disposable cylinders or connect to a standard 20-pound tank via an adapter hose. Disposable cylinders are genuinely convenient for camping and travel , no adapter, no dragging a full tank. The downside is that they run out faster than you expect. A 20,000 BTU two-burner grill burns through a one-pound cylinder in roughly an hour at full output.

For backyard use, a standard 20-pound tank is the better answer. It runs for many hours, costs less per BTU, and removes the anxiety of watching the flame get weaker mid-cook. Most of the grills on this list accept a standard regulator hose, though you may need to purchase the adapter separately for models designed around disposable cylinders.

### Cooking Surface vs. Portability

This is the central trade-off in the small propane grill category, and it's worth being direct about it before you buy. A grill that packs small and moves easily will have a smaller cooking surface than one that stays in one place. You cannot fully optimize both. The tabletop models on this list , the Cuisinart and the Charbroil , prioritize portability and accept a smaller grate area. The Royal Gourmet PD1305H and the Monument Mesa400MF prioritize cooking capacity and sacrifice some packability. Decide which matters more to your actual use case before comparing specs.

### Where and How You'll Actually Use It

Be honest about this. A grill that needs to go in a truck bed every weekend has different requirements than one that sits on a concrete patio and moves twice a year. For genuine portability , camping, tailgates, picnics , weight and packed dimensions matter as much as BTU output. For a compact backyard setup, durability and cooking surface matter more. The broader [gas grill landscape](/gas-grills/) includes options that don't require portability trade-offs at all, and it's worth a look if you're on the fence about whether a small portable is actually what your situation calls for.

### Lid and Covered Cooking

Not every small propane grill has a lid, and that matters more than buyers often realize before their first cook. Without a lid, you can't trap heat for covered cooking, which rules out whole chicken, bone-in thighs, thick steaks, or anything that benefits from circulating convective heat. Burgers and brats don't care. Most other things do. If your cooking goes beyond simple direct-heat grilling, buy a grill with a functional lid , not just a wind guard, but a lid that latches and seals well enough to hold temperature.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What size propane tank do small portable grills use?

Most small portable propane grills connect to either one-pound disposable cylinders or standard 20-pound tanks via an adapter hose. Disposable cylinders are convenient for camping but burn through quickly on two-burner models running at full output. For backyard use, a 20-pound tank is more economical and extends your cook time significantly. Many grills designed for disposable cylinders accept a compatible adapter hose sold separately.
### Is a two-burner grill enough for a family of four?

For most families cooking burgers, chicken breasts, or vegetables, yes , two burners across a 200, 300 square inch cooking surface handles four servings without crowding. The limitation is zone cooking: you can run one burner high and one moderate, but you don't have the flexibility of a three- or four-burner layout. Bone-in chicken and thicker cuts become more manageable on a three-burner model like the [Royal Gourmet PD1305H](product:royal-gourmet-pd1305h-3-burner).
### Can I use a small propane grill on an apartment balcony?

That depends entirely on your building's rules and local fire codes, which vary significantly. Many apartment buildings prohibit propane grills on balconies outright due to fire risk. Check your lease and local ordinances before buying. If open-flame grills are restricted, electric grills are the typical alternative , propane grills of any size may not be permitted in enclosed or semi-enclosed outdoor spaces.
### What is the difference between the Cuisinart CGG-306 and the Royal Gourmet GT2005?
Both are two-burner tabletop propane grills, but they prioritize differently. The [Cuisinart CGG-306](product:cuisinart-chefs-style-tabletop-portable) leads on build quality , stainless steel construction and a more refined ignition system. The [Royal Gourmet GT2005](product:royal-gourmet-gt2005-2-burner) adds foldable side tables and foldable legs, which gives you a working surface and removes the need for an external table to cook on. If build quality is the priority, the Cuisinart.
### How do I prevent rust on a small propane grill?
Dry the grates and exterior after cleaning , standing water is the primary cause of rust on both stainless steel and raw cast iron grates. Use a grill cover between uses, especially if the grill sits outdoors. For cast iron grates, a light coat of cooking oil before and after use prevents oxidation. Stainless steel is more forgiving but still develops surface rust if neglected.
