Protecting your deck or patio from grease drips, heat, and rogue embers is one of those unglamorous details that separates a good backyard setup from an expensive repair bill. A [grill mat](/tools-and-grates/) sits beneath or on your grates and handles the mess before it becomes a problem , whether you're running a weekend brisket session on composite decking or just want to stop killing the grass under your kettle.

The differences between mats are real and matter: size, material, fire resistance, and intended placement (under the grill versus on the grates) pull the category in completely different directions. Getting that distinction right before you buy will save you from returning a mat that works fine , just not for your situation.

## What to Look For in a Grill Mat

### Under-Grill Mat vs. On-Grate Mat

This is the most important distinction in the category, and it's one the product listings regularly blur. Under-grill mats are large-format floor protectors , fiberglass or rubber-backed materials that sit on your deck or patio surface beneath the grill. They catch grease drips, deflect radiant heat, and protect composite, wood, or grass surfaces from long-term damage. On-grate mats are thin, non-stick PTFE sheets that sit directly on your grill grates and serve as a cooking surface , useful for small vegetables, fish, and anything prone to falling through the grates.

Buying an on-grate mat when you needed a deck protector, or vice versa, is a more common mistake than it should be. Read the dimensions before anything else. A mat listed as 15 x 13 inches is going on your grates; a mat listed as 48 x 36 inches is going on your deck. If you've got a 22-inch kettle on composite decking, you need the latter. If you're trying to grill salmon without losing half of it to the coals, you need the former.

### Size and Coverage

For under-grill protection, bigger is almost always better , up to a point. Your grill footprint isn't just the base; it's the splash radius of a grease splatter or a wind-shifted coal. A mat that barely fits under the grill legs leaves the surrounding deck exposed to exactly the problem you bought it to prevent.

A practical rule: add at least 12 inches to your grill's widest dimension and match that against the mat's listed size. For a standard 22-inch kettle or a mid-size gas grill, a 48 x 30-inch mat covers the base and a reasonable buffer. Larger cabinet-style grills or grills with side tables benefit from 60-inch or longer coverage. Overbuying on size is rarely a problem; underbuying is a recurring one.

### Fire Resistance and Material Construction

Not all fireproof claims are equal. Look for mats that specify fiberglass-reinforced construction or multi-layer composites rather than single-layer felt or basic rubber. Double-sided designs add a layer of utility , if one surface gets stained or damaged, you can flip the mat and extend its useful life.

For rubber-backed mats, the non-slip function is genuine and useful, especially on smooth composite or tile surfaces. The trade-off is that rubber requires more active cleaning than fiberglass to stay grippy , grease bakes into textured rubber over time. Fiberglass-dominant mats wipe cleaner, but may shift on smooth surfaces without a grip layer underneath.

### Ease of Storage

This is the criterion most buyers skip during purchase and regret after. A 65 x 48-inch mat is substantial , it doesn't roll up like a yoga mat and fit in a cabinet. Most large under-grill mats fold rather than roll, and repeated folding on thick or stiff materials creates crease wear at the fold lines.

Before buying a large-format mat, decide where it lives when the grill is covered for winter. If you've got dedicated storage , a shed, a large deck box, a garage wall , the largest sizes are practical. If your storage situation is limited, a more moderately sized mat that you'll actually store and reuse beats a large one that lives under the grill year-round and traps moisture. Browsing the full range of [grill tools and accessories](/tools-and-grates/) before committing to a specific format is worth doing , context about what else you're running alongside the grill sometimes changes the size decision.

## Buying Guide

### Know Which Problem You're Solving First

The grill mat category splits cleanly into two different products solving two different problems, and conflating them leads to returns. Under-grill floor mats protect surfaces , decks, patios, grass , from grease drips, radiant heat, and embers. On-grate cooking mats protect food from sticking and falling through grate gaps. A buyer who needs deck protection and orders on-grate mats has solved nothing; a buyer who orders a 60-inch floor mat expecting a non-stick cooking surface is equally disappointed. Decide which problem you're solving before you look at any product listing.

### Matching Mat Size to Grill Footprint

For under-grill floor protection, size selection follows a simple principle: measure the grill's total footprint including side tables, then add a foot of buffer on each active side. A compact kettle on a 22-inch diameter base needs far less mat than a full-size cabinet gas grill with fold-out side tables. The mats in this lineup range from 48 x 30 to 65 x 48 inches, which maps roughly from compact/mid-size grill coverage to large gas grill or pellet smoker coverage. Getting this measurement right before ordering is the single most important pre-purchase step.

### Deck Surface Type and Material Match

Not every mat works equally well on every surface. Rubber-backed mats like the Cuisinart grip smooth surfaces , tile, composite planking, sealed concrete , with genuine stability. Fiberglass-dominant mats without a grip backing can shift on those same surfaces. On grass or gravel, grip matters less and breathability matters more , a mat that traps moisture against soil can damage the lawn it's protecting. Match the mat's construction to your specific surface type rather than defaulting to the largest or cheapest option. The rest of your outdoor [tools and grates setup](/tools-and-grates/) will inform this too , a mat that works alongside a heavy smoker cart behaves differently than one beneath a lightweight portable grill.

### Fire Resistance: Understanding the Claims

Every mat in this category claims some version of fire resistance. What varies is the construction backing those claims. Multi-layer fiberglass composites with double-sided treatment provide meaningful protection against radiant heat and occasional ember contact. Single-layer mats or basic rubber without an insulating layer provide considerably less. For deck protection on wood or composite surfaces , both of which can sustain heat damage at surprisingly low temperatures , the quality of the fire-resistance construction matters more than the presence of the claim on the label. Look for mats that specify construction materials, not just outcomes.

### Storage and Seasonal Use

Large under-grill mats require genuine storage planning, and that planning is worth doing before purchase rather than after. A mat that lives outdoors under the grill year-round traps moisture, invites mold on some materials, and degrades faster than one that gets stored properly between seasons. Folded fiberglass mats develop crease wear at fold lines over time , if you're storing flat, that's less of a concern. Rubber mats stored in cold conditions can stiffen but typically recover at ambient temperature. The practical question: do you have a dedicated storage location large enough for a folded 60 x 48-inch mat? If yes, buy the size you need. If no, buy down a size rather than leaving an expensive mat exposed to the elements indefinitely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a grill mat and an under-grill mat?

A grill mat , in the on-grate sense , is a thin non-stick PTFE sheet that sits on your cooking grates and acts as a cooking surface for small or delicate foods. An under-grill mat is a large-format floor protector that sits beneath the grill on your deck, patio, or grass to catch grease and deflect heat. The [Grill Mat Set of 6](product:grill-mat-set-of-6) is the on-grate cooking mat in this lineup; every other product here is an under-grill floor protector. They are not interchangeable.
### What size under-grill mat do I need for a standard gas grill?

Measure your grill's full footprint including any side tables, then add at least 12 inches of buffer on each side where grease and embers are likely to land. Most standard mid-size gas grills land comfortably under a 60 x 40-inch mat like the [Protebox 60 x 40 Inch](product:protebox-60-x-40-inch), while compact grills and kettles fit well under a 48 x 30-inch mat. Larger cabinet-style grills or pellet smokers with extended footprints benefit from the 65 x 48-inch format.
### Are these mats safe to use on composite decking?

Most fiberglass and rubber-backed under-grill mats are designed specifically for use on composite decking, which is more heat-sensitive than wood and more expensive to repair or replace. The Cuisinart Premium mat's non-slip rubber construction is particularly well-suited to composite surfaces, providing grip stability and a protective layer against both heat and grease penetration. Always confirm that the mat lies flat without bunching at edges, since gaps allow heat and grease to reach the surface beneath.
### Can I leave an under-grill mat outside year-round?

Technically yes, but it shortens the mat's useful life. Prolonged moisture exposure can degrade adhesion between layers on multi-layer fiberglass mats and promote mold on materials that don't fully dry between uses. Rubber-backed mats tolerate outdoor exposure better than pure fiberglass, but sustained UV exposure fades and stiffens the rubber over time. Bringing the mat indoors or storing it in a covered deck box between grilling seasons extends its life considerably and keeps it performing as intended.
### Do on-grate grill mats work on charcoal grills?

Yes, the [Grill Mat Set of 6](product:grill-mat-set-of-6) is rated compatible with gas, charcoal, and electric grills. The key limitation on charcoal is temperature management , PTFE-coated mats have a safe operating ceiling around 500°F, and a charcoal grill running wide-open can exceed that. Keep the vents modulated to maintain a controlled temperature range, and avoid placing on-grate mats directly over actively flaming coals rather than established embers. For low-and-slow charcoal cooking, they work cleanly.
